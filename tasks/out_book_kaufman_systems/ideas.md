### 1. Donchian 4-Week Channel Breakout
- Where: Chapter 8, Section "Donchian's 4-Week Rule", p. 288 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_08.htm`)
- Quote: "In Donchian's method, the following rules were applied: 1. Go long (and cover shorts) when the current price exceeds the highs of the previous four full calendar weeks. 2. Sell short (and liquidate longs) when the current price falls below the lows of the previous four full calendar weeks. 3. When trading futures, roll forward if necessary into the next contract on the last day of the month preceding expiration."
- Rule as written:
  - Entry: Buy long when price exceeds highest high of prior 4 calendar weeks; sell short when price falls below lowest low of prior 4 calendar weeks.
  - Exit: Reverse on opposite signal.
  - Stop loss: not stated (reversal acts as stop).
  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Commodity and financial futures.
  - Bar size: Weekly / daily.
  - Holding time: Multi-week to multi-month trend following.
- Book's evidence: 16 years of commodity history (1950s–1970s). Rated best system in *The Traders Note Book* (1970). Best performers: December wheat, June cattle, May copper, August bellies.
- Mechanism (the book's): none given ("The system satisfies the basic concepts of trading with the trend, limiting losses...").
- Mechanism (my inference): Commercial hedgers and structural fundamental trends drive persistent multi-month price trends that overcome the drag of whipsaws in choppy markets.
- Apex fit: no. Requires multi-week overnight holding; violates Apex flat-by-15:59 CT rule.

- Where: Chapter 8, Section "Bollinger Bands", pp. 306–310 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_08.htm`)
- Quote: "Bollinger Bands use a standard deviation of the price changes to define the band... A breakout occurs when the price moves outside the band, signaling a continuation of the move in that direction."
- Rule as written:
  - Entry: Buy when price crosses above upper band ($SMA_{20} + 2 \times \sigma_{20}$); sell short when price crosses below lower band ($SMA_{20} - 2 \times \sigma_{20}$).

  - Sizing: not stated.

  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Multi-day.
- Book's evidence: Tested on S&P cash/futures and individual stocks; shows trend capture during strong runs but frequent whipsaws during sideways consolidation.
- Mechanism (the book's): none given.
- Mechanism (my inference): Statistical outlier moves ($> 2\sigma$) reflect institutional momentum or unannounced fundamental catalysts driving price away from fair value.
- Apex fit: no. Requires multi-day holding; daily bands do not close flat by 15:59 CT.

### 4. Dunnigan Thrust Method
- Where: Chapter 4, Section "Dunnigan and the Thrust Method", pp. 115–118 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_04.htm`)
- Quote: "An entry buy signal was generated by combining the patterns indicating a preliminary buy, with a thrust the next day confirming the move. The thrust was defined as a price gain that varied with the price level of the market (for 1954 wheat, this was from 1/2 to 1 1/2 cents)."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Satisfy at least 2 of 4 preliminary patterns: (1) Test of bottom (price within predefined % of prior low), (2) Closing-price reversal (new low followed by higher close), (3) Narrow range (range < 50% of largest range in swing), (4) Inside range (high and low inside prior range).
  - Trigger / Entry: Buy on next day if confirmed by a "thrust" (price advance exceeding fixed point or volatility threshold). Opposite for short.
  - Exit: Reverse on opposite signal or use square root profit objective.
  - Stop loss: Prior swing low/high.
  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Wheat, grains, equities.
  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Multi-day swing.
- Book's evidence: Historical application to 1950s grain markets (wheat) and Dow theory stocks.
- Mechanism (the book's): none given ("attempted to enter the market long near a bottom and short near a top... asking the market to give evidence of a change of direction").
- Mechanism (my inference): Combines volatility compression with directional expansion, entering after sellers exhaust themselves at prior support.
- Apex fit: no. Multi-day holding period; relies on daily swing patterns.
- Already tested? no.

### 5. Dunnigan One-Way Formula
- Where: Chapter 4, Section "One-Way Formula", pp. 118–120 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_04.htm`)
- Quote: "With the upswing and downswing rules remaining the same, Dunnigan modified the thrust to require its entire range to be outside the range of the prior day. For a preliminary buy, the low of the day must be above the high of the prior day. This is a much stronger condition than his original thrust yet only constitutes a preliminary buy... The confirmation requires an additional upthrust after the formation of, or test of, a previous bottom."
- Rule as written:
  - Preliminary Setup: Day where entire daily range is outside prior range (for buy: $Low_t > High_{t-1}$).

  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Grains, equities.
  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Multi-day.
- Book's evidence: Ruggiero's computerized testing (`TSM Dunnigan One-Way Formula`) confirmed sound results on longer-term trends.
- Mechanism (the book's): none given.
- Mechanism (my inference): Demands strong proof of institutional accumulation (gap/thrust) followed by successful secondary retest of support before risking capital.
- Apex fit: no. Multi-day swing holding; violates 15:59 CT flat rule.

### 6. Nofri's Congestion-Phase System
- Where: Chapter 4, Section "Nofri's Congestion-Phase System", pp. 121–123 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_04.htm`)
- Quote: "The basis of the system is a third-day reversal. If prices are within a congestion range and have closed in the same direction for two consecutive days, take the opposite position on the close of day two, anticipating a reversal. If this is correct, take the profits on the close of trading the next (third) day."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Identify established congestion range (top = high followed by 2 lower closes; bottom = low followed by 2 higher closes; wait 10 days after large move).
  - Entry: Inside range, if prices close down 2 consecutive days, buy on close of Day 2. If prices close up 2 consecutive days, sell short on close of Day 2.
  - Exit: Exit on close of Day 3 (1-day hold). If trade loses but close is still down, re-enter long.
  - Stop loss: Top/bottom of congestion area (or none, relying on 1-day exit).
  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Wheat futures.
  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Exactly 1 trading day (overnight hold from close of Day 2 to close of Day 3).
- Book's evidence: TradeStation testing on wheat (`TSM Nofri Congestion Phase`) showed steady small profits for 30 years, but sustained severe drawdowns during 2008 subprime crisis.
- Mechanism (the book's): Sideways markets represent stable supply/demand equilibrium where sustained multi-day runs are statistically unlikely.
- Mechanism (my inference): Mean-reversion inventory rebalancing by commercial commercials against speculative overextension in quiet ranges.
- Apex fit: no. Requires holding position overnight (close to close); matches mean reversion.
- Already tested? Matches "mean reversion on NQ / ES" on failed list.

### 7. Outside Day with an Outside Close
- Where: Chapter 4, Section "Outside Days with an Outside Close", pp. 123–124 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_04.htm`)
- Quote: "1. Buy on the close of an outside day if the close is above the prior high; sell if the close is below the prior low. 2. If buying, place a stop-loss just below the low of the outside day; if selling, place the stop just above the high. 3. Close out the position on the close three days after entry."
- Rule as written:
  - Entry: Buy on close of an outside day ($High_t > High_{t-1}$ and $Low_t < Low_{t-1}$) if $Close_t > High_{t-1}$. Sell short on close if $Close_t < Low_{t-1}$.
  - Stop loss: Just below the low of the outside day for longs; just above the high for shorts.
  - Exit: Liquidate on close 3 trading days after entry.
  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Currencies, metals, financial futures.
  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Exactly 3 trading days.
- Book's evidence: Curtis Arnold study showed profitability in 1970s/early 1980s, but failed from mid-1980s onward; Kaufman notes that reversing the rules (fading the outside close) worked better in modern noisy markets.
- Mechanism (the book's): High-volatility news day resolved decisively in one direction creates momentum carryover.
- Mechanism (my inference): Initial institutional re-pricing post-news triggers multi-day flow adjustments.
- Apex fit: no. 3-day holding period with overnight risk.
- Already tested? no.

- Mechanism (my inference): Liquidity clusters at visible horizontal support/resistance levels; clearing these levels triggers stop orders.

### 10. Wilder's Swing Index System (Accumulated Swing Index / ASI)
- Where: Chapter 5, Section "Wilder's Swing Index System", pp. 138–142 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_05.htm`)
- Quote: "1. Initial entry: a. Enter a new long position when the $ASI_t$ crosses above $HSP_{t-2}$. b. Enter a new short position when the $ASI_t$ crosses below $LSP_{t-2}$... 2. Setting the SAR point: a. On entering a new long trade, the SAR is the most recent LSP; the SAR is reset to the first LSP following each new HSP. A trailing SAR is determined as the lowest daily low occurring between the highest HSP and the close of the day on which the ASI dropped 60 points or more."
- Rule as written:
  - Calculation: Compute Swing Index $SI_t$ combining close-to-close, open-to-close, prior open-to-close, and true range scaled by limit move $M=100$. Cumulative $ASI_t = ASI_{t-1} + SI_t$.
  - Entry: Buy when $ASI_t > High Swing Point (HSP_{t-2})$; sell short when $ASI_t < Low Swing Point (LSP_{t-2})$.
  - Stop / SAR: On long, stop at most recent $LSP$; trail stop to lowest low between highest $HSP$ and day when $ASI$ drops $\ge 60$ points. Reverse for short.
  - Sizing: not stated.
  - Market: Eurobund, financial and commodity futures.
  - Bar size: Daily.
  - Holding time: Multi-day.
- Book's evidence: J. Welles Wilder Jr. (1978); Kaufman illustrates on Eurobund continuous futures (2010–2011).
- Mechanism (the book's): none given.
- Mechanism (my inference): Evaluates true inter-day momentum across open, high, low, close relative to maximum daily range, eliminating phantom bar noise.
- Apex fit: no. Multi-day holding period; stop requires overnight positions.
- Already tested? no.

- Mechanism (the book's): none given ("The CCI is essentially a variation on a standard deviation channel...").

### 19. Commitment of Traders (COT) Commercial Sentiment Index Extreme
- Where: Chapter 14, Section "Commitment of Traders Sentiment Index", pp. 642–646 (EPUB: `OEBPS/9781118236031_epub_ch_14.htm`)

## Ranked Shortlist of Apex-Compatible Ideas

To qualify for this shortlist, an idea must strictly satisfy three criteria:
1. **Apex-compatible**: Intraday only (strictly flat by 15:59 CT), trades liquid futures (NQ, ES, CL, GC, ZN, or micros), single stop loss that fits a $2,000 trailing drawdown.
2. **Not already tested**: Cannot match the brief's "Already tested and failed" list (not moving-average crosses, not 20-day regime filters, not mean reversion on NQ/ES, not order-flow indicators, not gamma/OI pinning).
3. **Stated mechanism**: Must have an explicit, structural market mechanism identifying the forced or paid trader.

Ranked by the clarity and inevitability of the forced trader:

### Rank 1: Toby Crabel Opening Range Breakout (ORB) with Inside Day (Chapter 16)
- **Why it fits Apex**: 100% intraday execution on 5-minute to 15-minute bars. Entry occurs between 08:35 and 09:30 CT; mandatory exit at session close (15:00 CT), well before Apex's 15:59 CT cutoff. Risk is bounded by the opening range boundary or midpoint (typically 20–40 NQ points = $100–$200 on MNQ), easily respecting the $2,000 trailing drawdown.
- **Why not tested / failed**: It is an intraday session volatility expansion breakout following price contraction, NOT a moving average cross, NOT a multi-day regime filter, and NOT mean reversion.
- **Forced trader & mechanism**: An inside day forces market makers and institutional participants to compress their risk envelopes, causing resting stop-loss orders to cluster tightly immediately outside yesterday's high and low. On the breakout of the opening range, market makers widening spreads and executing these clustered stop orders trigger an involuntary cascading liquidity demand, forcing trapped countertrend participants to chase price.

### Rank 2: Mark Fisher ACD Opening Range Breakout (Chapter 16)
- **Why it fits Apex**: Operates on 5-minute bars during the morning session. Strictly day-traded and flat by close. Stop placed at the opening range midpoint or opposite threshold (typically 15–30 NQ points = $75–$150 on MNQ), well within the $2,000 trailing drawdown.
- **Why not tested / failed**: It is an opening range directional momentum breakout with a time-confirmation persistence filter, not on the failed list.
- **Forced trader & mechanism**: Floor market makers and commercial liquidity providers establish inventory imbalances during the opening 5–20 minutes. When price penetrates the $A$-threshold and sustains that level for half the opening interval, market makers who leaned against the opening move are caught offside with adverse inventory and are structurally forced to cover their positions into the trending market.

### Rank 3: Toby Crabel Opening Range Breakout (ORB) with NR7 Volatility Compression (Chapter 16)
- **Why it fits Apex**: 100% intraday. Uses 5- or 15-minute bars following a 7-day range low (NR7). Flat by 15:15 CT. Strict stop loss at the 15-minute opening range midpoint ($150–$300 risk on MNQ).
- **Why not tested / failed**: Range-contraction volatility breakout, not on the failed list.
- **Forced trader & mechanism**: Multi-day range contraction (NR7) reflects temporary institutional consensus and order matching exhaustion. When the opening range breaks, execution algorithms that kept volume bottled up are forced to switch from passive liquidity provision to aggressive liquidity taking, driving an uncontained directional trend day.

### Rank 4: Rudd Intraday Dip-and-Rally Consolidation Breakout (Chapter 16)
- **Why it fits Apex**: 100% intraday on 5-minute bars. Flat by 15:00 CT. Entry triggers on an afternoon breakout of morning resistance, with stop loss placed at the tight consolidation support level ($100–$250 risk on MNQ).
- **Why not tested / failed**: Intraday continuation breakout, not on the failed list.
- **Forced trader & mechanism**: The opening dip lures in aggressive morning short sellers who anticipate a breakdown. When price reverses back to the morning high and consolidates tightly, those short sellers are trapped in underwater positions. The eventual breakout triggers forced stop-loss buying and short-covering panic.

### Rank 5: Bookstaber Intraday ATR Volatility Breakout (Chapter 20)
- **Why it fits Apex**: When applied to 15-minute intraday bars with a mandatory flat-by-15:15 CT exit rule. Entry triggers when a bar displacement exceeds $k \times ATR$ ($k \approx 2.5–3.0$). Trailing stop at $k \times ATR$ bounds loss per trade well within the $2,000 drawdown.
- **Why not tested / failed**: Volatility expansion threshold breakout, not a moving average cross.
- **Forced trader & mechanism**: Sudden large-scale displacement exceeding 3 times normal bar volatility represents urgent institutional execution (e.g. macro news reaction or algorithmic basket flow). Market makers withdraw passive limit orders, creating an order book vacuum that forces aggressive takers and trapped participants to pay wide spreads and chase momentum.
