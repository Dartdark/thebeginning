### 1. Failure Test (Wyckoff Spring / Upthrust at Key Structural Levels)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Failure Test, pp. 165–172; Chapter 10, pp. 267–275
- Quote: "For a short entry, the market trades above a clearly defined resistance area, but immediately reverses on the same or the following bar and closes back under the resistance. There can be significant volatility, volume, and activity on the breakout, but there should be no real conviction beyond the level. Enter short on the close of the bar that closes back below resistance. The buy setup is exactly symmetrical."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market probes beyond a significant, well-defined support or resistance level (such as prior swing high/low, trading range boundary, or major pivot). Best when the market is mature, extended, or showing momentum divergence on the trading timeframe.
  - Trigger: Price trades beyond the level, but immediately reverses on the same bar or the very next bar and closes back inside the level (below resistance for short, above support for long). Enter on the close of the bar closing back inside (or at the market open of the next bar).
  - Stop: Hard stop placed 1 tick beyond the extreme of the failure test bar (the probe high/low).
  - Target: Two-stage exit: Target 1 at 1R (equal to initial dollar risk) or opposite consolidation boundary, where 25% to 33% of the position is exited. Move stop on remaining position to breakeven, trailing behind subsequent structural pivots.
  - Time Stop: If Target 1 is not reached within 3 to 4 bars of entry, exit or scratch the position at market.
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures (ES, NQ, CL), equities, and forex; any timeframe from intraday (5-min, 15-min) to daily.
- Book's evidence: Extensive chart evidence: Figure 6.1 (FOSL daily), Figure 6.2 (EURUSD 60-min), Figures 10.12–10.24 (Cotton, Crude Oil, S&P 500, Wheat).
- Mechanism (the book's): Stop-running and trapped liquidity. Markets naturally probe for liquidity clustered beyond key structural boundaries. When stop-loss orders are triggered and no aggressive institutional follow-through buying/selling emerges to absorb supply, the market snaps back inside the range. Breakout traders are instantly trapped offside; their forced liquidations provide the immediate momentum fuel for the countertrend reversal.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Fully compatible with intraday NQ/ES/CL/MNQ trading. Extreme tight structural stop (1 tick past probe extreme, typically 10–25 NQ ticks = $50–$125 on MNQ), easily fitting within the $2,000 trailing drawdown. Position is managed actively and closed flat well before 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No. (The brief notes general mean reversion on NQ/ES failed, but this is a specific structural liquidity-run failure test with tight structural stops, not statistical Bollinger/RSI band mean reversion).

### 2. Pullback: Buying Support or Shorting Resistance (Classic Trend Continuation)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Buying Support or Shorting Resistance, pp. 172–179; Chapter 3, pp. 69–95
- Quote: "Pullbacks are probably the most important structural feature of trends. For many traders, pullbacks are the quintessential with-trend trade, using the countertrend pullback to position in the larger trend at an advantageous price... the setup leg preceding the pullback should be at least as strong as previous trend legs in the same direction. In other words, it should not break the pattern of the trend and should not show momentum divergence."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market is in an established trend identified by higher highs/lows and sloping 20-period EMA. Preceding impulse leg must show strong directional momentum without MACD divergence. A shallow, low-momentum countertrend retracement develops toward dynamic support/resistance (the 20 EMA or broken prior resistance).
  - Trigger: As price tests the 20 EMA or support/resistance zone, enter when price turns back in the trend direction, triggering on a break of the previous bar's high (for longs) or low (for shorts).
  - Stop: Placed just beyond the swing low/high of the pullback consolidation (typically 1.5 to 2.0 ATR away, or 1 tick beyond the reaction pivot).
  - Target: Target 1 at the prior trend extreme (initial scale-out of 25%–33% at 1R), stop moved to breakeven, remaining position trailed behind reaction pivot lows or 20 EMA.
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures, stocks, forex; 5-min, 15-min, 60-min, daily.
- Book's evidence: Multiple worked examples: Figure 6.3 (Boeing daily), Figure 6.4 (Gold futures daily), Figure 6.5 (EURUSD 60-min), Figures 10.1–10.11.
- Mechanism (the book's): Institutional re-accumulation/distribution. Institutional trend participants operating with multi-day execution mandates re-enter the market at dynamic value (the 20 EMA) as countertrend profit-taking subsides.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Standard intraday trend continuation on NQ/ES/CL; flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 3. Pullback: Lower Time Frame Breakout Entry (Multi-Timeframe Pullback)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Entering Lower Time Frame Breakout, pp. 179–183; Chapter 7, pp. 219–228
- Quote: "The difference is the actual entry trigger, which, in this case, is on a breakout of some structure within the pullback itself, usually visible most clearly on a lower time frame... Taken by itself, the breakout on the lower time frame is not a compelling entry... The higher time frame provides context for this otherwise unimpressive breakout, and the upward momentum from the intraday time frame transitioned into a larger move on the daily time frame."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Higher timeframe (HTF, e.g., 15-min or 60-min) is in a confirmed trend and experiencing a low-momentum pullback into its 20 EMA.
  - Trigger: On a lower timeframe (LTF, e.g., 3-min or 5-min, 3x to 5x faster), identify the countertrend structure (a micro-trendline, downward channel, or horizontal resistance). Enter on a confirmed breakout of this LTF structure in the direction of the HTF trend.
  - Stop: Placed just beyond the lower timeframe reaction pivot low (offering significantly tighter risk than an HTF structural stop).
  - Target: Target 1 at the HTF trend extreme; trail remainder with an HTF trailing stop.
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures and equities; HTF 15m/60m, LTF 1m/3m/5m.
- Book's evidence: Figure 6.9 (Dendreon Corp daily chart with 78-minute LTF breakout entry).
- Mechanism (the book's): Fractional liquidity alignment. Countertrend scalpers on the lower timeframe are trapped when higher-timeframe institutional buying overwhelms the micro-structure, triggering rapid short covering that propels the HTF trend.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Exceptional fit for NQ/MNQ: allows entering a 15-min trend with a tight 3-min/1-min stop, keeping dollar risk tiny ($75–$150) well below the $2,000 trailing drawdown while capturing HTF trend moves. Flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 4. Complex Pullbacks (Two-Legged Countertrend ABCD Retracement)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Trading Complex Pullbacks, pp. 183–188; Chapter 3, pp. 88–95
- Quote: "Complex pullbacks, which are pullbacks composed of two distinct countertrend legs, are very common, especially in mature trends. A good understanding of these structures is important, because they will often result in losses for traders who are trading simple pullbacks. Furthermore, many of the best pullbacks—those followed by the strongest, cleanest moves—are complex pullbacks..."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: In an established trend, the pullback develops into an ABCD 2-legged structure: Leg AB (first countertrend impulse), Leg BC (shallow retracement in trend direction), and Leg CD (second countertrend impulse). Leg CD typically terminates near the price length of Leg AB ($CD \approx AB$) or exhibits momentum divergence on the MACD.
  - Trigger: Enter at the completion of Leg CD when price reverses and breaks the high of the prior bar (for longs) or the low of the prior bar (for shorts), or breaks the micro-trendline connecting B and D.
  - Stop: Hard stop 1 tick beyond the extreme of Leg CD pivot D.
  - Target: Retest of primary trend origin (Point A), with initial scale-out of 1/3 at 1R.
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures, stocks, forex; 5-min, 15-min, daily.
- Book's evidence: Figure 6.10 (EURUSD 60-min ABCD complex pullback).
- Mechanism (the book's): Shaking out premature trend traders. The first countertrend leg (AB) induces early trend traders to buy prematurely; the second countertrend leg (CD) stops them out and traps countertrend breakout traders, thoroughly clearing weak hands before institutions resume the primary trend.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Excellent pattern for intraday NQ/ES; tight structural stop, flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 5. The Anti (Trend Termination / First Pullback of New Trend)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - The Anti, pp. 188–194; Chapter 10, pp. 285–292
- Quote: "This trade is a very specific pullback variation that attempts to enter the first pullback after a potential trend change... both parts are important: the initial market structure that provides justification for potential countertrend trades, followed by a shift of momentum that shows that the dominant trend players have lost control of the market. The actual entry for this trade is in the first pullback following the strong countertrend price movement."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Two distinct prerequisite phases:
    1. Trend Exhaustion: An existing trend shows structural exhaustion (momentum divergence on MACD, parabolic climax, or a failed test of highs/lows).
    2. Change of Character Impulse: An aggressive countertrend wave emerges, breaking prior reaction swing points and registering a new momentum extreme on the MACD (e.g. Fast Line crosses zero and hits a new swing low/high).
  - Trigger: Wait for the first pullback against this new countertrend impulse (which should be low-volume and shallow). Enter as the pullback stalls and turns back in the direction of the new countertrend impulse (breaking the prior bar's low for short, or high for long).
  - Stop: Placed 1 tick beyond the swing high/low of the first pullback consolidation.
  - Target: Target 1 at the low/high of the initial impulse wave (1R exit for 1/3 position); trail remainder with stop at breakeven for a 2R to 3R extension or full trend reversal.
  - Market / Bar: Futures (ES, NQ, Coffee, Wheat), FX, stocks; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figure 6.11 (EURUSD 60-min), Figure 10.29 (Coffee futures), Figure 10.30 (Wheat futures).
- Mechanism (the book's): Trapped legacy trend traders. Dominant players in the old trend are blindsided by the sharp institutional shift of momentum. When price produces the first weak pullback, trapped traders attempt to buy/sell out of habit. Their orders are quickly absorbed, and as price rolls over, their forced stop-loss liquidations trigger a cascade in the direction of the new trend.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Highly suited for intraday NQ/ES/CL trend shifts. Allows catching major trend transitions with the tight risk of a small pullback stop, easily conforming to Apex drawdown limits. Flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 6. Breakouts: Entering in the Preceding Base (Pre-Breakout Coiling / Absorption)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Entering in the Preceding Base, pp. 194–198; Chapter 5, pp. 135–144
- Quote: "It is possible to enter in these formations preceding the breakout in order to have better trade location and to sidestep the potential slippage... A very small set of these levels become extremely important, and, when violated, the pressure gives rise to a strong move beyond the level."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market approaches a major horizontal resistance/support level and forms a tight, narrow-range consolidation (base) directly below resistance (or directly above support). Characteristic patterns include ascending triangles, high-tight flags, or progressively higher lows compressing into resistance (classic Wyckoff absorption).
  - Trigger: Enter long inside the base near the bottom of the consolidation, or on a break of the micro-high within the base, *before* the major resistance line is broken.
  - Stop: Placed 1 tick below the low of the tight pre-breakout base.
  - Target: Open-ended breakout expansion; scale out 1/3 at 1R to finance risk, trail remainder through the breakout run.
  - Market / Bar: Equities, futures, forex; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figures 6.12–6.14.
- Mechanism (the book's): Institutional order absorption. Large smart-money buyers absorb all selling supply directly below resistance without letting price drop. When supply is exhausted, the market pops through resistance with minimal resistance.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Very small risk per trade ($50–$100 on micros) due to tight basing stops. Intraday execution flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 7. Breakouts: Entering on First Pullback Following Breakout (Retest Entry)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Entering on First Pullback Following, pp. 198–202; Chapter 5, pp. 138–142
- Quote: "The first pullback after a breakout offers a spot to initiate a position in the direction of the new trend supported by the confirmation of a successful breakout. This entry also avoids the volatility of the actual breakout area, and the uncertainty inherent in positions established in the prebreakout base."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market breaks out of a significant trading range with wide-range bars and expanding volume/volatility, confirming a genuine structural breakout.
  - Trigger: The initial breakout impulse exhausts and pulls back to retest the broken breakout level (old resistance becoming new support) or the rising 20 EMA. Enter when price holds the retest level and triggers above the high of the prior bar.
  - Stop: Hard stop placed just below the low of the retest pullback (or just back inside the old range).
  - Target: Minimum 1:1 measured move of the breakout base height, or trailing stop behind successive swing lows.
  - Market / Bar: S&P 500 futures, commodities, stocks; 5-min, 15-min, daily.
- Book's evidence: Figure 6.15 (S&P 500 futures 5-min intraday chart).
- Mechanism (the book's): Sidelined institutional liquidity entry. Institutional traders who missed the initial breakout enter on the first dip to fair value; trapped breakout shorts who failed to cover on the breakout capitulate and buy on the pullback.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Classic intraday futures setup (Figure 6.15 explicitly demonstrates an intraday S&P 500 5-min chart). Flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 8. Failed Breakouts (Fading the False Breakout Back into Range)
- Where: Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates - Failed Breakouts, pp. 202–206; Chapter 5, pp. 144–148
- Quote: "Most breakouts fail... In the very common case of a volatile breakout with a pullback that violates the breakout level... traders can capitalize on failed breakouts by shorting when the market slips back inside the trading range."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market breaks above the boundary of an established trading range, but fails to generate immediate follow-through (exhibiting narrow bars, falling volume, or immediate upper shadows).
  - Trigger: When price drops back below the breakout level and closes inside the trading range, enter short (mirror for failed breakdowns).
  - Stop: Hard stop placed 1 tick above the highest high of the false breakout.
  - Target: The opposite boundary of the trading range (mean reversion across the range).
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures, stocks, forex; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figures 6.16–6.18.
- Mechanism (the book's): Trapped breakout momentum traders. Market makers and institutional range traders absorb breakout buying, and as liquidity vanishes, they push prices back into the range, triggering a cascading liquidation of long breakout stops.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Marginal. While it can be traded intraday and closed flat by 15:59 CT, the brief explicitly notes: "Already tested and failed: mean reversion on NQ / ES". Fading across the range is vulnerable to trending regime days.
- Already tested? Yes (matches brief's "mean reversion on NQ / ES").

### 9. Keltner Channel Band Fade (2.25 ATR Band Reversion)
- Where: Chapter 7: Tools for Confirmation - Channels: Emotional Extremes, pp. 209–213; Table 7.1
- Quote: "The Keltner channels that I use are set at a level that contains between 85 and 90 percent of all trading activity (the range of the bar, not just the close) across a wide range of markets... In many markets, there actually is a slight statistical edge to fading moves to the upper or lower Keltner channel... A good rule of thumb is to not consider any fade trade unless the market is extended beyond the bands..."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Modified Keltner Channels: 20-period EMA baseline $\pm 2.25 \times \text{ATR}(20)$. Market must be in a confirmed non-trending / sideways trading range.
  - Trigger: Price pushes outside the upper band (or lower band) and forms a reversal candlestick (closing back inside the band). Enter short (or long).
  - Stop: Hard stop 1 tick beyond the extreme excursion high/low.
  - Target: Dynamic mean reversion back to the 20-period EMA.
  - Market / Bar: Futures (Wheat, Sugar, Corn), equities; daily and intraday.
- Book's evidence: Table 7.1 (empirical analysis of 2,403,774 bars across multiple asset classes verifying 85–90% containment of all bar range activity).
- Mechanism (the book's): Short-term liquidity exhaustion and emotional overextension. Aggressive retail market orders temporarily clean out the limit order book, but lack the capital to sustain prices away from fair value; market makers step in to push prices back to the mean.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Poor. Intraday mean reversion on index futures (NQ/ES) has negative expectancy after commissions and slippage.
- Already tested? Yes (matches brief's "mean reversion on NQ / ES").

### 10. The "Free Bar" Exhaustion Climax Reversal
- Where: Chapter 7, pp. 210–213; Glossary, p. 434
- Quote: "Free bars are bars that are pushed to such a dramatic extreme that the low of the bar is above the upper band, or the high of the bar is below the bottom channel—the entire bar is outside the channel. These are rare, but potentially significant, events that can indicate a condition of extreme imbalance in the market."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: On a chart with 20 EMA $\pm 2.25 \times \text{ATR}(20)$ Keltner Channels, an entire price bar prints completely outside the channel band (for a high free bar: $\text{Low} > \text{Upper Band}$; for a low free bar: $\text{High} < \text{Lower Band}$). The market must be in a mature, extended trend.
  - Trigger: Enter countertrend when price trades 1 tick below the low of the high free bar (or 1 tick above the high of the low free bar).
  - Stop: Hard stop 1 tick beyond the extreme of the free bar.
  - Target: The 20-period EMA.
  - Market / Bar: Futures, equities; daily and intraday.
- Book's evidence: Table 7.1 and Figure 7.8 (Sugar futures daily free bar).
- Mechanism (the book's): Panic buying/selling climax. Represents a total capitulation of opposing market participants, exhausting all available aggressive orders at the extreme.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Marginal. Intraday fade; can be traded on micros, but carries mean reversion risk.
- Already tested? Yes (matches brief's "mean reversion on NQ / ES").

### 11. MACD Fast Line Momentum Divergence
- Where: Chapter 7: Indicators: MACD - Fast Line Divergence, pp. 213–219; Appendix B, pp. 419–427
- Quote: "Figure 7.14 Momentum Divergences in Wheat Futures... Fast Line Divergence... The distance between these two averages is one way to measure the momentum behind a market's movements, and this lower peak in the indicator suggests that the second price high was made on lower momentum."
- Rule as written:
  - Indicator: Modified MACD: Fast Line = $\text{SMA}(3) - \text{SMA}(10)$; Signal Line = $\text{SMA}(16)$ of Fast Line.
  - Setup: Price makes a higher swing high, but the MACD Fast Line registers a lower peak (bearish divergence); or price makes a lower swing low, but MACD Fast Line registers a higher trough (bullish divergence). The MACD line must pull back to or cross zero between the two peaks.
  - Trigger: Do not enter on divergence alone. Enter when price breaks the intervening countertrend micro-trendline or forms a Failure Test at the second peak.
  - Stop: 1 tick beyond the second price peak.
  - Target: The 20-period EMA or previous reaction swing pivot.
  - Market / Bar: Wheat futures, currencies, stocks; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figures 7.14–7.16, B.8–B.12.
- Mechanism (the book's): Fading institutional participation. Higher price highs achieved on lower momentum indicate that aggressive buying has dried up; price is floating higher purely on lack of selling liquidity rather than real demand.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes when used as a filter for structural entries (Failure Test or The Anti).
- Already tested? No as a structural confirmation tool, but indicator-only filters are cautioned in the brief.

### 12. Trading Parabolic Climaxes (Exhaustion Retest / Blowoff Fade)
- Where: Chapter 10: Trade Examples - Trading Parabolic Climaxes, pp. 275–285
- Quote: "Parabolic climaxes are disorderly, volatile areas... In general, trade entries within parabolic climaxes are problematic. It is usually impossible to find the precise turning point... Rather than dealing with the uncertainty and poorly defined risk limits in the actual climax, astute traders can wait for a failure test on a retest of the trend extreme."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Market enters a vertical, accelerating parabolic advance or decline (consecutive wide-range expansion bars far outside Keltner channels).
  - Trigger: Never fade the initial vertical spike. Wait for the initial sharp reaction collapse, followed by an attempted retest of the climactic high/low. When the retest fails (forming a Failure Test—briefly probing the high/low and reversing, or forming a lower high on anemic volume), enter countertrend.
  - Stop: Hard stop 1 tick beyond the highest point of the parabolic climax / retest.
  - Target: 50% retracement of the entire parabolic advance, or dynamic retest of the 20 EMA.
  - Market / Bar: Futures (Coffee, Silver), individual equities; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figures 10.25–10.28 (Coffee futures and Silver futures parabolic climaxes).
- Mechanism (the book's): Short squeeze exhaustion followed by buyer vacuum. The initial parabolic spike is driven by forced margin liquidations of trapped short sellers. Once all shorts have been forcefully liquidated, there are no marginal buyers left to support prices. Astute traders fade the retest because the market faces an immediate liquidity vacuum below.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Highly suited for volatile intraday blowoff sessions (e.g. FOMC releases or morning NQ parabolic expansions). Stop is clearly defined above the retest high. Flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 13. Failure Test Failures (Breakout Continuation Squeeze)
- Where: Chapter 10: Trade Examples - Failure Test Failures, pp. 270–275
- Quote: "On one hand, failures of the failure test pattern are simple—the market makes a new extreme and keeps going... In the very common case of a volatile breakout... being aware of the patterns that typically set up failure of these patterns can provide additional insight into developing market structure."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: A Failure Test pattern forms at a major resistance level, but instead of dropping rapidly toward Target 1 within 3 to 4 bars, the market refuses to decline and coils in a tight sideways consolidation right against resistance.
  - Trigger: Enter long when price breaks above the high of the Failure Test bar (the exact stop-loss level of the failure test short sellers).
  - Stop: Hard stop placed just below the low of the tight consolidation base formed under resistance.
  - Target: Open-ended breakout trend leg; trail stop behind successive higher pivot lows.
  - Market / Bar: Liquid futures (ES, NQ, Crude Oil), stocks; 5-min to daily.
- Book's evidence: Figures 10.23–10.24.
- Mechanism (the book's): Trapped countertrend short sellers. Traders who shorted the failure test are trapped as the market refuses to fall. When price takes out the high of the failure test, their forced buy-stop orders trigger simultaneously with new breakout buyers, fueling an explosive continuation move.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Superb intraday breakout model on NQ/ES. Low risk (stop under tight consolidation), high momentum, flat by 15:59 CT.
- Already tested? No.

### 14. Time-Based Stop Loss Rule (Active Trade Management)
- Where: Chapter 8: Trade Management - Active Management (Time Stops), pp. 242–245; Chapter 10, p. 271
- Quote: "You should not have to wait more than three or four bars for the profit target to be hit, and many times it will be reached on the first bar following the entry. It is justifiable to reduce risk in the trade (by exiting part of the position) somewhere following B because the trade is not working as well or as cleanly as expected... holding becomes a matter of hoping rather than of positioning with a clean, working trade."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Applies to any trade entered on a technical template (Failure Test, Breakout, Pullback).
  - Rule: If price has not reached Target 1 or demonstrated strong momentum in the trade direction within 3 to 4 bars following entry, immediately reduce position size or exit the trade at market (scratching for near breakeven). Never sit in a stagnant position hoping for it to work.
  - Market / Bar: All markets and timeframes.
- Book's evidence: Figures 10.23–10.24 (demonstrating how time stops avoid catastrophic failure test blowups).
- Mechanism (the book's): Edge decay over time. Technical patterns create a localized, temporary order flow imbalance. If the market does not immediately follow through within 3–4 bars, the imbalance has dissipated and price action reverts to a random walk.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Crucial risk control for Apex evaluation and funded accounts; eliminates stagnation and protects the $2,000 trailing drawdown.
- Already tested? No.

### 15. Two-Tier Profit Scaling at 1R (Fixed Risk Multiple Management)
- Where: Chapter 8: Trade Management - Setting Price Targets, pp. 237–241; Chapter 9, pp. 249–262
- Quote: "I usually exit between 25 and 33 percent of the position on a limit order at a profit equal to my initial risk in the trade... and hold the remainder of the position for a larger move."
- Rule as written:
  - Setup: Position entered with known Initial Risk $R = |\text{Entry} - \text{Initial Stop}|$.
  - Target 1: Place a limit order to exit 25% to 33% of the position at $\text{Entry} + 1.0 \times R$ (for longs) or $\text{Entry} - 1.0 \times R$ (for shorts).
  - Stop Adjustment: Once Target 1 is filled, immediately move the stop on the remaining 67% to 75% of the position to breakeven (entry price).
  - Target 2 / Runner: Trail the remaining position behind structural reaction pivot lows/highs or the 20-period EMA to capture open-ended multi-R trend extensions.
  - Market / Bar: All markets; intraday and swing.
- Book's evidence: Extensive real-trade examples throughout Chapters 8, 9, 10, and Appendix C.
- Mechanism (the book's): Expectancy optimization. Taking partial profit at 1R locks in gains that mathematically eliminate the risk of a loss on the trade, allowing the trader to hold the runner stress-free for positive-skew windfall moves.
- Mechanism (my inference): None needed; book's mechanism is explicit.
- Apex fit: Yes. Directly supports the Apex $2,000 drawdown rule by de-risking positions quickly while allowing runners to build account equity.
- Already tested? No.
