# Evidence sheet 3: Option-expiration week long (dealer hedge unwind), Stivers & Sun 2013
Verdict: **DON'T TEST as a standalone day trade (power).** Rank 3.

1. **Who trades and why.** Option market makers delta-hedge customer positions; as monthly options approach the
   third-Friday expiry, short-stock hedges are reduced (Stivers & Sun, J. Banking & Finance 37(11), 2013: S&P 100 stocks
   earn higher returns in the expiration week, slightly lower the week after; drivers: hedge rebalancing and falling
   implied vol).
2. **Direction, size, timing?** Direction and timing from the calendar (Mon-Thu of the expiration week). Size: not
   estimable from our data before 2022 (The Bridge open interest by strike starts 2022-09).
3. **Reaches NQ?** Partly: the study is single stocks; index-level hedges hit ES / NQ.
4. **Separates from momentum?** Calendar-defined, so yes; random-week placebo is easy.
5. **Gap?** No: an 8:30-15:00 hold stacks on VB / ONB / RS days. Only usable as a size tilt on existing longs.
6. **Power.** 2018-22: ~60 expirations x 4 days = 240 day-trades. NQ's 8:30-15:00 return sd ≈ 1.5% → t 2 needs
   ≈ **0.19% a day** net (≈ $560 per NQ at 15,000). Calendar effects of this kind are usually a few hundredths of a
   percent a day. Not realistic.
7. **Already tested?** EOS (third-Friday open short, in the plan) and WATCHLIST #9 (Friday after the roll); the
   Mon-Thu expiration-week long is not in TIER_LIST.
