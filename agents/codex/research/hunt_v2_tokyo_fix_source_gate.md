## Eight evidence questions, before any new data

1. **Who trades?** The inherited idea card names Japanese importers buying USD for commercial
   settlement and fixing orders. That is a plausible class of participants, not authenticated dated
   orders, aggregate net flow or a quantitative measurement of who dominates the fix.
2. **Cause and obligation?** Commercial invoices and benchmark fixing could create price-insensitive
   USD demand. Export receipts, dealer prehedges, asset managers and anticipatory traders can offset
   it. The source does not establish that a calendar date creates net compulsory yen selling of a
   known amount in2018–26. Do not turn the story into measured causality.
3. **When and available to whom?** The inherited clock is09:55Asia/Tokyo, with Japan having no DST.

   or order-deadline eras would require source admission before price rows. Existing code's

4. **How big?** No admitted dated importer net-order or transaction-volume/imbalance input was
   established. A5/10 date indicator is a calendar proxy, not dollars. No expected net price move,
   native6J cost hurdle or supported gross-to-cost claim follows from the idea card.
5. **Direction and instrument transmission?** USD buying/JPY selling would conventionally imply
   USD/JPY up and USD-per-JPY6J down. That quote inversion is economic bookkeeping, not a newly
   observed edge. A late pre-fix short or post-fix reversal would need its own frozen rule and
   transient-pressure evidence; a reversal cannot simply be inferred from an active fixing window.

8. **Failure, diversification and executable risk?** USD demand may be offset or reverse, dealers
