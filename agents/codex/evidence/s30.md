## 1. Who trades, and what forces or causes the trade?

Hypothesis: cash-close selling can push an index near the bottom of its session range, followed by a rebound.
Risk cuts, stop orders and hedging are proposed explanations in the overview. The rule observes prices only;
it does not identify a forced seller, measure an imbalance, or demonstrate that a deadline caused the move.
This is a published-pattern replication hypothesis, with an economic explanation still to establish.

## 2. Direction, size and timing known before entry

At the completed 15:00 CT bar, IBS=(close−session low)/(session high−session low), from 26 bars stamped
08:45 through 15:00. If flat and IBS<0.20, buy one chart-symbol contract at the following bar's open, actual
approximately 15:00 CT (next bar stamped 15:15). If long and IBS>0.80, exit on the same next-bar convention.
Equality does nothing. Hold through nights/weekends/holidays; no stop, target or time exit.
Default Chicago chart clock avoids a manual US DST conversion. Native chart/session/fill properties are unverified.
No dollar flow or volume fraction is known from this rule. ContractQty 1 means a full NQ on an NQ chart;
the PDF's division by ten is accounting, not source-level micro sizing.

## 3. Does the flow reach the traded contract or its direct hedge?

Signals are measured on NQ, ES and RTY themselves, so no foreign-market timing transfer is required.
However, an index closing near its low does not show incremental forced buying tomorrow. ETF paper results
and spot cash-close execution do not by themselves establish an executable futures edge after costs.
