## 1. Who trades, and what forces or causes the trade?

The adopted RS parent follows NQ performance beyond its contemporaneous ES beta. This residual can
reflect NQ-specific demand, sector repricing or transient relative-price pressure; a positive residual
does not itself establish that NQ investors are buying. Marketable NQ orders measured independently of
the two price paths may distinguish continued direct demand from an unsupported cross-market residual.
The prediction is lower subsequent parent profitability when classified tape strictly opposes its side.

This is a conditional demand/inventory hypothesis on an existing leg. No identified compulsory fund
order, participant identity, inventory amount, stop list or hedge deadline is observed. Classification
does not reveal informed versus forced trading. Passive absorption could instead make opposed-flow
tickets better; published RS own-price-opposed tickets were already stronger. That is a serious
falsifier and required control, not a reason to change sign after results. A test of incremental input
on an adopted book piece is justified; an assertion of a measured new forced flow is not.

## 3. Does it reach the traded contract or its direct hedge?

The input is NQ's own tape, so it does not require a stock-level effect to sum into an index. MNQ and NQ
share Nasdaq100 exposure; this supports a connection, not proof of identical micro quotes/fills/receipt.
Cash baskets, QQQ, options and ES can net or redirect demand. A relative-price residual and aggressive
quantity need not measure the same inventory. No account hedge or diversification benefit is asserted.
