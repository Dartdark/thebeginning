# Till/Eagleeye commodity-index calendar spreads: NEEDS DATA / SOURCE SPECIFICATION

Summary: Scheduled index rolls identify a plausible two-maturity pressure mechanism.
This overview supplies no empirical return test or complete trading protocol.
Retain a conditional spread lead; do not repeat the failed outright CL timing proxies.
No economic trial is admitted from this source alone.

Source: Till and Eagleeye, January17 2017, SSRN2942340, uploaded five-page full text.
Return trials: zero new. Existing CL outright roll tests remain prior family evidence.

## 1. Who trades and why?
Commodity indices must replace expiring constituents according to published rules: sell the held maturity,
buy the replacement. Storage operators also hedge seasonal inventory. These are distinct mechanisms;
calendar predictability does not establish an unabsorbed price concession today.

## 2. Direction, size and timing known before entry
The source describes selling the front and buying the next maturity before an index roll, then unwinding during it.
It gives no exact index/calendar vintage, entry/exit session, roll weights, target maturity map or flow size.
Known modern dates and historical availability must be established before a bounded strategy protocol.

## 3. Direct traded contract
The example is wheat calendar spreads; seasonal discussion is natural gas and grains. This is not identified
NQ/ES demand. A futures-only spread is distinct from a storage operator's physical cash-and-carry transaction.

## 4. Information and data feasibility
Need point-in-time index methodology, constituent weights/AUM and roll schedule, actual front/deferred contract
identities, synchronized prices and both-leg execution/roll costs. Listed-spread quotes would improve execution
verification. Owned CL continuous prices cannot supply the missing second maturity or a wheat spread.
The existing paired-commodity data gates and acquisition limits are unchanged by this overview; no new matching
dataset is supplied. No repeated unchanged acquisition loop or continuous-price substitution is justified here.

## 5. Costs and occupancy
Two entries and two exits, native sizes and possible legging costs; no source gross edge is quantified against them.
Multiday source concepts do not fit the four Apex plans as written. Personal diversification is a hypothesis,
not an observed correlation or protection before an account breach. No event is selected because the book is flat.

## 6. Power
No source event census, spread variance, trade sample or net effect exists to estimate power honestly.
Count actual eligible dated rolls and measure input quality before any return trial; do not count both legs as
independent observations or make up a seasonal rule to produce a sample.

## 7. Priors and falsifiers
TIER_LIST records CL front-short business-days1–4 at t1.4 and earlier CL GSCI/BCOM timing tests at t0.5/0.7.
Those are outright proxies, not tests of the paired wheat spread, but prevent presenting another outright roll
clock as new. Broad commodity trend and weekly basis-reversal research already exist; neither is this exact flow.
A later registered spread prediction must beat native costs and matched nonroll dates without hindsight calendar choice.

## 8. Failure and diversification
Crowding, changed index rules, different replacement months and offsetting hedges may remove price pressure.
Weather/inventory shocks, delivery constraints and weak deferred liquidity can overwhelm it. Two opposing legs
do not guarantee a small loss: relative prices, margin and leg execution can deteriorate together.
Joint personal-book losses, actual integer exposure and chronological margins remain unmeasured.
