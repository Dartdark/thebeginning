# Nasdaq dealer concentration: DON'T TEST as a standalone futures strategy

Summary: Historical concentration/spread relationship is useful execution context, not a trading rule.
Dealer economic rents are inferred rather than measured net profits. No current NQ/ES edge or account benefit.
Source: [review](../results/nasdaq_dealer_486642_review.md), Kim/Mehrotra, September2003, SSRN486642.
New financial return trials: zero.

## Participant and cause

Incumbent stock dealers may have order-flow/information advantages. No forced timed futures order or obligation affecting our entry is identified. A rent paid to another participant is not automatically accessible alpha.

## Direction, size and clock

No directional signal, stop/exit, order type or integer quantity. Monthly concentration and contemporaneous regression outputs require original publication/receipt clocks and lagged estimation before prospective use.

## Transmission and independent data

Individual-stock dealer volumes/identities, effective spreads and IPO history differ from NQ/ES minute exports. Bounded filename search found no matching dealer/TAQ panel. No proxy input, current cost coefficient or dealer identity imputed.

## Costs, power and selection

Source sample months are not strategy trials/trades. Full effective spread differs from one-way cost and realized/net dealer P&L. No measured own-cost saving, eligible-event count or return power; arithmetic checks only.

## Failures and portfolio risk

Concentration/spread endogeneity, changing market design, stale quotes, inventory/adverse-selection costs and censored halving times prevent mechanical extrapolation. No measured common-loss reduction, liquidity role for our orders or offset before account breach.

## Decision

Retain E10/E56 execution-measurement cautions. No promising/data-blocked strategy queue entry or economic preregistration warranted; no live or platform changes. Future research diagnostics require a separately justified question and genuine synchronized native data.
