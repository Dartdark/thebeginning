    Strategy-Relative Market Regimes as Filtration Compressions
                                       Paul Alexander Bilokon

                                              1 April 2026


                                                Abstract
         Classical notions of market regimes are defined at the level of the underlying price process.
     We propose a different formalisation: regimes are defined relative to a given trading strategy
     through the conditional law of future profit and loss (PnL). We model regimes as adapted binary
     processes that compress the filtration and separate the distribution of future PnL while remain-
     ing temporally persistent. We formulate this as a stochastic control problem with switching
     costs, prove existence and uniqueness of optimal regime processes under mild conditions, and
     interpret regimes as minimal predictive state variables for strategy performance. Connections
     to information-theoretic formulations are discussed.


1    Introduction
Market regimes are typically defined as latent or observable states governing the dynamics of
an underlying price process. In such approaches, the regime is a property of the market itself,
independent of any trading strategy.
   However, in practice, the relevant object is not the behaviour of the price process but the
behaviour of strategy PnL. The same market dynamics may be favourable for one strategy and
unfavourable for another. This suggests that regimes should be defined relative to a strategy
rather than intrinsically at the level of the market.
   We propose a formalisation in which a regime is an adapted binary process that summarises
available information and separates the conditional distribution of future PnL. The regime is thus a
compression of the filtration that preserves predictive information relevant for trading performance.

10    Conclusion
We have proposed a definition of market regimes as adapted binary processes that compress the fil-
tration while preserving predictive information about future strategy PnL. The resulting framework
yields existence and uniqueness results, admits a dynamic programming characterisation, and pro-
vides a natural bridge between stochastic control and information-theoretic approaches to regime
detection.
