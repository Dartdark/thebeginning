// The data
Every number in this paper is computed from official CME
Market-By-Order (MBO) Level 3 data: the complete order-by-
order record of the NQ book, May 21, 2017 to July 6, 2026.
This is the same data class institutional desks buy. From it,
The Bridge reconstructs the full order book and serves
condensed per-minute features into MultiCharts, where every
test below is a plain PowerLanguage backtest.

trade of each minute: its signed size (buyer- or seller-

WHAT WE DID, STEP BY STEP.
 1. Built the dataset. Nine years of NQ MBO history fetched
    contract by contract, roll-spliced into one continuous
    feature file and one matching price series, both from the
    same tape.

 2. Verified alignment. Confirmed served features line up
    with chart candles before any strategy was trusted.

 3. Established the baseline. Long after every green candle,
    short after every red one, hold exactly one minute.
    2,991,516 trades: what the candle alone is worth.

 4. Measured the whale. Same one-minute bet, taken only
    when the minute's biggest print reached 100 contracts, in
    the print's direction. 6,780 trades.

 5. Swept the horizon. Holding period 1 to 30 minutes,
    mapping how long the print's information keeps paying
    before it decays.

 6. Swept the threshold. Minimum print size 10 to 300
    contracts at three horizons: where size starts to mean
    something, and where it stops.

 7. Split the sessions. Cash hours against the overnight,
    same rules.

 8. Located the print. Wick against body: does it matter
    where inside the candle the whale executed?

 9. Read the room. Classified every whale by the resting-
    book imbalance it arrived into: aligned, opposed, or
    balanced.

10. Assembled and stressed the final configuration. Full
    trade distribution, per-trade adverse excursion, 1,000

THE RESTING BOOK.
Level 3 data lets us do one more thing no candle can: read
the resting order book the whale traded into. For every whale
minute we compute the depth imbalance across the top 50
price levels per side, and classify the book as aligned
(leaning the print's way, imbalance beyond 10%), opposed
(leaning against it), or balanced (inside 10%).

The geometry is the healthy kind. A coin-flip hit rate
(50.87%) paired with a payoff ratio of 1.07 and a right tail
three times longer than the left: the largest winner is $14,765
against a largest loser of $4,285, and the outlier population
as a whole contributes positively (+$11,410 across 336
outliers). No single trade carries the P&L; the edge is diffuse,
which is what a structural effect should look like.

Adverse excursion. The average trade endures a maximum
adverse excursion of about $442 on its way to an average
result of $27, and the worst single excursion is $5,200. That
is the empirical case for the no-stop design: a stop tight

      AskSz = OFB_AskDepth( Levels );

      if BidSz + AskSz > 0 then
          Imb = ( BidSz - AskSz ) / ( BidSz + AskSz ) * 100;
