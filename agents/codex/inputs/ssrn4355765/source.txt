       There is increased interest in the dynamic relationships between cryptocurrency and commod-

       1     Introduction

       In recent years, cryptocurrency has experienced rapid development and received considerable at-




                                                                                  iew
       tention. It is considered an alternative investment vehicle, providing a highly profitable opportunity

       and great flexibility. These apparent characteristics of cryptocurrency have promoted investors to

       construct new investment strategies that differ from traditional investments (Pal and Mitra, 2009;




                                                                        ev
       Ma, Tian, Hsiao, and Deng 2022; Bergsli, Lind, Molnar, and Polasik, 2022). However, cryptocur-

       rency has higher volatility and risk than traditional investment assets and, therefore, requires more

       attention from investors. The increasing interest in cryptocurrency has led to a need for research




                                                             r
       on the hedging strategy that could offset or mitigate risk with traditional investment.

                                                          er
           Investment strategies that include cryptocurrency are widely used to achieve high returns. Al-

       though cryptocurrencies are highly volatile and risky, given their previously mentioned characteris-
                                               pe
       tics, the return on portfolios that include cryptocurrencies is relatively high (Gandal and Halaburda,

       2014; Eisl, Gasser, and Weinmayer, 2015). Several studies have investigated portfolios that include

       virtual currencies and also hedging assets with cryptocurrencies (Bouri, Azzi, and Haubo, 2016;
                                     ot

       Baur, Lee, and Hong, 2018; Park, 2022). For instance, Dyhberg (2015a, b) studies the hedging

       capability of cryptocurrency with commodities, equities, and the US dollar and finds that Bitcoin
                          tn



       acts as a hedge against uncertainty like other equities. Recent research shows that due to the weak

       positive or even negative correlation between cryptocurrency and equities, cryptocurrency acts as

       an effective diversifier and hedge in portfolios (Bouri, Molnar, Azzi, Roubaud, and Hagfors, 2017).
                rin




           An asset can be defined as a hedge, diversifier, and haven according to their relationships (Baur

       and Lucey, 2010; Ratner and Chiu, 2013). If an asset is uncorrelated or negatively correlated with
     ep




       another asset or portfolio, it is a hedge. However, if the asset shows this relationship in only the

       stress or turmoil of market conditions, it is a haven. Unlike a hedge and haven, a diversifier is an

       asset that, on average, positively correlates with another asset or portfolio. Gold has often been
Pr

                                                                                     ed
       used as a hedge for risky assets, and many studies have found that it is a hedge and haven for

       stocks and equities (Baur and Lucey, 2010; Baur and McDermott, 2010; Bredin, Conlon, and Poti,

       2015; Lucey and Li, 2014; Ciner, Gurdgiev, and Lucey, 2013). However, recently, some investors




                                                                                  iew
       have interest in alternative assets other than traditional investment assets, so cryptocurrencies are

       considered an alternative investment asset. Given the surge in interest, cryptocurrencies have been

       examined to identify their role and hedging capabilities against assets (Dyhrberg, 2016; Bouri,




                                                                        ev
       Gupta, Tiwari, and Roubaud, 2017; Bouri, Molnár, Azzi, Roubaud, and Hagfors, 2017; Corbet,

       Meegan, Larkin, Lucey, and Yarovaya, 2018).

           This study aims to investigate the relationship between cryptocurrency and commodity futures

       and determine whether cryptocurrency can be classified as a hedge against commodity futures. We

                                                          er
       attempt to capture the dynamic conditional correlations among assets by employing dynamic con-

       ditional correlation (DCC) and asymmetric dynamic conditional correlation (ADCC) multivariate
                                               pe
       generalized autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity (MGARCH) models. These models help

       uncover behavior of optimal hedge ratios and hedging effectiveness using conditional volatility and

       correlation estimates.
                                     ot

       examining the hedging capacities of traditional futures investment against cryptocurrency. To this

       end, we consider the four most commonly invested futures in financial markets: copper, gas, gold,

       and crude oil. To our knowledge, no other study has investigated the hedging capacities between
                rin




       cryptocurrency and commodity futures. We construct one-step-ahead hedge ratios using a rolling

       window scheme based on dynamic conditional correlation models, enabling us to analyze the effect

       of changing asset variability on the hedge ratios. Moreover, we evaluate the performance of the
     ep




       hedged portfolios according to the amount of variance reduction compared with unhedged portfo-

       lios.

       4     Concluding Remarks




                                                                                  iew
       In this paper, we used the multivariate GARCH models to explore the usefulness of using copper,

       gas, gold, and oil futures to hedge the risk associated with cryptocurrency investing. Using the




                                                                        ev
       DCC- and ADCC-MGARCH models, we evaluate the dynamic conditional correlations and one-

       step-ahead volatility to calculate and compare optimal hedge ratios and hedge effectiveness.

           In analyzing the relationships between commodity futures and Bitcoin, examining the hedging




                                                             r
       capacities of traditional futures investment for cryptocurrency is of great interest. To this end, we

                                                          er
       consider four of the most commonly invested futures in financial markets: copper, gas, gold, and

       crude oil. To our knowledge, no other study has investigated the out-of-sample hedging perfor-
                                               pe
       mance between cryptocurrency and commodity futures. We construct one-step-ahead hedge ratios

       using a rolling window scheme based on dynamic conditional correlation models, enabling us to

       analyze the changing variability in Bitcoin and commodity futures. Moreover, we evaluate the
                                     ot

       performance of the hedged portfolio by the amount of variance reduction compared with the un-

       hedged portfolio.
                           tn



           From the empirical analysis, we find that futures of gas and gold have hedge benefits. However,

       crude oil shows poor hedging performance. We also find that dynamic conditional correlations be-
