The trade story
Internal bar strength (IBS) says where a session closed inside its own range: 0 at the low, 1 at the high. When an
index future ends its cash session in the bottom fifth of the day's range, the day's selling ran right into the close.
S30 buys just after the close and holds until a session closes in the top fifth of its range, usually a few days later
(median hold 4 calendar days). The usual explanation is short-term overreaction: selling that has to be done by the
end of the day (risk cuts, stops, hedges) pushes price too far, and whoever buys it is paid by the bounce. S30 does
not measure who sells; it trades the published pattern on three index futures with one rule.
Rules
 Session                              The 08:30–15:00 CT cash session: 26 fifteen-minute bars on a full-session chart (bar stamps 08:45
                                      … 15:00).

 Signal                               At the 15:00 CT bar: IBS = (close − session low) ÷ (session high − session low).

 Entry                                Flat and IBS < 0.20: buy one contract at market on the next bar (15:00 CT; the futures keep
                                      trading).

 Exit                                 Long and IBS > 0.80: sell at market on the next bar.

 Nothing else                         No stop, target, filter or time exit. Positions are held through nights, weekends, holidays and
                                      contract rolls. A session with missing bars or no range makes no decision. The same file runs on
                                      @NQ, @ES and @RTY, one chart each.
