                                          ABSTRACT:



This study examines the performance of the Donchian Channel breakout strategy enhanced with

 the ATR-based volatility regime filter and ATR based risk management. Using BTC (Bitcoin)

daily data, the strategy enters long and short positions on range breakouts while restricting high

   volatility trades. Backtesting results indicate that the strategy significantly outperformed a

  passive based approach over the tested period, however these results were very specific and

     narrow and were highly sensitive to transactional costs, parameter settings, and market

conditions. The findings highlight the importance of volatility filtering and risk controls in trendy

                               systems that follow crypto markets.

                                      INTRODUCTION:

       Trend-following strategies have long been studied in financial markets, particularly

commodities and futures trading. Richard Donchian’s breakout methodology remains one of the

most popular and widely referenced systematic approaches.


Cryptocurrency markets provide a unique testing environment due to:


   -   High Volatility

   -   Strong directional regimes

   -   24/7 trading unlike stocks


This study investigates whether a Donchian Channel Breakout strategy, combined with volatility

filtering and ATR-based position risk management, can produce superior risk-adjusted returns

compared to passive holding in Bitcoin markets.




                                         METHODS:

2.1 Donchian Channel Construction

The Donchian Channel is defined as:

   •   Upper Band = Highest high over last N bars

   •   Lower Band = Lowest low over last N bars

Breakout conditions:

   •   Enter Long when price closes above Upper [1]

   •   Enter Short when price closes below Lower [1]
                                                                                      Poluri 3


Where Upper [1] and Lower [1] represent the prior period channel values to prevent look-ahead

bias.

Baseline parameter:

    •    Donchian Length (dcLen) = 55

2.2 EMA Trend Filter (Optional)

To reduce counter-trend trades:

    •    Long positions only if price > EMA(emaLen)

    •    Short positions only if price < EMA(emaLen)

Parameter:

    •    EMA Length = 200

This filter attempts to align breakout signals with broader trend direction.

2.3 Volatility Regime Filter

To avoid low-volatility whipsaw environments, trades are only allowed when:

ATR ≥ atrMult × SMA(ATR, atrBaseLen)

Where:

    •    ATR Length = 14

    •    atrBaseLen = 50

    •    atrMult = 1.0

This restricts trading to higher-than-average volatility periods.

2.4 Risk Management

Risk is controlled using ATR-based stops.

Stop Loss:

Stop Distance = riskATR × ATR

Parameter:

   •   riskATR = 2.0

Optional trailing stop:

Trail Distance = trailATR × ATR

   •   trailATR = 2.5

This creates dynamic risk scaling based on volatility.



                                        LIMITATIONS:

This study has several limitations:

   •   Slippage not fully modeled

   •   Fee assumption may be unrealistically low for many exchanges

   •   Single asset focus (BTC)

   •   No walk-forward testing

   •   Single time window, which can’t be replicated for all the other cryptocurrencies

Furthermore, crypto markets exhibit regime shifts that may reduce future effectiveness. With the

recent tank in BTC prices and ETH, volatility uncertainty has only increased, thereby making

this model further unstable.

                                         CONCLUSION:

        The Donchian Channel breakout strategy, when combined with volatility regime filtering

and ATR-based dynamic risk management, demonstrated strong performance in trending

cryptocurrency markets. However, results were highly regime-dependent and sensitive to

transaction costs and parameter choices. While the system appears robust in trending

environments, its effectiveness declines when no active trends are present. These findings

reinforce the importance of volatility filtering and adaptive risk controls in systematic trading

strategies.
