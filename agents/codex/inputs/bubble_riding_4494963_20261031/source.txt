             1     Introduction
                  rin




                 Stock market bubbles have provided a significant financial risk and resulted in heavy

             losses to global investors in history, making the reaction of investors facing bubbles a
        ep




             focal point of attention for academics. Fama (2014) states that the studies on bubbles

             (Shiller, 2000; Kindleberger et al., 2005) rely on beliefs that stock price declines are
Pr




             predictable and argues that stock prices are unpredictable and that rational investors

             correct mispricing by arbitrage. However, Greenwood et al. (2019) report that sharp




This preprint research paper has not been peer reviewed. Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4494963
             price increases predict heightened probabilities of crashes, and investors can identify




                                                                                    ed
             bubbles by some stock characteristics containing information relevant to forecasting

             expected returns. Abreu and Brunnermeier (2003) even suggest that the optimal choice




                                                                                 iew
             for investors discovering a bubble is to ride it. This paper will address whether we can

             predict price dynamics to bet against bubbles. We construct eﬀective bubble-riding

             strategies based on advanced bubble detection techniques in the Chinese stock market.




                                                        v
             If the trading strategies consistently yield abnormal returns, it’s feasible for investors




                                                     re
             to identify bubbles and benefit from riding bubbles.

                According to Temin and Voth (2004), Hoare’s Bank successfully rode the South Sea
                                                  er
             Bubble by trading the overvalued stocks of South Sea Company. Brown et al. (2000),
                                                pe
             Brunnermeier and Nagel (2004), and Fung and Hsieh (2000) report that hedge funds

             profited from bubbles by holding portfolios heavily tilted toward highly-priced

             technology stocks during the technology bubble period. Brooks and Katsaris (2005) use
                                      ot


             a three-regime model to study the bubble dynamics of the S&P 500 and form a bubble

             trading rule based on this model. Guenster and Kole (2013) examined investors’
                            tn




             decisions in the face of bubbles, proving that even individual investors may profit from

             investing in bubbles.
                  rin

             China Securities Index 300 (CSI300) market, including the most representative Chinese
        ep




             sample stocks. The GSADF test has been proven more effective than the SADF test of

             Phillips et al. (2011) and the methods of Hall et al. (1999) and Guenster et al. (2009).
Pr




             Then we construct three bubble-riding strategies based on the GSADF test, Strategies

                                                                                 iew
             in the sector markets at the weekly frequency and then explore their profitability against

             the benchmark (Strategy 4). The results show that our riding strategies perform better

                                                        v
             Chinese market.




                                                     re
                 The paper contributes to the literature by examining the profitability of the “riding

             bubble” strategies in the Chinese sector-level stock market. These trading strategies try
                                                  er

                                                pe

             Chinese investors facing bubbles. Additionally, the study unit of the paper is a sector

             characteristics of Chinese bubbles.

             2.2.1   Rules of bubble-riding strategies

                Strategy 1 buys any sector for which the GSADF test detects a bubble episode (the
        ep




             PSYI equals 1) and closes the positions when the bubble collapses (the PSYI

             switches from 1 to 0).
Pr




                Strategy 2 uses the MPSYI instead of the PSYI. Specifically, Strategy 2 invests in

             the sectors which experience bubbles and generate positive recent returns (the MPSYI




This preprint research paper has not been peer reviewed. Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4494963
             equals 1) and sells those sectors’ stocks if the bubbles burst or the recent returns turn




                                                                                    ed
             negative (the MPSYI equals 0).

                 Strategy 3 buys those sectors where the bubbles continuously exist at both the




                                                                                 iew
             previous and current periods (the DPSYI equals 1) and exits from the collapsing

             bubbles (the DPSYI turns 0).

                 If there are multiple sectors satisfying their conditions at the same time, these




                                                        v
             strategies will construct an equally-weighted portfolio. In the event of no bubbles, they




                                                     re
             hold the market index.


             2.2.2    Analysis of trading strategies
                                                  er
                 All bubble riding strategies try allocating the funds to the bubble sectors. Strategy 1
                                                pe
             relies only on the PSY method (i.e., the GSADF test) to time bubble sectors. However,

             the GSADF test can’t always keep bubble detection accurate. The false identification
                                      ot


             influences Strategy 1’s performance adversely. To reduce the impact of the test bias,

             Strategy 2 exploits the MPSYI that introduces the recent return information. Strategy 3
                            tn



             raises the investment threshold to avoid entering a sector too early by observing

             additionally the bubble signal at the previous period.
