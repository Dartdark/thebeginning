Trading Activity of Commodity Futures and
  Options Around USDA Announcements

                                   Tianyang Zhang



                                           Abstract

    The USDA announcement effects on commodity options have received limited atten-

 tion in the literature. We find that the trading volumes of futures and options fluctuate

 significantly before and after the release of the monthly World Agricultural Supply and

 Demand Estimates (WASDE) reports for seven agricultural commodities. The effects

 of the announcements on commodity options are less significant than those on futures.

 Furthermore, we provide evidence that informed trading exists in commodity markets

 before the release date. The information contained in the commodity futures and op-

 tions has a longer predictive horizon for post-announcement futures returns than the

 ending stocks forecasts in the reports.

1     Introduction

As a major public source of forecasts since the year 1973, the World Agricultural Supply
and Demand Estimates (WASDE) reports from the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)
play an important role in agriculture markets. Not surprisingly, the announcement effects
of USDA predictions on agricultural markets have been studied a lot. Most of these studies
focus on how the commodity futures markets are affected and provide evidence to confirm
whether the effects exist. For example, Adjemian and Irwin (2018) finds that the volatility
of agriculture futures spikes right after the release of WASDE reports but declines in a few
minutes. Adjemian (2012) analyzes the absolute value of overnight return before and after
the announcement date and confirms that the USDA announcement effect persists across
contract positions. There is also evidence that the co-movement of different commodity
futures returns exists around the release date of USDA predictions (Karali, 2012). Other
prominent examples of this line of work include Irwin et al. (2001), Indriawan et al. (2021),
and Ying et al. (2019), among others.
    However, as a critical part of commodity markets, whether commodity options markets
are affected by USDA announcements has been ignored in most cases. One example is from
Randall and Summer (1993), who use the daily data from year 1968 to 1989 and provide
some evidence that there is a USDA report effect in commodity futures and options markets.
In recent decades, the commodity options markets have developed rapidly and become an
important derivative in commodity markets. A commodity option contract is written with
a particular futures contract as the underlying security. Buying a call (put) option gives the
owner the right to buy (sell) the underlying commodity futures at a fixed price or the strike
price on or before the contract expiration date. The physical commodity option was first
introduced on February 15, 1985, as the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)
approved Amex Commodity Corporation’s physical gold bullion option contract.
    As an emerging market, how the commodity options market works has not been studied
much in the previous literature. Recently, Zhang (2022) examines hedging pressure and

                  Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4841490
liquidity provision in commodity options markets. Cheng et al. (2021) also confirms the ex-
istence of hedging premiums and demand effects in commodity options markets. In addition,
empirical results find that the commodity options can have a large effect on the underlying
asset commodity futures (Luo et al., 2021).
   The present study focuses on the commodity options for two main reasons. First, pro-
ducers would employ options to hedge production and other risks. Lapan et al. (1991) build
a theoretical model and shows that when market prices are biased, commodity options must
be included to construct optimal hedging strategies for the producers. Moschini and Lapan
(1995) also shows that the options can be used to hedge the joint production and price
risk for production firms. Therefore, after the WASDE reports are released every month,
producers have an incentive to adjust their hedging strategies by trading commodity fu-
tures and options. This will cause changes in trading behaviors of options and futures after
the announcements. For example, Cao and Robe (2022) find that the corn and soybean
option-implied volatilities are significantly lower for several trading days after the reports
are released. In this study, we provide new evidence that there exists USDA announcement
effects in commodity options markets, although these effects are less significant than those
in commodity futures markets.
   Second, commodity traders would use options to speculate and make profits. From the
perspective view of informed trading, the trading activity in options markets also contains
information about the future condition of agriculture markets. Empirical analysis shows that
options lead futures in reflecting new information in most commodities and speculation is a
key determinant of price discovery (Bohmann et al., 2019). Thus, an interesting topic is to
explore and compare the predictability of information in commodity markets and WASDE
reports before the announcements. In this study, we use ending stocks forecasts in the reports
as benchmark forecasts.
   Our study is also related to the literature on the forecasts from financial markets for
agriculture information. This study not only investigates the USDA announcement effects in


                                                3

                  Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4841490
commodity futures and options markets but also explores the information content in these
two markets in the pre-announcement period. Informed market participants may possess
information pertaining to WASDE reports and commodities markets that extends beyond
the information contained within the reports. We found that the information in commodity
options and futures markets has a longer predictive horizon than the ending stocks forecasts
from WASDE reports.
    We make three main contributions to the literature. First, we investigate the USDA an-
nouncement effects on commodity options markets based on trading volumes, which has not
been empirically studied in the previous literature. Second, our study finds that the USDA
announcement effects in commodity futures are more significant than that in commodity
options, while the options markets could be involved with more informed trading activities
before the WASDE reports are released. Third, the ending stocks forecasts are observed
to exhibit predictive efficacy within the five trading days following the release, with this
predictive capability diminishing rapidly thereafter. More importantly, this study concludes
that the information in commodity futures and options markets has longer predictive power
than the ending stocks forecasts in the WASDE reports.
    The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the data of
commodity markets and WASDE reports used in this study. The empirical analysis of USDA
announcement effects on commodity futures and options markets is in Section 3. Section
4 presents the predictive power of trading volumes in commodity markets and the ending
stocks forecasts from the reports. Section 5 concludes.



2     Data

The data of commodity markets used in this study are from Bloomberg and comprise in-
dividual futures contracts for seven agricultural commodities including soybean oil, corn,
cotton, soybeans, oats, sugar, soybean meal, and wheat. This study encompasses data span-

                  Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4841490
ning from January 1995 to December 2018. We follow Zhang (2022) and select a sample
of each commodity according to the options expiration dates, which is presented in Figure
1. Specifically, for each futures contract, we include the sample from the Tuesday of the
week before the option expiration date to 65 calendar days before the expiration date of the
option. To avoid the problems of outliers and the low liquidity in commodity futures and
options markets, we drop the two-sided 1% data based on the value of options to futures
trading volumes ratio. By applying the specified data constraints, the sample in our study
comprises 42691 commodity-daily observations.
   The summary statistics for commodities are in Table 1. The trading volumes of futures
and options are larger in corn markets than the other commodities, while cotton and soybean
meal have the smallest volumes in futures and options, respectively. Soybeans and wheat hold
the second and third positions in terms of both futures and options trading volumes. There
is an observable phenomenon that a commodity with substantial futures trading volumes
typically exhibit corresponding high options trading volumes. However, this trend does not
hold true for cotton, as it possesses the lowest futures trading volumes, yet its options trading
volumes rank as the third lowest among all seven commodities. Thus, it is not surprising
that the options-to-futures trading volumes ratio is largest in cotton, while the lowest ratio
is observed in the soybean oil markets.
   The forecasts of commodity markets are from USDA, an entity that publishes the monthly
WASDE reports to announce current and expected market conditions for agricultural com-
modities to participants in commodity markets. The number of USDA monthly forecasts
including in this study is 288, since the the data covers the period from January 1995 to
December 2018. A crucial forecast from WASDE reports is the expected ending stocks at
the end of each marketing year. Ending stocks, also referred to as carryout, are the amount
of a commodity left over after all demand has been satisfied and enters the supply side of
the market in the following marketing year. According to Good and Irwin (2014), ending
stocks may be the single most important factor that summarizes the price implications for

a commodity. Therefore, in this study, we set the ending stock forecast as the target to
compare the predictive power between the information in commodity markets and WASDE
reports. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the predictive
capacity of ending stocks forecasts in futures returns of agriculture commodities.



3     USDA announcement effect

3.1    Abnormal values of futures and options volumes

Intuitively, the futures and options trading activities around the announcement date could
be significantly different from the activities on other dates in the same month. In order
to investigate the announcement effect, we conduct the analysis about whether there are
significant abnormal values of futures and options trading volumes, just before and after the
announcement date.
    Following Adjemian (2012), an event window of eleven days, is established before and
after report publication. First, we separate the trading days for commodity i in month m
into two groups according to the announcement date Wm . One group consists of the ordinary
trading days Wm that are in the interval [Wm − 5, Wm + 5], which covers the five trading
days before and after the announcement. Another group is made of the trading days W−m
that are outside of the days in interval [Wm − 5, Wm + 5] in the same month. Next, for
commodity i in month m on day t, the measures for abnormal values of futures and options
volumes are constructed as the following:

   Tables 3 to 6 and Figures 2 to 5 report the results of cross-sectional regressions and
show the abnormal values of futures and options volumes associated with the event window
and the marginal effect of the WASDE reports on the announcement day. In the futures
markets, there are distinct spikes of abnormal futures volumes on the announcement date
across all seven commodities, which indicates that the abnormal values reach their peak on
the release date within the event window, which encompasses the five trading days before
and after the announcement date. On and after the release date, when the traders gain new
information from WASDE reports, whether it is good or bad news, they would adjust their
trading positions in accordance with the new information they get. However, the reactions
of different commodity markets are not the same. For corn, soybeans, and wheat, there is
a sharp increase in abnormal futures volumes on the day of the announcement accompanied



                                                8

                  Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4841490
by a rapid decrease in abnormal values just one day after the announcement, which indicates
that the information from WASDE reports is reflected on the futures contracts shortly in
these markets. In cases of the other four commodities including soybean oil, cotton, sugar,
and soybean meal, the decreasing trend of abnormal futures volumes after the announcement
also appears, but the decline rate is lower. Regarding the abnormal futures volumes in the
pre-announcement period, there are also relatively significant abnormal futures volumes on
days before announcement compared with that on announcement day in soybean oil, cotton,
sugar, and soybean meal futures markets. A plausible explanation for this phenomenon lies
in traders’ capacity to adapt their futures investments in accordance with their anticipations
of commodity forecasts preceding the release date of these reports. This would result in
greater fluctuations in trading volumes within the five days leading up to the release date
compared to the trading volumes observed during the remaining days of the month. Based
on the findings above, we can conclude that futures markets would react to the news from
WASDE reports right away after the announcement. In most cases, such reactions tend to
subside within five days following the release date.
   In commodity options markets, the impact of announcements is comparatively less signif-
icant than that observed in futures markets. In the cases of corn and soybeans, the behaviors
of options are similar to futures, with abnormal options volumes peaking on the release date
and subsequently diminishing within five trading days. However, it is important to highlight
that in the soybean oil, cotton, sugar, soybean meal, and wheat options markets, there is an
absence of distinct spikes in abnormal options volumes on the release date. Following the
announcement date, it takes a longer time for options volumes back to normal in comparison
to futures volumes across most commodity markets. This suggests that there is a prolonged
period required for the news in WASDE reports to be fully incorporated in options markets.
Interestingly, the abnormal options volumes are even higher after the announcement date in
soybean oil, sugar, and sugar meal markets. One plausible hypothesis is that options traders
may require a longer time to execute adjustments to their positions in options as compared

to futures. During the pre-announcement period, notably substantial abnormal options vol-
umes are observed, particularly in soybean oil, cotton, sugar, and soybean meal. Compared
with futures markets, this phenomenon is more notable in options markets. One possible
explanation is that there are more informed trading activities involved in options markets
and informed traders appear to leverage their private information regarding WASDE reports
to adjust their positions in options to hedge risks or pursue profits.



4     Post-announcement returns analysis

In the previous section, results show that significant abnormal futures and options volumes
exist in the pre-announcement period, which could be evidence that some market partici-
pants make investments based on their private information before the WASDE reports are
released. Therefore, in this section, we examine the predictive power of futures and op-
tions trading volumes for futures returns following the announcement. We use four return
windows: CUM(+0,+5), CUM(+0,+10), CUM(+0,+15), and CUM(+0,+20). CUM(X,Y)
indicates the cumulative return for each commodity from X trading days to Y trading days
after the announcement date.
    In order to compare the predictive capability of futures and options volumes with the
forecasts in WASDE reports, we set the ending stocks forecasts as the benchmark target.

year. In every month, WASDE reports provide the prediction of ending stocks for each
commodity. The forecast of ending stock for commodity i in month m is defined as ESi,m .

in ending stocks in month m for commodity i is constructed as the difference between the
forests in this and the previous month divided by the forecast in the previous month:

5    Conclusion

In this study, we examine the USDA announcement effects on commodity markets. In the
previous literature, most related studies focus on the reactions in commodity futures markets
from WASDE reports, whether commodity options markets are affected has received much
less attention. However, as a critical part of commodity markets, it is of great importance to
explore how commodity options markets react to WASDE reports. We analyze how the ab-
normal futures and options volumes change before and after the released date and conclude
that the USDA announcement effects exist in both futures and options markets. Specifically,
the effects are more significant in futures than options. In most commodities, these effects
fade away within five days in most futures and options markets, while the decline speed
is slower in options. Furthermore, our study provides evidence that options markets are
involved with more informed trading behaviors than futures since there are more options
markets that exist significant abnormal options volumes during the pre-announcement pe-
riod. Motivated by these findings, we conduct the analysis of post-announcement returns
and compare the predictive power between the information in commodity markets and end-
ing stocks forecasts from WASDE reports. The results show that commodity futures and
options have a longer predictive horizon than ending stocks forecasts. Our work should
provide guidance for further work in the area of commodity options markets and WASDE
reports. For example, a detailed analysis to explore the reason why the signs of coefficients
for futures and options volumes in predicting post-announcement returns are opposite is an
interesting avenue for future work.

A      Model for ending stocks and futures prices

Following the rational storage model in Zhang and Li (2022), the amount of crop to be stored
to the following period shall be compensated by the carry in the futures markets:


                                Et [pT (zT + cT )] − pt = g(cT |ct )


where pT , cT , and zT are the commodity price, the level of ending stocks, and the new
production of the crop at the end of marketing year T . The spot price and the carryout of
commodity from the previous marketing year are pt and ct . What’s more, g(cT |ct ) is the cost
function of storing cT , conditional on the initial carryout ct .
    If we assume that there is the absence of a risk premium, the expected commodity prices
equal to futures prices with the same maturity date T :


                                     Et [pT (zT + cT )] = ft,T


since the price function pT is decreasing in cT , there is a negative relationship between cT
and ft,T .
