02                                          THE IDEA.

Hu, Jin and Wang (2025) study the autocorrelation of daily US stock returns from 1897 to 2024. It changes over time, but one feature is
stable, and appears in the main foreign markets too: rebounds are more likely after negative days. A daily strategy built on this
asymmetry earns a higher Sharpe ratio than the index.

Ricochet keeps only the asymmetric part and moves it to the E-mini Nasdaq-100 overnight. Two choices are ours: the down day is
measured from the open to the close of the regular session (the paper uses close to close), and the position is taken at the evening
reopen, 17:05 Chicago time.

03                                         THE SETUP ON THE PRICE.

▪      Signal. Yesterday’s regular session (08:30-15:00 Chicago) closed below its open, by any amount.
▪      Entry. 17:05 Chicago time, at the evening reopen, one E-mini NQ contract ($20 per point).
▪      Stop and target. 50 points below and 100 points above the entry, checked bar by bar; a bar that opens beyond a level exits at
       the open.
▪      Re-entry. After a stop or a target, buy again when the price touches the S1 pivot and closes back above it; one bar of pause; at most
       four trades per session.
▪      Time exit. 15:00 Chicago time.
▪      Costs. $9 per trade, included in every number.

09                                         VERDICT.

Ricochet does what most backtests do not: it stays positive on almost four years it never saw, with a stop on every trade and a simple
idea behind it. Two things keep it a case study: the out-of-sample t is just below 1.96, and the rebound it was built on is weak in the
data, while the re-entries do the work.

▪     Forward. Follow it on new data with the rules frozen as they are; no re-optimisation.
▪     Size. One NQ is $20 a point: on a prop account it would run on micro contracts.
▪     Afternoon. Out of sample, trades opened after noon lose (−$7,480): a known weak spot.

A                                         GLOSSARY.

▪     Development and out of sample. Development (to 25 Oct 2022): the data used to choose the rules. Out of sample (from 26 Oct 2022): data
      never used for any choice, opened once.
▪     Exploratory out of sample. Opened for a strategy the protocol does not admit: it counts as use of the data, never as promotion.
▪     t on sessions. The mean net of the sessions with trades divided by its standard error; around 2 means the result is unlikely to be chance.
▪     HAC. A standard error robust to changing variance and correlation between nearby sessions (Newey and West, 1987).
▪     AV threshold. The minimum mean net per trade for a market, set to cover costs and slippage: $70 on NQ.
▪     S1 pivot. The first support level of the classic floor-trader pivots, computed from the previous day’s high, low and close.
▪     Plateau (zone t). The t of a neighbourhood of parameters, not of the single best point.
