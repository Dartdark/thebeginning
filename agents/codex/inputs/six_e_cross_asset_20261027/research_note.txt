6E Cross-Asset Package (6 strategies) – Request for Feedback
HYPOTHESIS
The euro is one of the most efficient markets in the world. On its own chart (calendar effects, breakouts, mean reversion, volume) I couldn’t find anything that survives costs. But the euro absorbs information from other markets slowly: US bonds, oil, US stocks and gold. Over weeks it follows the trend in those markets, and after a big shock day in them it keeps adjusting the next day. Every strategy in this package trades the euro on what other markets did, not on what the euro did.
RESEARCH COMBINED (signal / timing / condition)
- Economic momentum: trends in macro fundamentals predict currencies (Dahlquist & Hasseltoft, “Economic Momentum and Currency Returns”).
- Cross-asset time-series momentum: one market’s trend predicts another’s (Pitkäjärvi, Suominen & Vaittinen 2020).
- Bonds lead other markets (Hong, Torous & Valkanov 2007).
- Order-flow shocks have persistent price impact in FX (Evans & Lyons 2002). This gives the next-day timing for the shock strategies.
- Risk-on / risk-off spillover into safe-haven currencies (Ranaldo & Söderlind 2010).
- Volatility-managed positions (Moreira & Muir 2017).
- Connors RSI(2) pullbacks and weekly breakouts, used only as entry timing inside the macro direction.
THE 6 STRATEGIES
Daily bars, decisions at the 16:00 ET close, 6E / M6E, multi-day holds. Personal account, not prop rules.
1. Macro Momentum + vol target. The position is the average of three trend signs, each a blend of 10/21/63-day trends:
Scaled to 5% annual volatility, capped at 1.5×.
- + ZN trend: bonds up = US yields down = euro up.
- − crude oil trend: Europe imports energy.
- − ES trend: money flows into US assets.


1. RSI(2) pullback in the macro direction. Buy RSI(2) < 10 only when Macro is long; sell RSI(2) > 90 only when Macro is short. Exit at the 5-day average or after 10 days.
2. Weekly breakout in the macro direction. Close beyond last week’s high or low, only in the Macro direction. Exit at the opposite level or after 10 days.
3. Macro-Shock Follow. If ZN moves more than 2 standard deviations vs its last 60 days, or the euro’s 8:29–9:30 ET data reaction does, hold the euro in the shock direction for 1 day. About 27 per year.
4. Stock-Shock Follow. If ES moves more than 2.5 standard deviations, hold the euro in the same direction as stocks for 1 day. About 8 per year.
5. Gold-Shock Follow. If gold moves more than 2.25 standard deviations, hold the euro in gold’s direction for 1 day. About 12 per year.
RESULTS
Python backtest on 1-minute 6E data, stitched from individual contracts and back-adjusted, Jan 2018 – Oct 2026. Costs: $1.20 per M6E position change.
Per 1 M6E:
- Macro Momentum + vol target: Sharpe 0.98 (2018–22 1.03 / 2023+ 0.91), ~$440/yr, max drawdown −$1,220.
- RSI(2) in macro direction: Sharpe 0.36 (0.55 / 0.05), ~$170/yr, ~14 trades/yr.
- Weekly breakout in macro direction: Sharpe 0.40 (0.25 / 0.56), ~$270/yr, ~14 trades/yr.
- Macro-Shock Follow: Sharpe 0.91 (1.14 / 0.50), ~$310/yr, max drawdown −$480.
- Stock-Shock Follow: Sharpe 0.87 (1.09 / 0.56), ~$210/yr.
- Gold-Shock Follow: Sharpe 0.95 (1.02 / 0.86), ~$260/yr.
Combined package (M6E: Macro 2, RSI2 1, Weekly 1, Macro-Shock 2, Stock-Shock 1, Gold-Shock 2):

ROBUSTNESS
- In-sample vs out-of-sample: every strategy is positive in both 2018–22 and 2023+.
- Placebo:
  - Each shock strategy beats 1,000 runs of random days with random directions; actual Sharpe is above the 99th percentile.
  - Macro Momentum beats 300 runs of its signals shifted randomly by 60–250 days (actual 0.90 vs 99th percentile 0.72).
- Plateau:
  - Gold-Shock is positive in both periods for every threshold 1.75–3.0 × hold 1–2 days (20 of 20 versions).
  - Macro-Shock thresholds 2.0–2.5 are all positive.
  - Macro Momentum lookback sets score 0.56–0.89.
- Walk-forward: parameters chosen on 2018–21 only, then tested out of sample on 2022–26:
  - Macro weights 0.99
  - Macro-Shock 0.82
  - Gold-Shock 0.57
  - Stock-Shock 0.31 (weakest leg, kept small)
- Acting one day late: Macro Momentum still scores 0.79.
- Control test: a walk-forward machine-learning model on 28 cross-asset features (retrained yearly, 2020–26 out of sample) scored Sharpe −0.14 / 0.04. The simple economic rules beat it, which I take as evidence the package isn’t just data mining.
WHAT FAILED ON 6E (so nobody repeats it)
- Trend following on the euro’s own chart: TSMOM 1–12 months, Donchian/Turtle, 50/200 MA.
- Retail setups on their own: RSI(2), Bollinger, inside bar, weekly breakout, Turnaround Tuesday, Sunday gap fill.
- Intraday:
  - London/Frankfurt/NY opening-range breakouts.
  - Asian-session mean reversion.
  - The ECB-fix pattern: real, but about 1 bp, too small after costs.
- Calendar effects:
  - FOMC days and the FOMC cycle.
  - Month-end and quarter-end dollar flows.
  - Day-of-week and month-of-year seasonality.
- Other: volume-conditioned reversal, macro fair-value reversion, 52-week-high anchoring, residual reversal and residual momentum.
PORTFOLIO FIT
- Correlation with my intraday NQ book: about 0.06.
- Correlation between the six strategies: −0.11 to 0.18, excluding the Macro legs.
- Different market, holding period (days to weeks) and drivers, so it’s real diversification for a personal account.
WEAK SPOTS
- Not verified in MultiCharts yet; Python only. The next step is one MultiCharts strategy with Data1 = M6E and Data2–5 = ZN, CL, ES, GC on daily bars.
- 2020 was flat (COVID whipsaw across bonds, oil and stocks).
- Three of the six are one-day shock follows. Different triggers and low correlation, but the same mechanism: slow absorption of big news. If markets start pricing news faster, they’d weaken together.
- The weaker legs: RSI2-in-macro has faded since 2023 (0.05), and Stock-Shock had the weakest walk-forward (0.31).
- One currency only. Carry and cross-currency momentum aren’t possible without more pairs.
