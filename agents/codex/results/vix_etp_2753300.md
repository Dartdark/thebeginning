## What is it about?

Three distinct questions: price discovery between volatility ETP pairs, using long-volatility ETPs to hedge
equities, and a daily basis-triggered multiday volatility strategy. ETPs hold rolling VIX futures exposure,
not spot VIX. Their index roll, daily reset, fees, issuer terms and actual market prices matter.

The strategy/hedging data spans June 21, 2006–April 25, 2014: artificial prelaunch indicative values purchased
from Six Figure Investing, then Yahoo market closes after inception. Price-discovery tests instead use
one-minute WRDS TAQ data through December 2012, excluding the first/last 15 minutes. Do not mix those samples.

## What does it find?

The source identifies persistent negative carry for long short-term VIX products in its sample, and poor
long-run equity-hedging performance after ETP inception. But the blanket claim that all hedge portfolios
worsen risk-adjusted returns is broader than its own Table 9: the 90-day rebalanced VXZ/equity example has a
slightly higher daily Sharpe than unhedged equities (0.023 vs 0.021), at much lower returns (3.3% vs 5.1%
annualized). The authors discuss sensitivity to rebalancing and the synthetic 2008 crisis exposure.
Do not interpret low correlation or lower daily volatility alone as a useful hedge for our actual account.

Table 7 reports common-factor weights: VXX 0.75 versus VIXY 0.25, XIV 0.54 versus SVXY 0.46, UVXY 0.57 versus
TVIX 0.43. Introductory prose swaps the inverse/levered leadership description; table/body/conclusion are the
record here. Statistical price leadership is not a tested latency, queue or after-cost arbitrage strategy.

### Trading rule in Section 5 / Table 10

Interpret relative basis as `(front-month VX − spot VIX) / spot VIX`; source prose describes a percentage
difference but implementation/denominator and roll conventions still require source-code reconciliation.

- Enter XIV when basis > +8%; retain until basis < +6%.
- Enter VXX when basis < −8%; retain until basis > −6%.
- Otherwise stay in cash when flat; switch products if the opposite entry condition is met.
- Optional hedge: allocate a fixed capital fraction to SH alongside XIV, or SPY alongside VXX.
  The table's hedge ratio is a capital weight, not an estimated beta hedge or a futures contract count.
- No fixed protective stop is specified. Source mean time between changes is 18 trading days.

## Why might it make money?

Compensation for bearing volatility/crash-insurance risk. A futures premium can converge and benefit inverse
exposure while long exposure may benefit in backwardation. This is not cash-and-carry arbitrage: spot VIX
cannot be held/delivered, basis is not guaranteed P&L, and futures prices can jump before an exit.
Positive front-future/spot basis is also not identical to front/second-futures contango. Keep those definitions
separate from the VIX/VIX3M curve proxy in the existing portfolio sleeve.

## Can we build on it?

Not yet as a causal historical reproduction, and not as the current source implementation.

**Timing:** pp.20–21 use ETP/spot closing values and front-futures settlement after closing, yet describe
deciding just before the end and fill at the ETP close. Actual settlement publication and all signal inputs
must be known before the trade. A preclose snapshot, after-hours trade or next-session open is a distinct
protocol requiring its own data and preregistration. A source-sized spread penalty is not a substitute.
The repository's primary-clock review in E51 already establishes VX settlement-reference changes over time.

**Product lifecycle:** the [issuer's SEC-filed notice](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1053092/000095010318001572/dp86358_ex9901.htm)
states XIV triggered acceleration after its February 5, 2018 intraday indicative value reached 20% or less
of the preceding close. That is material counterevidence to extrapolating a 2014-ended inverse-product backtest.
It does **not** establish that this specific switching rule held XIV or suffered that loss: such a claim
would require an audited position replay. Termination/redemption and proceeds must remain in any continuation.

[ProShares' SEC-filed notice](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1415311/000119312518059052/d503117dex991.htm)
changes SVXY from −1x to −0.5x and UVXY from 2x to 1.5x, effective after February 27, 2018. Substituting SVXY,
short VIXY, modern SVIX or synthetic perpetual XIV changes payoffs, reset/financing, timing and tail behavior.
No such substitution was selected for this review. This is an ETF/ETN multiday strategy; none of the four Apex
intraday futures homes receives it as written. A personal-account adaptation remains a separate proposal.
