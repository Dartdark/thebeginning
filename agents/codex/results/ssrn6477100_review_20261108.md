## 1. What is it about?

An account of iterative attempts to build a cryptocurrency trading bot and whether a regime switch or added
complexity improves it. The author combines RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands and Fear & Greed; adds a Trend Score,
different position sizes/entry blocks, Q-learning and sentiment variants; then interprets results using complex
adaptive systems and proposes a division of work between humans and automated execution.

The main sample uses Binance hourly OHLCV for BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, XRP, ADA, DOGE, AVAX, LINK and DOT,
June 2022–March 2026, split into eight periods. The paper describes 80 main asset-period experiments and more
than 160 total experiments. It selects surviving assets using 2026 rankings, explicitly acknowledging survivorship.
These counts are **not independent market histories or independent trading opportunities**.

## 2. What does it find?

### Main study: improvements still leave negative returns

Table 1, p. 8, reports means across 80 asset-period experiments, **not annualized portfolio returns**:

| Author profile | Mean period return | Reported mean alpha | Mean drawdown | Win rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V1 base bot | −4.33% | −19.08% | 6.0% | 38.4% |
| V3 dual-mode | −2.76% | −17.52% | 8.6% | 50.1% |
| Buy-and-hold | +14.87% | — | Not supplied | — |

The V1→V3 paired comparison has author p=.043 and small effect d=.230. Better average return and win rate do
not establish profitability; reported mean drawdown increases. Removing Flat23 changes V3's alpha from −17.52%
to −3.69% (p=.565). The author appropriately reports sensitivity and acknowledges that Flat23 is not a formally
significant period-level Grubbs outlier. Dropping a major rally is not evidence that an implementable strategy
could avoid it, and a positive median does not repair the negative mean or supply account-level protection.

### EMA switching: a smaller loss, not a demonstrated edge

The **different 35-experiment sample** in Table 8, p. 10, reports:

| Author profile | Mean period return | Reported mean alpha | Alpha p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Always-on V3 | −2.27% | −14.83% | .171 |
| EMA(50/200) adaptive | −0.64% | −13.19% | .217 |

The paired improvement is +1.63 percentage points, t=2.185 / p=.036, d=.369. Our arithmetic check of a standard
two-sided t with 34 degrees of freedom gives p≈.03588, consistent with the quote **under its independence model**.
That checks a supplied statistic, not the underlying sample, serial/cross-asset dependence or strategy execution.
Both average returns remain negative and neither reported alpha is positive/significant. No risk-matched cash,
constant-exposure or equally sparse random-entry baseline is shown to isolate a tradable regime advantage.

There are eight retrospective periods shared across correlated cryptocurrencies, and the study iteratively changes
its rules and tests many variants. Treating 35/80 asset-period pairs as independent can overstate precision.
No cluster/dependence-aware inference, selection-adjusted p-value or DSR is supplied for the adopted switch.
Table 4's claimed walk-forward improvement does not provide exact fold dates, purge/fit/availability records,
frozen code or evidence that V3's design was untouched by the supposedly unseen periods. A computable EMA
is causal only with correct data/fill clocks; it does not establish that the overall research process is unselected.

### Relative bear alpha is not guaranteed positive profit or insurance

The main regimes are defined **afterward** by period-end buy-and-hold return: bull >+10%, bear <−10%, sideways.
Those labels cannot be used to decide whether to trade at the period's start. Conditional alpha in those buckets
does not establish a live crisis hedge. Simply carrying less long exposure can outperform a falling asset.

If Table 3's “alpha” is simple excess return, its bear rows imply the following arithmetic:

| Source period | Buy-and-hold | Reported V3 alpha | Implied V3 return under that definition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear22 | −18.6% | +19.0% | +0.4% |
| Bull24b (classified bear) | −22.3% | +21.7% | −0.6% |
| Bear25 | −43.6% | +31.2% | −12.4% |

These are conditional calculations on published numbers, not independently reconstructed returns. Table 5 also
directly reports negative static-V3 return (−3.55%) alongside positive alpha (+4.56%), so the distinction does not
depend on that calculation. No evidence shows cash arriving before an intraday Apex breach or losses being capped.
No human traders' intervention decisions are evaluated: the EMA test is mechanical. Claims that human macro or
geopolitical judgment would improve it are explicitly untested, not demonstrated human–AI superiority.

### Other source results and numerical boundaries

- Q-learning averages −5.09% versus static V3 −3.55%, p=.201, only six test observations. Failure to show a gain
  is not proof that reinforcement learning cannot work. Source power estimates are author planning claims.
- Incremental sentiment variants also lose and do not significantly improve V3. Fear & Greed already enters
  V3, so this does not establish that independent sentiment information has no value.
- Table 7 fee-study means are **−3.47%, −6.89%, −10.15%, −16.22%** at modeled costs .1/.2/.3/.5% respectively.
  Regressing those quoted means reproduces approximately −3.2 percentage points per .1-point fee increase.
  The source's “viable below about .2%” claim is not established: even its lowest tested fee has negative mean
  return and negative mean alpha. Crypto percent fees, partial positions and turnover do not translate into MNQ costs.
- Table 1 does not exactly reconcile simple same-sample excess returns: −2.76−14.87=−17.63%, versus reported
  alpha −17.52%; V1 similarly implies −19.20%, versus −19.08%. The .11/.12-point differences exceed ordinary
  rounding of three two-decimal means. An alternative alpha definition/weighting might explain them, but no
  raw ledger is provided to reconcile it. This is an unresolved accounting convention, not an allegation of fabrication.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

**No consistently profitable strategy is demonstrated in the reported aggregate/adaptive results.** Relative
performance in falling markets can arise from uninvested cash, lower beta, smaller exposure or fewer entries;
that does not establish short alpha, capital-preserving insurance or a timing edge over comparable exposure.
The composite price/sentiment rules propose reversal/regime timing, but the specific incremental mechanism is
not identified beyond price/volume patterns and retrospective regimes.

The crowding/CAS explanation is a hypothesis. The author acknowledges no direct competing-order-flow evidence;
late entry after a large move is an alternative explanation. A few selected indicator strategies failing to beat
buy-and-hold cannot establish semi-strong market efficiency, and the study does not causally identify algorithmic
adaptation. Nor does it show that discretionary human activation decisions outperform mechanical ones.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**Retain the methodological cautions; no new strategy test is warranted.** The source does not supply positive
absolute/relative net evidence, a fully specified V3 or a distinct ES/NQ market mechanism. Owned BTC minute
exports and an ETH perpetual 30-minute file are not the exact ten-asset Binance hourly/sentiment panel. More
data would not by itself create merit for a new indicator/regime search.

Earlier repository trend/indicator/regime and overnight sentiment filters already carry substantial selection
history (TIER_LIST, including the overnight sentiment study). Those are not exact tests of this crypto system;
neither their negative results nor this paper can be generalized to every regime filter. A new EMA lookback,
trade suppression rule or manual activation protocol would require a distinct justified hypothesis and its own

## 5. Can a strategy code be made?

**A prototype can be coded; a faithful V3 or human–AI policy cannot be reproduced from these details alone.**
The source provides broad V1 buy/sell conditions, 10%-of-balance sizing, 5% stop/10% target, and some V3 settings:
Trend Score 30–55 gets 3x size/15% target, a Danger Zone labelled “55+” blocks new entries, minimum hold eight hours and cooldown
18 hours. It does not fully specify the Trend Score weights/normalization and mode logic, indicator periods/
initialization, long/short/exit priority, warm-up, sentiment publication clock or exact bar-to-fill convention.

The EMA(50/200) condition is mechanically codeable, but “deactivated, default to hold” (p. 7) and “avoiding new
entries” (p. 11) leave cash versus buy-and-hold allocation, existing positions and stop management unclear.
An implementation must freeze that behavior, past-only feature availability, next executable fills, native units,
cash/fee accounting and chronological evaluation. The proposed human risk judgments additionally need observable
as-of decisions and a fixed protocol. Filling in these gaps would be a new strategy, not source replication.
No such strategy or MultiCharts code is built or deployed here; only the quoted-table/statistical audit exists.
