# FX floor versus screen: useful microstructure evidence, no tested trading edge

Summary: Electronic FX futures had tighter estimated spreads in the paper's 2003–04 sample.
Price discovery shifted toward Globex; Yen's March-2004 common-factor weights are near a tie.
These are venue-level statistical findings, not a net-return or executable lead/lag backtest.
Analysis code is feasible with paired data; no financial strategy is fully specified or validated.
Retain execution/data-clock lessons; do not start a proxy strategy test or change the plan.

Source: Aysegul Ates and George H. K. Wang, *Liquidity and the Evolution of Price
Discovery on Floor versus Screen-Based Trading Systems: An Analysis of the Foreign
Exchange Futures Markets*, May 2005, [SSRN754946](https://ssrn.com/abstract=754946).

## 1. What is it about?

How liquidity and price discovery differed between open-outcry floor trading and
Globex for identical CME Japanese Yen, British Pound and Euro futures. It uses
CFTC time-and-sales and Computer Trade Reconstruction data from January 2, 2003
through March 5, 2004, during parallel 07:20–14:00 Central sessions (pp. 11–13).

Prices are placed on a 30-second grid using each interval's last transaction;
empty intervals carry forward the prior print. Nearby contracts roll one week
before expiry, and half-days are excluded. A VEC model is fitted daily, with
Gonzalo–Granger common-factor weights and Hasbrouck information-share bounds.
These are retrospective decompositions of the common efficient-price process.

## 2. What does it find?

Electronic trading has lower estimated spreads, including after controls for
volume/volatility and the British Pound tick-size change. Table 1's all-contract means:

| Market | Floor | Globex |
|---|---:|---:|
| Japanese Yen | $22.01 | $18.22 |
| British Pound | $23.07 | $17.54 |
| Euro FX | $21.74 | $15.94 |

Units are historical estimated US-dollar spreads per contract, not fees, current
quotes or our NQ/MNQ costs. The source estimates spreads from transaction-price
reversals; it lacks actual bid/ask quotes for spread decomposition (pp. 13–16,
endnote 10, Table 1 p. 36).

The reported price-discovery role shifts toward Globex. Examples from common-factor
weights: Euro electronic share rises from 54.27% in March-2003-contract trading to
73.77% in March-2004-contract trading; Yen rises from 28.65% to 50.11%. Yen is thus
essentially tied in the latter example, rather than overwhelmingly screen-led.
The corresponding Pound electronic weight is 57.26% in March 2004 (Tables 3–5).
The authors find associations with relative spreads, activity and market share;
they do not establish a universal low/high-volatility leadership rule or causal
liquidity effect (pp. 23–28). No trading P&L, Sharpe or net-cost strategy result is reported.

**Source-table caveats:** Table 3.C prints Yen mean information-share midpoints
0.4932/0.5860, which do not sum to one; the printed Globex bounds imply midpoint
0.5068. Table 5.B prints Euro mean common-factor weights 0.3019/0.6891, also not
summing to one. Both discrepancies are visible in the PDF, not merely extraction
errors. The arithmetic script preserves them. They may be printing errors; they
do not prove the underlying study is invalid, and I have not silently corrected or
used them as precise quantitative calibration.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

The paper does not demonstrate an investment profit. Its mechanism is that lower
trading costs and operational efficiency attract informed activity, changing which
venue incorporates information first. A venue can contribute more to price discovery
without leaving a profitable, tradeable delay elsewhere.

A historical estimated-spread difference is not an achievable net saving for our
orders. Price discovery is not the percentage of correctly forecast trades, expected
return or probability of success. Same-day liquidity regressions also do not supply
an ex-ante direction or identify a forced signed flow to NQ/ES.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**As methodology and execution context.** It reinforces the need to distinguish
liquidity from volume, quote-based costs from reversal estimators, and descriptive
leadership from an executable forecast. Institution/contract/tick regimes matter;
do not import the 2003–04 floor/screen coefficients into today's electronic markets.

Owned `data/6E_1min_2026.csv` has date/time/OHLCV; matching filenames span 2017–26.
That is not paired floor/Globex transaction history for this sample or synchronized
executable bid/ask data. No raw CFTC panel accompanies the paper. Resampling those
single-market bars cannot reconstruct missing venue prints or quote queues. A
modern mini/micro or spot/futures comparison would be a distinct research hypothesis,
not replication; contract sizes, basis and dissemination clocks must be handled.

Do not use the paper's same-day fitted weights or contract-period 90th/10th
volatility percentiles as historically available entry features. Nor does carrying
forward a stale trade mean its price remained executable. Hasbrouck bounds arise
from correlated innovations and ordering; their midpoint is a summary, not uniquely
identified causal leadership or an estimated execution-latency window (pp. 21–24).

The cheapest justified next step for any separately proposed two-market signal
is a data/clock audit: native contract IDs, timezone/DST and event/receipt timestamps,
quote/trade freshness, executable sizes and costs. Establish available data and an
economic rule before fitting or comparing returns. No data purchase, credential
inspection, logger/VPS change or unsupported source substitution was performed.

## 5. Can a strategy code be made?

**Analysis code: yes. Faithful financial strategy code: not specified by this paper.**
A tool could align timestamped venue prices, fit VEC models and calculate factor
weights/information-share bounds, subject to the data requirements above.

A trading engine additionally needs a pretrade signal, estimation window using
only available past data, entry/exit thresholds, stop/time exit, native integer
quantities and a full latency/fees/spread/fill model. The paper supplies no complete
set of those rules. Inventing them now would introduce a new economic trial.
No trading code was created; the saved script only checks selected source arithmetic.

## Decision

Retain the methodological lesson under execution reference E10; **do not advance
a directional backtest or promising-strategy queue item from this source**. Source
inspection and limited arithmetic are not independent empirical replication, native
execution savings or evidence of a place in the evaluation/funded/personal plans.
