# Deep RL stock ensemble: WATCH as a method; legacy replication fails admission

Summary: the authors report a daily Dow-stock selector with 13.0% annual return, Sharpe 1.30 and -9.7% drawdown in 2016–2020.
We recovered paper-era code and verified the public raw data, rather than treating data as unavailable.
Five predeclared synthetic groups expose a same-close decision/fill clock, negative cash, fractional shares and a fee-counter defect.
The source also computes turbulence from price levels rather than the paper's returns, with further version/specification mismatches.
The original implementation is not admitted to economic replication; no historical agent training, strategy returns or portfolio gains were verified.

Source: **Hongyang Yang, Xiao-Yang Liu, Shan Zhong and Anwar Walid**, *Deep Reinforcement Learning for Automated Stock
Trading: An Ensemble Strategy*, ICAIF 2020, uploaded `ssrn-3690996.pdf`, nine pages, PDF metadata created 2020-11-02.

## 1. What is it about?

A daily, long-only equity-share portfolio chooses among PPO, A2C and DDPG reinforcement-learning agents.
The state has 181 components: cash plus prices, holdings and MACD/RSI/CCI/ADX for 30 stocks.
Actions buy, hold or sell shares subject to cash/holding constraints; rewards are changes in portfolio value.
A turbulence rule liquidates holdings and pauses buying in stressed markets. This is a stock-allocation method,
not an NQ timing rule, and selling owned shares is not evidence of a short strategy.

The "ensemble" selects one agent by the preceding validation window's Sharpe; it does not average three policies.
The paper uses a growing training window, a rolling three-month validation window and the next quarter for trading.
Initial training is 2009-01-01 through 2015-09-30; initial validation is 2015 Q4; reported trading is 2016-01-04 through
2020-05-08. Continuing to retrain on completed earlier quarters can be causal: it is not automatically training on
future test labels. Exact training/model-selection clocks still require verification.

## 2. What does it find?

**Published Table II, not our independently replicated returns:**

| Strategy | Total return | Annual return | Annual volatility | Sharpe | Max drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ensemble selector | 70.4% | 13.0% | 9.7% | 1.30 | -9.7% |
| PPO | 83.0% | 15.0% | 13.6% | 1.10 | -23.7% |
| A2C | 60.0% | 11.4% | 10.4% | 1.12 | -10.2% |
| DDPG | 54.8% | 10.5% | 12.3% | 0.87 | -14.8% |
| Minimum variance | 31.7% | 6.5% | 17.8% | 0.45 | -34.3% |
| DJIA | 38.6% | 7.8% | 20.1% | 0.47 | -37.1% |

Reported initial capital is **$1,000,000**, stock-share units, with **0.1% of value per buy or sell**, not native futures
commissions. PPO earns the most total return; the ensemble has the highest reported Sharpe and smallest drawdown.
Sortino, profit factor, Calmar and Ulcer are not reported in this table and are not invented here.
The paper attributes crash protection to turbulence-triggered exits, but does not supply a clean ablation establishing
the incremental value of learned policies versus the same risk overlay on a simple passive allocation.

These are one reported historical experiment, not confidence intervals, a count of all tuning attempts or independent
evidence of current-book contribution. Three agents do not imply that only three research variants were tried.

## 3. Why might it make money?

The proposed explanation is learned exposure/allocation among liquid equities, with different agents adapting to
different market conditions and the turbulence overlay reducing crash exposure. Market participation, cash timing,
stock weighting and trend/risk features can account for gains; no forced independent participant flow is established.
The source does not isolate how much profit comes from equity exposure, indicators, the turbulence rule, quarterly
selection or RL itself. Better Sharpe than the DJIA does not resolve those attribution questions.

A valid later study would need matched passive/cash-risk baselines, the same turbulence overlay, simpler price-based
rules or a frozen linear policy, and multiple predeclared training seeds under identical executable clocks. We did not
run such a rescue or new parameter search after the admission failures below.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**Retain as a lower-priority personal-account method lead, blocked at reproduction mechanics. Do not adopt the supplied
legacy engine or launch agent training from these results.** Its source merits a concrete implementation check, which
was completed; that check fails the predeclared gate rather than earning a full backtest.

The paper's old GitHub URL now redirects to **AI4Finance-Foundation/FinRL-Trading**, whose current contents are substantially
different. We located the latest commit before the uploaded PDF's creation date, **5b9290ed706efa34aaff96e10761af428a154076**
(2020-10-19), archived the inspected files with the MIT notice, and verified their exact Git blobs. This is a paper-era
snapshot, **not proof it generated Table II**. Its `trained_models` tree contains no trained policy checkpoints.

### Actual data acquisition and no-return census

Downloaded the pinned public stock CSV and SPY/DJIA benchmark CSVs, verifying all three against their Git blob hashes.
The raw stock panel contains **87,780 rows, 30 tickers, 2,926 dates, 2009-01-02–2020-08-17**; each date has 30 rows,
with no duplicate date/ticker keys or missing raw fields. Prices are positive and adjustment factors nonzero.
The data are therefore available for inspection; no WRDS account request was needed for this bounded check.
Runtime market files stay outside the committed research archive; URLs, byte counts, Git blobs and SHA256 receipts
are retained in [data_acquisition.json](../inputs/rl_ensemble_3690996_20261030/data_acquisition.json).

The paper says its pool is Dow constituents as of 2016-01-01. Actual point-in-time membership, ticker/company lineage,
corporate actions and adjustment vintages were not certified. A universe chosen before the 2016 trading period is not
automatically survivor bias for that trading period, although applying a future-selected universe to earlier training
needs disclosure. The raw historical ticker labels alone do not resolve these questions.

### Five synthetic scenario groups: verified mechanics, not a historical backtest

The harness executes original class bodies/methods and the original named turbulence function via AST, with inert
Gym/space/seeding stubs. It neither imports training/broker modules nor installs TensorFlow or executes README commands.
All financial method bodies remain unchanged. Invented prices/actions are not observed failure rates or economic trials.

| Group | Observed result in pinned source | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Fee-inclusive cash bound | $1,000 cash, $10/share and a 100-share buy leaves **-$1 cash**, in training, validation and trading engines. | Affordability is floored using gross price, then fees are added; the stated nonnegative-cash constraint can fail. |
| Integer shares | Normalized action **0.12345** becomes **12.345 shares** after the 100-share scaling, in all three engines. | Integer conversion is commented out; the implementation differs from the paper's integer-share formulation. |
| Full-sale fee counter | Selling 10 shares at $10 credits **$99.90**, but the `cost` counter records **$0**; high-turbulence liquidation has the same issue. | Cash correctly pays the $0.10 fee; the diagnostic fee total undercounts. This is **not** evidence that the cash P&L omits those fees. |
| Decision/fill clock | State includes a $10 close and same-date indicators; an action fills at **$10**, then the engine marks the next $20 close. A next-day $99 open is unused. | No verified executable quote after the observation. Same-close indicators plus same-close fills require an unavailable decision/auction protocol; a causal later fill cannot be silently assumed. |
| Specification consistency | On invented full-rank prices, source turbulence **54.7051** matches a price-level Mahalanobis statistic; the paper's return-based counterpart is **34.8980**. Code uses 63-day validation and a July 7 cutoff; paper uses three months/May 8, while README says 12 months. | Paper/code/data versions do not identify one exact reproduction. The synthetic statistic is not a stock strategy result. |

Additional static findings within group 5: preprocessing backward-fills missing features; this requires a fold/ticker
availability audit, but we did not establish the incidence or return impact of holdout leakage. Validation Sharpe
scales daily mean/std by `sqrt(4)`, not the annual daily factor. A common positive scale does not change the agent
ranking, so that print/scaling defect alone does not explain the paper's reported advantage.

### Gate and account disposition

The causal execution-clock, cash/integer constraints and paper/code differences block economic replication under
the committed stop rule. No historical RL training, replay of saved equity curves, fresh strategy return, parameter
selection, Monte Carlo or BASE/ADD/REPLACE/EMPTY account comparison was run. We did not repair the engine and call the
result an exact reproduction. The paper's reported returns remain unverified, rather than disproven by these fixtures.

The four Apex homes cannot trade the source's daily stock basket as written. Converting it to intraday NQ futures,
altering rewards or compressing the state to a single contract would define a new strategy. Personal-account research
is the only plausible later home, with actual capital, permitted instruments and executable share/cost rules specified;
no measured diversification, survival or payout benefit is claimed.

Smallest later check, only with a clarified source/frozen corrected specification: reconcile a single decision from
timestamped features through a genuinely later executable quote, fee-inclusive cash and integer shares. Then establish
the point-in-time universe, turbulence definition, fold-local transformations and seed/checkpoint protocol before a
new bounded economic preregistration. Passing those steps would still not establish alpha or authorize live changes.

## Evidence boundary and handoff

Source evidence: full uploaded paper and inspectable archived code. Independent evidence: five synthetic admission
groups, exact source/data hash checks and no-return panel census. Full empirical replication and independent
strategy-checker verification remain absent. The source's 2016–2020 results are not a fresh holdout for our research.
No paper/strategy was promoted, no shared trading code or allocation changed, and no VPS/broker action or external
message occurred. The method lead is retained in the verification queue; the execution-clock/cash/schema lesson is
