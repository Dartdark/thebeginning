# SPI 200 algorithmic trading: execution reference, no new directional strategy

Summary: Viljoen, Westerholm and Zheng study SPI 200 futures order/trade messages in January–December 2009.
Algorithmic-activity proxies are associated with lower contemporaneous spreads/adverse selection and later information changes.
The paper reports no executable directional rule, net trading strategy or verified NQ/ES execution saving.
Retain its trade-signed spread/markout decomposition, with the printed sign inconsistency resolved explicitly.
Verdict: execution/microstructure reference; DON'T TEST a new alpha rule from this source alone.

Source: user-uploaded full 40-page PDF, [SSRN 1913693](https://ssrn.com/abstract=1913693),
*Liquidity and Price Discovery of Algorithmic Trading: An Intraday Analysis of the SPI 200 Futures*.
PDF metadata dated August 2012. Review date: 2026-10-30. Economic trials: 0.

## 1. What is it about?

The study examines liquidity and price discovery in Australian SPI 200 equity-index futures during
January 1–December 18, 2009. Reuters Tick History supplies transaction/order prices, millisecond timestamps,
buy/sell order identifiers and book-message information. Only nearest-maturity contracts in the 09:50–16:30
day session are analyzed; the much longer night session is excluded (printed pp. 4–5).

There are no identified algorithm-versus-human trader labels. The proxy is **negative dollar volume per
electronic message**, with trades, quote revisions and cancellations in the first five book levels contributing
to message traffic (pp. 5–6). Greater activity in this proxy means a less negative number, not necessarily more
traded volume, and it is not a measured percentage of orders submitted by algorithms.

## 2. What does it find?

The authors report less inferred algorithmic activity near the opening and closing intervals, and more in the
middle of the session. Their simultaneous-equation/GMM estimates associate the proxy with lower contemporaneous
effective spreads, adverse-selection measures, volatility and PIN, with later increases in some information/cost
measures (pp. 11–19). Their interpretation is strategic participation in lower-cost periods and a contribution
to information incorporation, including liquidity provision when spreads are wider.

These are historical source estimates, not independently reproduced causal effects or signals that predict
the direction of an executable next trade. Lag instruments and structural assumptions do not establish the
identity or profitability of a particular trader. A one-year Australian result does not establish modern CME behavior.

Table 2 reports daily average **half-spread** measures in basis points: effective 1.36, realized 0.23 and
adverse selection 1.13. These are descriptive source numbers, not our commissions, ticks or expected savings.
The paper's relatively low midday *absolute* information variance must not be confused with its higher
midday *proportion* of variance attributed to private information (p. 15).

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

No costed trading strategy, profit series, standalone Sharpe or implementable entry/exit rule is demonstrated.
Liquidity suppliers can receive spread revenue but lose it to informed counterparties, inventory changes and
costs. The paper's realized-spread metric is not a verified net market-making profit or something a small
MultiCharts strategy can collect by changing a market order to a limit order. No queue or fill opportunity is supplied.

## 4. Can we build on it?

Yes, as an **execution diagnostic**, when correctly aligned native quotes and broker fills exist. Separate
the spread component from subsequent quote movement rather than calling all deterioration slippage.
Reuse existing diagnostics and E01/E04 rather than create a new directional signal or live logger from this review.

Let D=+1 for a buyer-initiated trade and -1 for a seller-initiated trade, P the trade price, M0 the valid
prevailing midpoint immediately before the trade and M5 the same-contract midpoint five minutes later.
In proportional half-spread units:

```
effective_half_spread = D * (P - M0) / M0
realized_half_spread  = D * (P - M5) / M0
signed_price_impact  = D * (M5 - M0) / M0
effective_half_spread = realized_half_spread + signed_price_impact
```

Multiply by 10,000 for basis points; full-spread conventions use an additional factor of two.
The price-impact term describes adverse selection to the liquidity supplier. Keep the initiating trade side
separate from our parent-order side, especially for passive fills. M5 is a posttrade outcome, not a feature
available at submission. This decomposition is neither a five-minute directional strategy nor realized account P&L.

**Source inconsistency:** printed Equation 5 on p. 8 omits D. I inspected the rendered PDF page to verify this
is not merely text-extraction loss. Table 2 explicitly describes adverse selection as a signed midpoint change,
and Equations 3–4 imply the signed identity above. Do not copy the unsigned printed equation silently; reconcile
this choice before any reproduction. This does not establish that the authors' unavailable code used the wrong sign.
