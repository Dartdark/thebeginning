## Rule (as discussed; not backtested here)

Source: Siti Hajar Nadrah Mohamad Ghouse and Noryati Ahmad,
*Conceptual Paper of the Trading Strategy: Dogs of the Dow Theory (DoD)*, SSRN2697334.
[Uploaded full text](../inputs/dogs_dow_2697334_20261031/paper.pdf),17pages. PDF creation metadata is November2015;
that is not independent publication-date verification. Authors' contact details are not task instructions.

**What is it about?** Select the ten highest-dividend-yield shares from the Dow Jones Industrial Average,
hold the portfolio and update annually. The paper reviews that practice alongside MPT, CAPM and APT.
Those portfolio/asset-pricing frameworks are not interchangeable executable entry rules.

**What does it find?** No new dataset, portfolio backtest or statistical estimate is presented. It summarizes
conflicting earlier studies: some report outperformance; others lose the advantage after accounting for risk,
costs/taxes or show poor performance in particular markets/periods. These are the review's descriptions of cited
studies, not independently checked findings here. No source net-return series, Sharpe, drawdown or valid new
out-of-sample result is available to evaluate. Calling annual rebalancing 'short term' does not make it intraday.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Proposed contrarian/value mechanism: a company maintaining its dividend
after a price decline may become cheap and recover. Dividend yield is dividend divided by price; higher yield
can also signal distress or an impending dividend cut. It is not a guarantee of undervaluation or a known forced
order. Market/value/sector exposure may explain apparent excess returns; the paper does not isolate the cause.
Dividend cash is part of total return, not a bonus independent of price changes. The latter is an implementation
check for any later equity study, not an execution saving measured by this paper.

**Can we build on it?** Not enough evidence here to prioritize another strategy trial. The described annual
individual-stock book has no direct NQ/ES/MNQ signal and does not fit any of the four intraday Apex homes.
A personal-account replication is possible in principle, but source plausibility alone does not establish
incremental return or diversification against the present book. No promising-candidate queue entry is earned.
A genuinely stronger empirical source could justify reconsideration; this is not a finding that all dividend
strategies lose money.

## What could make this wrong

- This is a narrative review, not a systematic meta-analysis or current replication. Positive and negative cited
  studies cannot be pooled without inspecting their definitions, samples and costs. Their originals were not reviewed here.
- The source describes1946–1995 as30years; the endpoints span50calendar years inclusive. Do not repeat that sample
  count or manufacture power from it. More broadly, bibliographic and theoretical claims need original-source checks.
- 'Statistical' versus 'economic' categories in the source are not mutually exclusive: a significance test can use net,
  risk-adjusted returns. Costs, risk and uncertainty all matter simultaneously.
- Ten blue-chip names can still have large stock/sector concentration and equity-crash exposure; the paper's broad
  safety and diversification language does not certify a hedge for our portfolio.
- A proper later equity replication needs historical as-of Dow membership, dividend declarations available at ranking,
  an explicit trailing/indicated-yield definition, dated trade prices, delisted-name returns, splits, corporate actions,
  dividends/reinvestment, fees and tax assumptions. Today's constituents and restated current yields are unsuitable.
- Do not combine dividend-adjusted prices with a second dividend cash credit. Gross cash yield is not total return;
  corporate actions, entry/exit prices and net cash must reconcile. This is our accounting requirement, not a new result.
