# One Ticker Deep: useful attribution lesson; no new strategy admission

Summary: A stock-trend rule's author-reported walk-forward success depends heavily on NVIDIA.
On the 99-name universe, removing NVDA changes Sharpe 1.53→1.05 versus SPY1.30; CAGR 41.3%→23.2% versus 20.8%.
Useful lesson: chronological validation still needs concentration, benchmark and exposure attribution.
The residual drawdown claim is historical and qualified by warm-up cash, missing delistings and counterexamples.
Retain as methodology; no new return trial, intraday adaptation or current-book change.

## Five questions

1. **What is it about?** A daily US-stock long/cash trend selector and why passing walk-forward validation can
   still conceal dependence on a particular stock or cohort. It studies2020–2024 using26 stocks selected in 2026
   and a cleaner, approximate99-name January2020 S&P100 universe. The latter still omits unavailable delistings.
2. **What does it find?** Author-reported stitched OOS Sharpe is 1.36/1.53 for the 26/99-name portfolios,
   compared with SPY1.30. Re-running selection without NVIDIA reduces these to 1.13/1.05. CAGR edges fall from
   13.3/20.5 percentage points to 2.2/2.4. Historical drawdowns remain shallower in these four walk-forward runs,
   but broad claims of guaranteed or universal risk reduction are unsupported. None is our reproduced performance.
3. **Why does it make money, if it does?** The rule concentrates in recent positive trends; this sample supplies
   a very large winner in NVDA, high-volatility cohorts and market beta. Cash reduces exposure when few names qualify.
   That is an exposure description, not an identified forced-flow premium or proof that the supply of such winners
   recurs. Conversely, reliance on a rare winner alone does not prove a genuine trend mechanism cannot work.
4. **Can we build on it?** Yes: distinguish original-holdings P&L attribution from an exclusion/reselection
   counterfactual, test concentration sensitivity and compare on matched warm-up dates and feasible exposure.
   This is a reference for the next independently justified portfolio study, not an instruction to re-optimize our
   book or exclude NVDA. No compelling incremental stock strategy or distinct intraday futures mechanism clears
   the evidence gate here. No new acquisition/return test is warranted by this source's own fragile result.
5. **Can a strategy code be made?** Yes, the signal and target weights are codeable; AppendixA provides them.
   Every five trading days rank positive scores P/SMA(n)−1, hold the strongest k at target1/k each, leave unfilled
   slots in cash and carry targets between rebalances. Default n100,k5; source selection searches36 combinations.
   An exact executable reproduction still needs the missing engine, complete universes/data, walk-forward boundary
   rules, execution timing, adjusted-price/corporate-action treatment and costs. This is a personal-stock concept;
   forcing daily flat NQ/MNQ would create a different hypothesis. No new trading code was warranted or run.

## Key results — source claims, not independently reproduced

Stitched OOS spans roughly mid2022 through December2024,627–629 days depending on scenario. Source parameters
are selected on trailing 378-day windows and tested in 126-day blocks. Cash/risk-free return is assumed zero;
cost is 5bp per unit gross turnover, without a native fill/impact model. Values below are source-rounded.

| Portfolio | Sharpe | CAGR | Maximum drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
|26 names, with NVDA|1.36|34.1%|−18.6%|
|26 names, exclusion and full reselection|1.13|23.0%|−19.2%|
|99 names, with NVDA|1.53|41.3%|−13.4%|
|99 names, exclusion and full reselection|1.05|23.2%|−16.7%|
|SPY, reported corresponding OOS span|1.30|20.8%|approximately−24%|

Table 3's26-name folds range from Sharpe−0.69 to 3.26, with two of five negative. The author acknowledges that
1.36 versus 1.30 is statistically indistinguishable over this short span. No paired uncertainty interval supplied
here establishes that the 99-name Sharpe improvement is statistically reliable. Exact common dates still need
reconciliation; the source labels its scenarios627–629 days, so not all stitched observations are identical.

## What the attribution establishes — and what it does not

- Removing the largest ex-post winner is a sensitivity diagnostic. The author re-runs parameter selection as
  well as holdings; the CAGR difference is therefore **not** NVDA's realized P&L contribution in the original book.
  Different selected windows, replacement stocks, exposures and turnover all contribute. A frozen-position
  contribution ledger and separately labeled reselection experiment answer different questions.
- Full-sample volatility terciles use future information and are correctly described as diagnostic. They are
  not a valid forward high-vol filter. Even the “lower two terciles underperform” slogan needs a metric qualifier:
  the original-universe medium bucket has Sharpe 0.84 versus SPY0.76, although CAGR 14.3% versus 14.4%.
  The broadly stronger high-vol cohort does not by itself identify causal alpha or prove repeatability.
- SPY alone does not isolate timing from universe/sector tilts. Relevant future controls would include matched
  eligible-universe buy-and-hold and feasible exposure/risk matching, with precommitted design/trial accounting.
  Those are missing controls, not new tests authorized by this source review.

## The drawdown claim needs limits

The four displayed walk-forward runs support a narrower empirical observation: shallower drawdowns **in their
reported sample**. They do not establish a reliable future hedge, a guaranteed drawdown cap or protection before
an Apex account breach.

1. **Warm-up cash:** AppendixA schedules the first target at `prices.index[window]` and fills prior targets with
   zero. With data starting January2020, the baseline's100-day warm-up puts it in cash during the COVID crash.
   §4.1 acknowledges this. SPY is invested throughout. The audit derives the initial zero-target rows from the
   code; it does not calculate how much this affected returns or claim that all OOS protection is artificial.
   Use pre-sample warm-up/common eligibility dates to isolate protection from start-date luck. The missing engine
   is needed to verify fold-local warm-up, state carry and scoring masks too.
2. **Source counterexamples:** Table 2's20d/top 3 grid cell has−35.7% drawdown versus SPY−33.8%; Table 4/Figure 5
   broad high-vol bucket has−38.6%. Thus the introduction's “−13% to −19% across every specification” cannot describe
   all tests. Those magnitudes pertain to a subset of walk-forward results. Baseline drawdown is−23.2%.
3. **Survivorship and cash:** The broader list remains an approximate reconstruction and omits mid-sample M&A
   delistings. AppendixA provides only an abridged list, not all99 tickers. Cash yield, benchmark cash comparison,
   gaps, execution friction and the dependence of target-weight turnover on drift need exact reconciliation.
   The emitted target matrix alone does not establish a self-financing portfolio holding fixed shares between
   rebalances; the companion accounting engine would settle that convention.
4. **Close timing:** A one-row weight shift avoids multiplying a same-row return by its own close-derived signal,
   but does not alone demonstrate a feasible same-close fill. Specify when the close is known and the next
   executable trade, then retain the intervening exposure. Do not silently treat adjusted closes as executable prices.

## Disposition

**METHOD REFERENCE / DON'T TEST as a new strategy.** Source statistical success is concentration-sensitive,
the author does not recommend believing the headline alpha, and the residual risk claim lacks a fully executable
causal reproduction. No promising-strategy TODO entry or new data request is justified. This does not reject
all trend following or modify existing portfolio/risk rules. The prior managed-futures overview is a separate
[trend-family review](ssrn5140633_review_20261108.md), not a replication of this stock selector.
