## 1. What is it about?

A trade-entry execution overlay on a proprietary Nifty 50 futures trend-continuation strategy. Rather than
enter immediately, place a limit 0.25% below the original long entry or above the original short entry, and
cancel an unfilled order. The historical study keeps a trade when its recorded maximum adverse excursion
(MAE) reaches the offset, moves its entry to that limit, and retains the original realized exit (pp. 5–6).
This is a price-offset resting-order policy, **not** the fast-alpha paper's opposite-five-minute-return policy.
There is no separate rebound/continuation confirmation test in the stated rule.

## 2. What does it find?

The author reports 3,908 closed hourly-resolution Nifty-futures positions, April 13, 2010–January 30, 2026:

| Author result | Immediate baseline | Selected 0.25% pullback |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trades | 3,908 | 2,448 |
| Retained signals | 100% | 62.64% (rounded to 63%) |
| CAGR | 12.3% | 31.9% |
| Annualized Sharpe | 1.38 | 3.33 |
| Sortino | 2.59 | 10.11 |
| Maximum drawdown | −11.6% | −10.8% |

Figures are **author claims**, not reproduced market results. The baseline also reports 52.4% win rate,
profit factor 1.27 and Calmar 1.06. The assumed cost is **6.02 bp per completed round trip**, comprising
India-specific statutory/exchange charges and a two-index-point slippage allowance (p. 7). This is not an
ES/NQ commission schedule, dated Indian fee reconstruction or measurement of this limit order's fill quality.

Table 1 shows eight nonzero thresholds from 0.05% to 0.50%, plus baseline. Side-specific threshold results and
interpolated 0.35% values are additional author selection views. The Sharpe gain is approximately 141%, but
both threshold choice and its favorable neighborhood use the same historical population. A plateau reduces
one concern about an isolated optimum; it does not supply a chronological untouched validation set or correct
selection across thresholds/sides. No such split, multiple-testing correction or matched random-drop control
is supplied. Underlying signal/exit rules are expressly withheld; raw trade/MAE data and replication code are
not attached, so the original strategy and published metrics cannot be independently reconstructed here.

The author permutes the selected trade order 10,000 times and places realized drawdown at the 87th percentile
(p. 9). This describes sequencing risk **conditional on the chosen trades and assumed returns**. It does not
validate limit fills, the selected threshold, the return sample, calendar marks or future performance.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

One component is mechanical: a filled long enters lower or a filled short enters higher while the old exit
price remains fixed. That improves the retained trade's gross price difference by construction. The author
also proposes stronger conviction after pullbacks and implicit removal of weaker signals (pp. 9–10), but
explicitly does not separate those explanations. A falling-price buy limit alone does not observe conviction,
order flow or a subsequent rebound. Missed immediate winners and fills during deteriorating signals can offset
the price concession. Total net dollars across the original opportunity/calendar set matter, not only average
profit or Sharpe after removing nonfills.

The paper's p. 10 statement that the 37% foregone trades are profitable “by construction” is false. A trade can
lose less than the offset, or make a small gross profit that costs consume. Our invented long with entry 100,
minimum 99.80 and exit 99.90 has MAE 0.20%: the 99.75 limit never fills, yet the original trade loses 0.10%
gross and **0.1602% after the paper's fee assumption**. This counterexample challenges the inference, not the
undisclosed actual sample's profitability. The source could establish the skipped set's profitability only by
publishing its realized returns and uncertainty, not from the MAE condition alone.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**Retain it as a qualified execution diagnostic; no new strategy sweep is warranted from this source.**

MAE used ex post to assess a genuinely resting order is **not automatically look-ahead bias**. With a causally
known order level, a fixed virtual-parent cancellation rule, continuous observable prices and an ideal fill
model, an eventual crossing can identify hypothetical eligibility. However, the original trade's entry, exit
and one worst excursion do not identify first passage, queue fill, order lifetime eligibility, partial fills,
post-entry stops/targets, interim marks or account survival. Table 1's `MAE >= threshold` also accepts a touch,
which is weaker than the text's trade-through assumption. Hourly granularity cannot certify price continuity.
Gaps require actual order/quote chronology; they are not uniformly unfavorable or favorable to resting limits.

There is also an order-lifetime mismatch: historical eligibility runs until each original realized exit, whereas
the implementation paragraph uses the strategy's “typical holding horizon.” Those can produce different trades.
A fixed virtual parent can produce a causal cancellation event if its complete rules are known and kept running;
one cannot silently infer that event or continue using old exits after changing a fill-dependent live parent.

Our seven synthetic checks establish only these mechanical limits:

| Invented check | Result / implication |
|---|---|
| Skipped gross loser | MAE below the offset does not imply profitability |
| Skipped small gross winner | Fees can make it a net loser |
| Identical entry/exit/MAE/MFE, different paths | First fill occurs at step 1 versus step 3; a step-2 expiry fills only one |
| Threshold touch | MAE eligibility need not meet a stricter trade-through fill rule |
| Revised percentage denominator | Long and short return adjustments differ; simply adding the offset is not exact |
| Fixed-return permutations | Under fixed-fraction compounding terminal wealth is unchanged; sequencing is not selection validation |
| Two-point cost units | At illustrative index levels 5,000/25,000, two points is 4/0.8 bp, not a constant bp charge |

These are invented unit examples, **not seven economic trials, historical backtests or measured error frequencies**.
No simulation of the author's Nifty strategy was performed.

## 5. Can a strategy code be made?

**Yes for a separately specified execution wrapper; no for reproducing this proprietary signal from the paper.**
For a known parent, code can run it virtually, place a native tick-rounded limit at a predeclared adverse offset,
start only after the signal is available, enforce exact cancellation/expiry, replay first passage/trade-through,
preserve targets and emergency exits, and record all nonfills/unknowns with fees, marks and integer exposure.
Whether brackets remain at original levels or re-anchor to the child fill is a material policy choice to freeze.
That is a research tool, not a verified new directional alpha or permission to sweep offsets across every book.
The source recommends re-estimating thresholds; that recommendation is not an instruction overriding our gates.
