# Trading Strategy for Bear Markets: personal-account research lead; needs the full commodity panel

Summary: Vojtko and Padyšák give a concrete monthly cross-sectional commodity-skewness strategy.
Their reported annual return is 8.43% gross / 6.67% after modeled costs; equity correlation is -0.32.
The costed maximum drawdown is 17.46%, with a 44-month drawdown/recovery period.
This is a possible personal-account diversifier, not a demonstrated hedge for our actual book or an Apex intraday rule.
Verdict: NEEDS DATA / replication details; retain the lead, do not invent a reduced-universe backtest.

Source: user-uploaded full 10-page PDF, [SSRN 3507947](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3507947), December 2019 file metadata.
Review date: 2026-10-30. Economic trials: 0. All source performance numbers below are author claims, not reproduced results.

## Rule and rationale

At each month end, compute each commodity's skewness from the preceding 12 months of daily returns.
Rank the full 22-commodity universe; buy the four lowest-skew commodities and short the four highest-skew.
Rebalance monthly with equal commodity weights (PDF p. 4). The stated universe is soybean oil, corn, cocoa,
cotton, feeder cattle, gold, copper, heating oil, coffee, live cattle, lean hogs, natural gas, oats,
orange juice, palladium, platinum, soybeans, sugar, silver, soybean meal, wheat and crude oil.

The proposed mechanism is investor preference for lottery-like positive skew: investors may overpay for a small
chance of a very large gain, depressing subsequent expected returns. The strategy takes the other side.
This is a behavioral risk-premium hypothesis, not an observed compulsory trading deadline.
It is a cross-sectional ranking, not a claim that low NQ skew predicts a profitable next-day NQ long.

It is a conditional historical summary, not a rule to turn the strategy on using a future month's S&P return.

Negative average correlation does not guarantee a gain in every crash, and eight positions do not establish neutral risk.

## Replication and execution limits

- The stated test period is April 30, 1992–January 31, 2019, while charts and wealth narration start in 1991.
  Resolve the actual formation/warm-up and return dates before attempting numerical replication.
- The PDF does not adequately identify the price-data vendor, continuous-series adjustment, exact daily return
  construction or month-end decision/next-fill convention. These choices matter for measured skew and executable returns.
- The cost model assumes no leverage, zero interest on collateral-free cash, $2 per transaction, 0.05% modeled
  slippage and a 0.85% management fee (p. 7). These are assumptions, not verified costs at the trader's size.
- It assumes an average $50,000 contract, calls $1 million a minimum modeled portfolio and $5 million optimal for
  tracking weights. These are the authors' implementation assumptions, not universal minimums or proof that micros
  can reproduce all eight exposures in a small personal account. Native multipliers and integer quantities need testing.
- The text says to hold the most liquid contract and roll on the first day of its delivery month. These conventions
  need explicit reconciliation. Delivery/first-notice and broker deadlines vary by contract and can occur earlier;
  do not copy this roll rule directly into live trading.
- The 1,000 resampled equity paths produce a reported 10th-percentile scenario of 4.03% annual return and 19.83%
  drawdown (p. 8). A percentile path is not a worst-case or maximum-loss bound; the paper does not establish that
  resampling preserves serial dependence and crisis clustering.

## Fit and next justified check

**Apex evaluations and funded accounts:** no placement as written; month-long positions require overnight holdings.
Closing daily would define a different strategy whose returns are not established by the paper.

**Personal account:** potentially useful because it proposes a broad commodity spread rather than another NQ exposure.
But we do not have an identified complete panel for all 22 named markets. Testing only our available CL/GC or adding
NQ/ES would change the cross-sectional ranks and the strategy. No reduced-universe rescue or returns were run.

The next justified work is a source/data feasibility check: bind the original 22-market price/return and roll
definitions, native contract identifiers, available dates and next-executable formation prices. Obtain the underlying
Fernandez-Perez et al. reference (SSRN 2671165) if implementation detail is needed. Before a return study, commit a
frozen replication plan with actual costs, integer sizing, a separate post-2019 evaluation and ordinary commodity
momentum/carry controls. Then evaluate addition/replacement/empty-slot contribution to the actual personal book.
That would be a new study; this review establishes no Sharpe, Monte Carlo outcome, trading grade or allocation.

The earlier COT paper (SSRN 2311859) remains retained as execution reference E44 and in its separate review.
