## 42. Predictable End-of-Month Treasury Returns
**SSRN [3440417](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3440417), Hartley and Schwarz.** [Author full paper](https://kristaschwarz.com/EOM.pdf), dated November 22, 2019 internally; author website labels 2022.

Source summary: Long Treasury exposure from the close t trading days before the final trading day, exit month-end close. Table 8's 1990–2018 futures results for t = 3 average .12%, .17%, .27% for 5-, 10-, 30-year contracts, with standard errors .02%, .02%, .03%. These are monthly-window returns, not annualized or account returns. The mechanism is benchmark additions and insurer demand; cash results survive auction controls. Cash transaction-cost discussion cites 2–3 bp off-the-run spreads, not a futures execution simulation. No independent held-out/postpublication futures test identified. Futures close timestamp/roll convention are incomplete; Table 4's 2015–2019 heading conflicts with the stated 2018 sample end.

**Build:** Known exchange trading-day calendar plus validated contract prices can define a frozen month-end hypothesis. Specify entry as E−t close, not vaguely “last three dates.” Do not choose t after looking at local returns. Exact original replication needs its contract selection and closing-price definition; avoid importing cash Sharpe ≈ 1 into futures.

**Fit:** Personal only, multiple overnight holds. Intraday truncation would be a new hypothesis. Lower data complexity than curve strategies, but owned Treasury history, roll treatment and execution endpoints are not verified here. Default-branch ID/title/author searches found no exact paper entry; codex/work absence is not established. Overlaps the broad calendar/MOM–TOM family, though the insurer-demand mechanism and bond-specific evidence differ. Priority: source/data clarification, not immediate coding.

## 43. Mean Reversion Trading on the Naphtha Crack
**SSRN [5034739](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5034739) / [5032815](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5032815)** are same-title 2024 records, counted once. [Author full paper](https://scaillet.ch/pdfs/naphtha.pdf), [Energy Economics 2025 publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2025.108620).

Source summary: ICE naphtha-versus-Brent daily settlements, May 2014–February 2024. Fade large volatility-standardized daily moves, typically holding four days, with $1.50/bbl stop and $3 target. §§ 3.2–3.3, 5.1–5.3/Table 2 charge $.10/bbl each way plus $.20 stop slippage. Seven selected variants average $.62/bbl net per trade, but only 1.1–3.6 trades/year. Validation uses five-fold 80/20 rather than chronological forward holdout; 24 variants are further selected using validation performance. Naphtha's stale/illiquid 19:30 settlement relative to 16:30 active trading is central to the explanation. § 3.1 describes retail absence and broker/clearing barriers.

**Build:** Not currently practical. Signal/fill causality is decisive when settlement staleness generates the apparent opportunity: a synthetic closing crack value is not proof of simultaneously executable legs. Need licensed native quotes, leg units, execution timestamps, OTC/clearing access and chronological selection-free evidence. No purchase or acquisition proposed.

**Fit:** Personal overnight only; no Apex/intraday or current-data qualification. Economic relative-value mechanism could diversify equity trend exposure in theory, but realized portfolio benefit is unproven and sparse events cannot carry a portfolio. No exact ID/title matches in repository search or inspected codex/work MINED_INDEX/TIER_LIST/TODO/WATCHLIST. Retain as mechanism reference, not ahead of accessible candidates.
