## Five questions

1. **What is it about?** Choosing how to combine many climate measurements into one macroeconomically relevant index. The Assemblage VAR learns nonnegative weights summing to one and estimates vector-autoregressive dynamics together. Component-space weights attach to named inputs; rank-space weights attach to the lowest/highest/etc. contemporaneous values, so the names receiving weight change through time.
2. **What does it find?** Authors report that richer regional/NOAA component panels imply peak industrial-production contractions of about0.24%–0.32%, versus0.16% with the transformed equal-weight benchmark. National reweighting changes the response much less; rank-space improvement is strongest for NOAA. Regional ACI weights allocate about40% to wind extremes (national about65%); NOAA rank weights emphasize distribution tails. These are estimated macro impulse responses, not our reproduced returns or forecasts. The paper uses monthly1975–April2025 data, excludes January2020–June2021, and displays pointwise68% bands. Different specifications identify innovations to different indices, not one common physical shock.
3. **Why does it make money, if it does?** It demonstrates no trading profit. Proposed economic channels are storm/heat/flood damage to production, infrastructure, labour and housing. The method selects measurements that better fit a macro system, including the climate index's own predictability. It does not establish unexpected tradable news, later NQ/ES price direction, excess returns after costs, or risk reduction before an account breach. A contraction estimated months after a climate innovation is not an intraday short signal.
4. **Can we build on it?** Yes, as a research tool: define the economic target before choosing how to aggregate inputs, compare learned weights against equal weights, shrink unstable weights, distinguish named-input weights from tail weights, and propagate estimation uncertainty. For our project, a future independently justified macro/flow-feature study could use those comparisons. However, this paper supplies no reason to reopen failed indicator/flow combinations or optimize current strategy weights. Smallest justified next check was the source/data and normalization audit completed here; a trading experiment is not earned.
5. **Can a strategy code be made?** **The index/VAR research code exists in R and can be implemented in Python. A complete trading strategy cannot be reproduced from the paper because none is specified.** A directional system would additionally need a justified market/horizon/side rule, original release vintages and available-at times, rolling training and tuning, executable fills, stops/exits, native integer sizes and costs. Its technical feasibility would not establish profitability. No MultiCharts strategy written.

## Method in plain language

For each candidate weight vector, construct the climate index, fit a VAR containing it and the macro variables, and score the improvement over a mean-only model **for that same candidate index**. The printed score is half the sample size times the log ratio of the benchmark and VAR residual covariance determinants. Comparing each candidate with its own mean-only representation avoids rewarding a mere rescaling that lowers the index's variance. Equal weights are the shrinkage/reference comparator, not the second likelihood in that score (§2.1–2.3,pp.4–8).

Component-space uses shrinkage toward equal weights. Rank-space sorts each row then smooths adjacent rank weights. This is supervised measurement, not a portfolio of futures: weights on indicators are not positions or dollar risk weights. The VAR is linear in the resulting aggregate; nonlinear sorting of inputs does not establish nonlinear propagation or damage causality.

## How it could help our research

- **Target first:** determine whether we need activity forecasts, risk estimates or executable returns; a score optimized for one is not evidence about another.
- **Transparent comparator:** start with equal weights and a simple regularized target model. A learned index must add value on a shared chronological sample; adding another optimizer is not itself merit.
- **Causal construction:** fit scaling, transformations, rank-mean constraints and weights only using information available at decision time. Preserve calendar holes and original releases.
- **Uncertainty throughout:** re-estimate weights inside appropriate resampling when justified, distinguish fixed-weight from estimated-weight results, and acknowledge tuning uncertainty. Do not infer account safety from a narrow macro confidence band.
