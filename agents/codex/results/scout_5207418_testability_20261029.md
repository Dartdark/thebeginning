## 1. What is it about?

Whether U.S. Treasury auction timing conditions foreign-exchange returns around the following U.S. macroeconomic announcement. This is a specific scheduled-event interaction, with potential FX transmission, rather than a generic invitation to search calendar patterns.

## 2. What does it find?

The indexed April 2025 abstract reports approximately 8bp foreign-currency appreciation and pre-announcement drift when news follows auctions. The current SSRN metadata identifies a **March 20, 2026, 40-page revision** whose abstract specifies releases on the **day after** an auction and near-zero returns on other announcement days. These are source claims. Full tables, precise clocks, sample construction, standard errors, currency weights, cost treatment and a trading ledger were not obtained or independently verified. The April and March versions must not be silently combined into one specification.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

The authors propose that Treasury demand and primary-dealer risk-bearing capacity transmit issuance effects into FX. The obtained material does not establish that an accessible futures trader captures a net profit. Calendar information could be known before entry; announcement surprises, auction outcomes and interdealer trading statistics each have their own later availability clocks. No realized price-spike event label can substitute for a pre-release calendar.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**The lead warrants follow-up, but remains NEEDS DATA.** We have not recovered a source-based executable rule, so no exact entry/exit is proposed for preregistration. A 6E-only long has the sign of euro appreciation, but the paper's currency composition and native instrument remain unverified. A euro-futures proxy must be explicitly labeled and justified after the source is available; it cannot inherit a pooled FX return, transaction costs or significance.
