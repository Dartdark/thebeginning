## Four questions

1. **What is it about?** A long-only Bank Nifty reversal heuristic: after three consecutive red daily candles,
   buy near the third day's close and sell the following day. Longer red sequences permit rolling signals;
   dojis may count, without a numerical definition. Authors discuss current-month futures or an ITM call,
   choosing next-week options around Thursday expiry.
2. **What does it find?** Authors claim roughly 60% accuracy over five fiscal-year samples 2016–2021 and
   ₹358,346 gross on one assumed 20-unit lot. Our source-table audit confirms 136 rows,84 positive rows and
   ₹358,346.40 from multiplying printed index point changes by 20. It does not verify prices against market
   data, identify every eligible signal, or reproduce derivative fills. No independent net profitability result.
3. **Why could it make money?** A proposed rebound after sustained selling. The paper does not identify a
   forced buyer, measured liquidity premium or causal reason the fourth candle should reverse. Long-only equity
   exposure and sample selection may contribute. A green candle is not equivalent to a profitable overnight trade.
4. **Can we build on it?** Not as a new portfolio candidate from this evidence. Entries need an executable clock,
   doji rules, reentry/exposure policy, actual contract/option selection and fully specified exits. More importantly,
   Excel forecasts and the reported statistical tests do not establish a transferable edge. Deeper strategy
   investigation is not warranted now; no fresh NQ/ES adaptation or data-acquisition project is justified.

## Source rule versus reported accounting

The text recommends entering at least five minutes before the close, after observing the third red candle
(PDF 9), yet the table values entries at the final index close (PDF 11). That final candle label and final close
are not both known five minutes beforehand. An executable earlier observation rule changes the definition.

PDF 10 says close immediately after a next-day gap down, place a stop before entry, and book a profit target
before an initially green day turns red. Stop distance, target level, intraday crossing order and precise exit
time are not supplied. Table 1 instead uses index close minus previous close for every row, multiplied by 20;
our arithmetic audit verifies that identity throughout. Such close marks cannot establish that the stated
gap-down, stop or target policy actually occurred. No intraday path or contract-level execution ledger is provided.

The paper conflates index-chart outcomes with futures and ITM-call implementation. Futures basis, dated rolls,
native multipliers/lot-size history, fees and slippage need separate reconciliation. An option needs strike,
expiry, premium, implied volatility/time decay and executable prices; multiplying index points is not its P&L.
There is no stated capital/margin denominator supporting CAGR, Sharpe or portfolio sizing. No cost-adjusted
ledger, profit factor, independent maximum drawdown or funded payout comparison is supplied.

## Forecast and statistical audit

Table 2's historical counts are 27,24,26,29,30 trades and 16,15,17,17,19 wins. Its 2021–2025 rows are generated
forecasts, not observed future trades. The abstract calls the method linear regression, while PDF 15 explicitly
describes Excel FORECAST.ETS/AAA exponential smoothing. Neither supplies independent realized validation.

Applying one-way ANOVA to the printed groups reproduces count p-values approximately 0.00151,0.00233 and 0.00523,
and accuracy p≈0.53978. Small differences from printed F values reflect rounded summary inputs. The calculation
compares group means; it does not test correlation or prove a forecast works. Generated forecasts derived from
the same historical sample are not independent observations. Failure to reject an accuracy mean difference is
not evidence that forecast accuracy equals realized accuracy. There is no untouched return validation.

The gross rupee total and win fraction are source arithmetic only. No commission/slippage was deducted, no new
historical return definition was inspected, and none of these numbers is a current income estimate.
