# Leveraged Fibonacci strategy: insufficient evidence and incomplete coding rules

Summary: The paper reports +123.5% on DJI30 and +141,280% on ISE100 using leverage.
These are author claims, not independently reproduced results or evidence of Fibonacci alpha.
Essential pivots, regime labels, ellipse placement and exits are not defined algorithmically.
Code is possible only after supplying missing rules; it would otherwise be our own variant.
Do not advance to a backtest, funded bench or trading plan from this source.

## 1. What is it about?

A daily-chart system combining Dow-style higher peaks/troughs, Fibonacci retracements
and fans, phi ellipses, and leveraged pyramiding. It proposes long trend trades,
countertrend retracement buys, and shorts during perceived trend termination.
It starts longs using 10% of equity as margin, adds as price rises and permits up
to 60% margin commitment. Shorts start with 15%, then another 15% after confirmation
(pp. 10–18). These are margin allocations, not percentages of equity risked at a stop.

## 2. What does it find?

| Author-reported result | Start | Finish | Implied total return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DJI30 | $100,000 | $223,500 | +123.5% |
| ISE100 | 100,000 YTL | 141,380,000 YTL | +141,280% |

The arithmetic checks, but the trading results do not have a supplied trade-by-trade
reconciliation. Do not interpret the unusually large ISE result as verified profitability.
The source describes 1988–2005 observations used to develop its approach, tests
2000–2005, then reports beginning-2000/end-2004 balances and calls these four-year
returns (pp. 2, 4, 20). The periods overlap design observations and are inconsistent;
no clean held-out test is established, and no CAGR is inferred here.

It assumes 10x margin leverage, 0.2% transaction costs per contract and sufficient
liquidity at daily closing prices (pp. 1, 20). No completed-trade ledger, risk-matched
leveraged benchmark, independent parameter-selection period, maximum drawdown,
Sharpe or significance test is supplied. Its best-possible unleveraged buy/hold
comparison does not isolate skill at the strategy's leveraged risk exposure.

The conclusion suggests using 60-minute and 5-minute charts as **future research**;
the paper does not test an intraday version (p. 22).

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

The proposed explanation is catching sustained trends, adding to winners and
exiting failures, with countertrend Fibonacci entries and panic-sale shorts.
The paper does not establish that Fibonacci levels/ellipses add value beyond
ordinary trend following, reversal, discretionary chart selection or leverage.
Its psychology discussion identifies no observable forced-flow quantity or timing.
Leverage magnifies outcomes; it does not create positive expected returns.

The source acknowledges sideways-market failures (p. 21). Multi-month trend
classification and illustrated ellipses create an unresolved hindsight risk:
we need to know exactly what anchors and confirmations existed at each decision,
not simply whether an ellipse fits a completed chart.

## 4. Can we build on it?

No compelling next return test is earned. Missing rules prevent faithful replication,
and the source supplies weak evidence for spending a new price-pattern trial.
Our existing process requires testing costs and independently observable rules;
replacing phi ellipses with a chosen ZigZag or rolling breakout would be a new system.

The daily, multi-day design is incompatible with Apex's daily flatten constraint.
Its potential personal-account use still needs deterministic rules, native instrument
accounting, integer sizing and independent validation. Porting the idea to intraday
NQ or YM without those prerequisites is not a replication of DJI30/ISE100 results.

The no-stop countertrend branch illustrates a useful risk-accounting warning. It
allocates 20% equity to margin plus 30% reserve and describes a 35% maximum loss
under its maintenance-margin arithmetic (pp. 14–15). Reserves and maintenance levels
do not guarantee a fill or cap losses during gaps/liquidation. Likewise, under a
simple fixed-exposure illustration, 60% margin at 10x implies 6x equity notional;
a 7% adverse move is a 42% equity loss before costs. This is conditional arithmetic,
not the realized risk of the paper's changing positions or hedged variants.

Protective puts are suggested when technical stops are too distant (pp. 11–12),
but executable option selection, expiry, premiums/marks and hedge maintenance
are not specified sufficiently for an all-in portfolio replication.

## 5. Can a strategy code be made?

**Conditionally: the components are programmable, but the complete source strategy
cannot be coded faithfully from the supplied specification.** A directional system
could be written after fixing these missing rules:

- Causal peak/trough identification, confirmation delay and first/second-wave anchors.
- Numerical definitions and minimum durations for medium/long-term regimes.
- Phi-ellipse anchors, geometry, updates and as-of break/partial-exit rules.
- Exact support/resistance selection, Fibonacci-fan anchoring and branch precedence.
- Decision versus fill time: confirmation at the close is not proof of an executable
  fill at that same closing price; specify a justified auction submission or later fill.
- Integer position additions, margin schedules, loss/liquidation handling, full costs
  and precise options rules if that hedge branch is included.

I have not filled those gaps with arbitrary parameters or produced a strategy file.
The committed Python script only checks the paper's headline arithmetic and labels
the exposure examples; it is not strategy code or a historical test.

## Verdict and saved lesson
