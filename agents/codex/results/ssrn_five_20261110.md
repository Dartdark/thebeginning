## 1. Exploiting Commodity Momentum Along the Futures Curves — SSRN 2485314

Source: de Groot, Karstanje and Zhou, **May 21, 2014**, 51-page uploaded PDF. Main rules printed pp. 13–16,
Table 2 printed p. 43; [PDF](../inputs/ssrn_five_20261110/source/ssrn-2485314.pdf).

**What is it about?** Improve monthly cross-sectional commodity momentum by choosing the maturity actually held.
The universe comprises 24 GSCI commodities, January 1990–September 2011. Front-contract 12-month returns select equal-weight
top/bottom halves. Optimal-roll then longs the maturity with the largest adjacent-contract slope and shorts the smallest,
within 12 months. Its slope is `(F_previous−F_current)/(F_current * maturity_gap)`, with consistent time units; it is not a
log-basis formula. The all-contracts variant additionally changes ranking information. Low-turnover roll keeps the chosen
contract until expiry approaches or the commodity changes side instead of reselecting a maturity every month.

**What does it find?** Under its conservative cost scheme, Table 2 Panel D reports:

| Source portfolio | Annual excess return | t | Sharpe | Maximum drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Generic front-contract momentum |3.98%|1.15|0.25|−30.59%|
| Low-turnover roll momentum |8.42%|2.74|0.60|−23.24%|

These are source collateral/exposure conventions and modeled costs, not retail-margin-account CAGR. Standard cost
results differ; the low-turnover return is not 8.42% under every cost model. Alternative strategies share much of the
original momentum exposure and are not automatically separate independent sleeves.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Direction comes from relative momentum; maturity selection improves expected
convergence/roll economics and reduces turnover. This is a proposed explanation plus an historical costed comparison,
not direct observation of a compelled counterparty order or proof of a permanent premium. Deferred liquidity is worse.

**Can we build on it?** Yes, as a conditional **personal commodity portfolio implementation layer**. It requires a native
24-name maturity panel with expiry, selection, point-in-time liquidity, contracts/multipliers and cash/roll accounting.
Front-maturity slope uses shape-preserving cubic extrapolation; the exact imminent-expiry implementation also needs
reconciliation. Industrial-metal historical volume is partly backfilled in the source, unsuitable as original vintage.
Current front-continuous CL/GC files do not provide the strip. Existing free native-panel/license checks remain blocked.
Do not truncate holdings to intraday and claim the monthly return survives. Smallest next check: one native formation,
chosen maturity, hold/roll and cash ledger, before coverage/cost/power admission. Merge shared data needs with D06–D11.

**Can a strategy code be made?** **Conditional yes.** A monthly rank, maturity-selection and retention engine is
implementable after the native panel and roll conventions are admitted. No trading code or native return test is claimed.

## 2. Time Series Momentum — SSRN 2089463

Source: Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, **September 2011 version**, 62-page uploaded working paper, published 2012.
Equation 1/5 and Section 4.1–4.2; [PDF](../inputs/ssrn_five_20261110/source/ssrn-2089463.pdf).

**What is it about?** Each instrument follows the sign of its own trailing 12-month excess return, holds for a month and
scales by ex-ante volatility. This differs from cross-sectional ranking and does not skip the latest month by default.
Daily squared-return weighting has 60-day center of mass, delta=60/61, annualization 261, and lagged volatility use.

**What does it find?** Across 58 equity-index, currency, commodity and bond instruments in 1985–2009, all 58 source momentum
estimates are positive and 52 are pointwise significant at 5%. Source factor-adjusted alpha is about 1.58% per month.
That is not an expected return on the user's capital: the normalization is 40% volatility per instrument, with diversification
reducing realized portfolio volatility to about 12%. It is not a 40% account-risk recommendation or a contemporary net result.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Slow information adjustment, delayed positioning and hedger/speculator risk
transfer are candidate explanations. The source's association does not identify a forced trade or guarantee protection
through sharp reversals. Trend can benefit persistent extreme markets and still lose during fast reversals.

**Can we build on it?** Yes: a strong, simple diversified personal-account **baseline**, not a novel independent discovery.
Owned prices are a limited market subset, not the 58-name source universe or complete native excess-return/roll ledger.
Use admitted native returns, financing, publication-safe inputs, contemporary costs and integer lots before testing.
Existing failed intraday trend/NQ ports stay failed. A BTC version would be a separately specified adaptation: the source
predates BTC futures and contains no BTC validation. Smallest check: one instrument's warmup/signal/roll/cash transition,
then a genuinely specified universe; no automatic ticker substitution or flat-by-15:59 truncation.

**Can a strategy code be made?** **Yes for a research signal; conditional for an executable portfolio.** Source lookback,
volatility and monthly position rules are explicit. Missing native panels and account-risk accounting block a validated
trading implementation. There is no source-backed intraday Apex or BTC strategy ready to trade from this review.

## 3. Value and Momentum Everywhere — SSRN 2174501

Source: Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, **June 2012 draft**, 72-page uploaded PDF. Sections I.B–D/II/V and Table I;
[PDF](../inputs/ssrn_five_20261110/source/ssrn-2174501.pdf). Do not silently substitute a later published version.

**What is it about?** Combine value and momentum across eight market groups. Momentum uses prior 12 months excluding the
latest month; value differs by asset class. The study forms tercile portfolios and rank-weighted long/short factors,
plus 50/50 value/momentum combinations. It is not a single chart indicator with uniform stock/bond/FX definitions.

**What does it find?** Value and momentum are negatively correlated within/across markets, but each style has strong
common exposure across countries/assets. The draft's Table I Panel B global combination Sharpe is 1.36 for top-minus-bottom
portfolios and 1.45 for rank factors; narrative Section II.A says 1.42. These are gross returns through July 2011 with differing
market starting dates. We visually checked PDF page 53: the discrepancy is in the PDF, not an extraction correction.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Persistent value/momentum premia plus diversification between negatively related
styles. Funding-liquidity risk partly explains commonality; different underlyings can still hold the same economic bet.
The work does not prove low correlation or protection for our present NQ strategies.

**Can we build on it?** Yes as personal-portfolio architecture and a baseline for factor diversification. The uploaded
draft has a second implementation ambiguity: printed p. 9 describes bond value as negative past 5-year return, while
printed p. 14 and Table I Panel C identify 5-year yield change. Reconcile that definition/version before exact replication.
Need historical stock book/market with reporting lags, spot/FX/interest/yield/value inputs as appropriate, long warmups,
membership, borrow/costs and native returns. Generic close-only files cannot supply that full panel. No return test earned.

**Can a strategy code be made?** **Conditional yes.** Monthly asset-specific ranks and combinations are codeable; exact
replication first needs the bond-rule reconciliation and matching inputs. No immediate intraday port is established.

## 4. Carry — uploaded as SSRN 2298565

Source: Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt, **November 2016 draft**, 67 pages. The uploaded file lacks an embedded SSRN
footer; attribution retains the user filename and verified title/authors/version, not an invented footer match.
Equations 18–21, Tables II/IX; [PDF](../inputs/ssrn_five_20261110/source/ssrn-2298565.pdf).

**What is it about?** Carry is the return if the relevant price state stays unchanged, not an assured total return.
Its measurement is asset-specific: futures convergence, FX interest differences, bond roll-down and option decay differ.
Monthly demeaned rank weights sum to +1 long/−1 short. Current carry and a 12-month carry average are distinct variants.

**What does it find?** Diversified source carry has gross Sharpe about 1.20; average asset-class evidence is positive.
Costs materially change parts of the result. Table IX put-option carry falls from 1.80 gross Sharpe to −0.67 at two
half-spreads; call-option carry is also highly cost-sensitive. Broad carry profitability is not proof every leg survives
retail execution, and option carry should not inherit the commodity cost result.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Carry can reflect risk premia and constrained demand. The source finds common
recession/liquidity/volatility exposures that do not fully explain returns. Adverse price repricing can overwhelm the
carry collected. It is not a guaranteed yield, and this is not the first place to infer protection against VB/ONB tails.

**Can we build on it?** Yes as a personal cross-asset signal framework with asset-specific source conventions. Need
synchronized spot/forwards/curves, financing and collateral, native cash/rolls and option surfaces/execution where used.
Neither one continuous CL series nor a cryptocurrency's advertised funding rate reproduces the paper. A BTC basis or
perpetual version is new research with separate venue/settlement/borrow/funding clocks. Smallest next check: one
identified instrument's ex-ante carry, subsequent price change and full cash ledger, without assuming carry=profit.

**Can a strategy code be made?** **Conditional yes.** The rank/portfolio framework is implementable once native carry
fields and cash accounting exist. An exact across-class trading strategy cannot be validated from the owned OHLCV alone.

## 5. Market Inefficiencies Surrounding Energy Announcements — SSRN 3747330

Source: Alturki and Kurov, **August 2021 draft**, 39 pages, published 2022. Section 3.7, Tables 2/8 and Appendix A;
[PDF](../inputs/ssrn_five_20261110/source/ssrn-3747330.pdf).

**What is it about?** Sequential API and EIA oil inventory announcements. Prior API actual inventory change minus
Bloomberg's **EIA** inventory-change median consensus forecasts a component of pre-EIA WTI trading. This is not the
API actual minus API consensus, and not merely a crude-price momentum indicator.

**What does it find?** Source January 2011–August 2019, 385 Wednesday EIA events; source Table 8:

| Entry to exit relative to EIA | Full 385 events Sharpe | Extreme 77 events Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|
|−60 to−1 minutes|0.67|2.10|
|−90 to−1 minutes|0.62|1.05|
|−120 to−1 minutes|0.54|1.19|

Annualization uses event frequency, not 252 trades/year. Extreme deciles are roughly nine events/year, not a daily strategy;
past-only percentile construction is undocumented. No native transaction-cost deduction or net trading holdout is
demonstrated. Recursive appendix forecast tests do not establish Table 8 as net out-of-sample trading P&L.
Separately, Table 2's post-API 30 minute return predicts the pre-EIA−60/−1 minute return with OLS slope 0.17/R² 0.04.
That regression is not the fundamental strategy or evidence for a 2.10 Sharpe price-sign substitute.

**Why does it make money, if it does?** Some API information may enter EIA expectations/prices slowly, creating continued
adjustment before the next release. The source supports predictability in its setting, not a measured current forced
quantity or modern micro-account alpha. The illiquidity split uses the API-through-EIA interval, including future target
returns/volume, so it cannot be a known-at-entry filter without a separately justified lagged definition.

**Can we build on it?** Conditional intraday research lead, normal 09:30–10:29 Eastern (08:30–09:29 Chicago). Freeze the
actual entry/exit/fill clock, data rights and native costs before any return test. Exact source needs actual API values
and a Bloomberg EIA consensus snapshot known before entry, plus actual historical release/correction/receipt clocks.
Current API distribution is subscription-based; no purchase was made or requested. Owned fourteen-series macro data
contain no API/EIA series. A different website's forecast is not silently substituted for Bloomberg.

**New input work completed:** recovered 106 official API 2021/22 schedule rows; joined the existing retrospective EIA
calendar on matching inventory week and next-day Wednesday publication. This yields 90 matched date pairs (46 in 2021,
44 in 2022). Direct owned-CL reads confirm **all 90 have the required API/EIA endpoint and planned pre-EIA minute rows**.
This resolves the earlier empty-connector/row-read gap; do not request duplicate CL exports for that window.
The schedules permit date changes. The EIA calendar's exact-clock/original-publication flags remain false; actual API
receipts, original consensus vintages, CL export timezone, native contract/roll identity and micro execution are unverified.
These are retrospective coverage counts, **not 90 admitted tradable events**. No returns or signal moves were computed.

The repository already tested API response follow/fade and API-evening-to-next-morning price rules. A price-only
late-hour adaptation would be related-family research needing its own justification and committed protocol, not a
fresh independent discovery or automatic rescue. Exact source strategy remains separately NEEDS DATA. Smallest next
check: one original API release, contemporaneous pre-entry EIA consensus, dated CL contract and executable exit quote.
Do not replace these inputs merely to produce a backtest, or tune clocks/tails after seeing outcomes.

**Can a strategy code be made?** **Conditional yes.** With admitted original inputs, short when API actual − EIA consensus > 0,
long when the difference is < 0, enter EIA−60 minutes and flatten before release; exact tail/zero/fill/risk rules must be frozen. A source
research adapter can be written, but the reported performance cannot yet be reproduced faithfully. No trading code
or economic preregistration is claimed from the source/data audit.

## Research decision and retained lessons

For an intraday evaluation lead, prioritize the oil paper's missing-information clock, not a mechanical monthly port.
For long-horizon personal research, curve-aware low-turnover momentum and diversified TSMOM are useful foundations;
value/carry help specify a broader portfolio but are not five independently verified new alphas. A BTC trend/carry
adaptation is not a result of any of these pre-crypto samples.
