It proposes that AI-supported execution can crowd out fresh evidence about serious alternatives.

## 1. What is it about?

After a firm chooses a course of action, that course produces telemetry, milestones, costs and customer feedback. Alternatives receive fewer updates unless someone deliberately maintains them. The author calls this difference the **counterfactual observability gap**. AI may amplify it by repeatedly summarizing and scoring the evidence-rich chosen course.

The concern is that “more documented” can become confused with “better than the alternatives.” The proposed response is to preserve enough evidence, documentation and capacity to reconsider at least one serious alternative, without keeping every possibility alive indefinitely (abstract; sections 3–4, 7, 11).

## 2. What does it find?

**No measured effect is established by this paper.** Section 4 (pp. 7–11) formalizes evidence accumulation/depreciation and practical reversibility. Signs of important relationships are assumed; coefficients, evidence weights and viability thresholds are not estimated from a dataset. The equations organize a hypothesis rather than validate it.

Section 6 (p. 13) states ten hypotheses. Section 8 (pp. 16–17) proposes a 2×2 experiment comparing single-recommendation versus role-differentiated AI, and execution-only dashboards versus maintained alternative evidence. Its suggested 480–560 participants and eight rounds are a study design, not a sample that was collected. Appendices A–C propose measurements and protocols.

Section 10 (pp. 18–19) discusses Tesla, Meta, Zillow and GE as illustrations and explicitly disclaims causal proof. It does not establish that missing alternative evidence caused those firms' outcomes. Results attributed to other cited studies are not results newly demonstrated here.

Material weaknesses: construct measurement remains unvalidated; proxies can differ in diagnostic value; AI-specific causality needs human/non-AI controls; and an evidence-rich incumbent may actually be superior. Preserving alternatives has a cost. Section 11 acknowledges that routine, low-switching-cost decisions may benefit from narrowing the option set.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

It demonstrates **no trading profit or financial performance benefit**. The proposed organizational benefit is reduced cost or delay when changing course after adverse news. That is an untested prediction here, not an estimated return.

There is no participant compelled to trade NQ/ES, signed flow, information-to-entry clock, executable signal, stop/exit rule, event frequency or cost-adjusted return series. Test power and account contribution cannot be calculated from this source. Corporate “strategic futures” must not be interpreted as an instrument-specific futures-market hypothesis. It would be inappropriate to invent an NQ signal from the terminology.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**Yes as a research-process reminder; no as a trading foundation.** The repository already retains frozen forward rules, failed tests, independent checks, drift alarms and BASE/ADD/REPLACE/EMPTY comparisons. The useful addition is to make the freshness and comparability of evidence explicit whenever an existing serious alternative is reviewed.

The following is a local adaptation of section 7/Table 5, not a proven performance improvement or an amendment to repository gates:

| At an already-authorized review | Minimal record to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the comparison before new results | Incumbent and one eligible frozen alternative; exact rule/code/input versions; why the alternative remains serious; existing rejection/retirement rule |
| Check comparable coverage | Common eligible dates, data cutoff and missing periods; distinguish genuine zero-signal days from missing observations |
| Compare like evidence | Live realized fills labelled separately from hypothetical/paper fills; same costs, sizing, account constraints and opportunity set where a fair comparison permits |
| Separate two questions | Operational health: did the chosen system run? Comparative merit: does it outperform BASE and the appropriate EMPTY/REPLACE control? A healthy heartbeat cannot answer the second question |
| Preserve a way to revisit | Existing code/specification, source hashes, latest review and the smallest next check; do not require another search to reconstruct the rule |
| Fix reconsideration and stopping rules | Use the existing frozen checkpoint/drift rule. Do not change thresholds after a bad week, automatically replace the incumbent, or resurrect failed definitions |

This is especially relevant to the recent Swing Target replay: better component accounting did not establish a better funded plan. It is also relevant to the extended-data audit: a longer price grid does not make stale trade lists current. These are repository findings, not evidence validating Barri's organizational theory.

A practical next step at the next existing comparative review is to include both evidence cutoffs and live-versus-paper status beside its incumbent/alternative table. No new dashboard, data collection, recurring automation, strategy backtest, or multi-agent system is necessary for this source-only review. The paper's proposed role-differentiated experiment does not instruct or authorize us to spawn agents.
