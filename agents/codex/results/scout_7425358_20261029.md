# Calendar spreads: useful execution reference; no directional strategy

Summary: Retain listed-spread versus separate-leg execution as a future comparison for grains and livestock.
Authors report lower quoted execution costs, roughly half the synthetic comparison; no savings reproduced here.
No signed forced-flow predictor or eligible return test was established.

## Four questions

1. **About:** Ma, Serra, Peterson and Irwin compare listed calendar spreads, separate-leg synthetic spreads and implied liquidity in corn, soybeans, live cattle and lean hogs.
2. **Findings:** Their conference paper uses grains 2015–2024 and livestock 2015–2019. Section 3.1, printed pp.6–7, reports lower quoted costs for listed spreads, roughly half the synthetic comparison. Section 4.1.2 reports greater adverse selection with implied liquidity without a comparable overall realized-spread gain. Section 4 explicitly disclaims causal identification.
3. **Why money:** This is potential execution-cost reduction and lower legging exposure, not demonstrated directional alpha. Better displayed liquidity does not guarantee better realized fills or market-making profit.
4. **Build on it:** Yes, as an execution reference. Compare synchronized executable listed-spread quotes against both legs, including fees, size, delay and partial-fill risk. We lack those inputs; do not substitute continuous OHLC or add this to the strategy queue.

## Our assessment and smallest future check

Author-reported quote comparisons are not our order fills. The synthetic comparison assumes simultaneous leg execution, while our eventual real orders would have latency and partial-fill risk. A useful benchmark therefore needs a common decision time, actual spread/leg identifiers, paired quantities, receipt clocks and a gross-to-net cost ledger. Avoid double counting shared implied liquidity. Static counterfactual quotes do not represent how all participants would reprice if market design changed.

Price discovery is not a ready trading signal. A common-price contribution does not alone establish a predictive lead we could trade after receipt. Transfer to ES/NQ or micros remains unverified. Price-limit episodes also require separate execution-risk treatment before any study is admitted.
