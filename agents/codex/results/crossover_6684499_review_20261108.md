## Five questions

1. **What is it about?** A fixed 50/200-day SMA crossover on AAPL, MSFT, JPM, XOM and JNJ, January2010–December2023.
   Each stock is long or cash, without leverage or shorts, with a 20% portfolio slot. The author compares it with
   a passive equal-weight portfolio and reports2010–2018 versus 2019–2023 individual-stock results.
2. **What does it find?** Reported portfolio annualized return is 12.04% versus 17.27%, Sharpe 0.779 versus 0.849,
   and maximum drawdown−25.79% versus−35.23%. All five stocks have lower full-sample strategy annualized returns.
   The portfolio mean daily return difference has t−2.169,p0.030. This is source-reported negative raw benchmark
   performance, not a formal statistical test of risk-adjusted alpha or our independently reproduced result.
3. **Why does it make money, if it does?** The long/cash rule can participate in stock appreciation while sitting
   out some declines. It makes positive absolute returns in the author's portfolio test but fails to beat the same
   basket passively held. Trend persistence is proposed, without an identified forced flow or causal return premium.
   Lower exposure and missed rebounds can explain risk/return differences, but no controlled attribution proves
   their contribution. Do not treat the negative result as evidence that all markets are efficient.
4. **Can we build on it?** Retain another bounded negative study and the distinction between lower drawdown,
   raw excess return and risk-adjusted advantage. No positive source result justifies another crossover sweep,
   ML optimizer or regime filter. MSFT's individual OOS result is insufficient to select it after seeing the table.
   This adds no distinct mechanism beyond existing [indicator](market_indicators_2449344_review_20261108.md)
   and [stock-trend attribution](one_ticker_7073258_review_20261108.md) reviews. No new data purchase/acquisition
   or return test is warranted at this failed source/evidence gate.
5. **Can a strategy code be made?** Yes: compute50/200-day averages, enter after an upward cross and exit after
   a downward cross, carrying the position between crosses. Delay decisions to an executable subsequent fill;
   allocate20% per eligible stock slot and charge the stated 10bp per side. Exact replication still needs the
   original code/data, warm-up and initial-state policy, cash yield, signal/fill timing, portfolio rebalancing
   and metric definitions. This overnight stock strategy is a possible personal research form, not a source-faithful
   intraday Apex rule. Code feasibility does not establish a profitable strategy; no trading code was warranted.

## Interpretation and reproduction limits

- **Return convention:** Methodology defines annualized return as `(1 + mean daily return)^252 − 1`.
  This is not the geometric CAGR of the compounded path. Do not relabel the reported 12.04%/17.27% as CAGR.
  Prices are described as adjusted closes and Table 1 describes log returns; original code is needed to establish
  whether strategy compounding consistently uses simple versus log returns.
- **Sharpe dimensions:** The prose describes annual excess return divided by annual volatility but prints an
  additional sqrt252 and a 2% annual risk-free input. The daily/annual units are ambiguous. No return series or
  implementation is supplied to resolve the actual calculation; the reported ratios remain source claims.
- **Statistics:** A t-test of mean daily strategy-minus-benchmark returns does not test Sharpe equality or a
  factor-model alpha. Fat tails and dependence require appropriate inference; large sample size alone is not a
  blanket cure. No HAC/block-bootstrap replication or multiple-testing adjustment is established. Rounded
  confidence limits ending−0.0000 do not reveal the exact bound.
- **Diversification claim:** Both portfolios hold the same five names; observed drawdown differences cannot be
  attributed solely to sector diversification. Cash timing/exposure, warm-up and rebalancing differ or are
  unresolved. AAPL/JNJ full-sample drawdowns are deeper under the crossover; protection is not universal.
- **Portfolio/fills:** Averaging five stock return streams with constant20% slots implies a portfolio weight
  maintenance convention. Rebalance turnover and associated costs are not reconciled against charging fees only
  at crossover position changes. Same adjusted-close signal and return timing also need the next-fill clock;
  a one-day shift alone does not establish attainable closing execution.
- **Warm-up/cash:**200-day averages require prehistory or a common delayed evaluation start. Initial regime
  handling, warm-up benchmark treatment and whether idle cash earns the same risk-free rate subtracted in Sharpe
  are unspecified. Original historical downloads/adjustment vintages are unavailable in the supplied PDF.
- **Selection and scope:** The five surviving stocks span four sectors, with two technology names, not five
  independent risks. The stated historical split does not certify a prospective untouched holdout given later
  stock selection. A16-cell full-sample AAPL heatmap is not White's Reality Check and does not solve data snooping;
  proposing RL/boosting to improve it supplies no evidence those methods work.
