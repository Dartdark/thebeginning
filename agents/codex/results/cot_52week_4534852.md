# Commodity futures at 52-week extremes: behavior explains positions, not a proven return signal

Summary: Laubsch, Smales and Vo study 12 commodity markets, September 1992–December 2022.
Commercial positions tend to react contrarily to prices; non-commercial positions follow momentum.
Responses weaken at 52-week highs and strengthen at lows, but the authors find little market-timing ability.
Their regressions do not establish an executable, cost-adjusted strategy; CL/GC offer no convincing new lead.
Verdict: DON'T TEST as a strategy from this source alone; zero new economic trials, no plan changes.

Source: Joshua Laubsch, Lee A. Smales and Duc Vo, *Commodity Futures Trading at the 52-Week High and Low*,
uploaded version dated August 8, 2023, [SSRN 4534852](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4534852).

## What is it about?

Weekly CFTC Legacy commercial and non-commercial long/short positions in natural gas, heating oil, WTI,
corn, soybeans, wheat, copper, gold, silver, cocoa, coffee and sugar. Positions are long minus short divided
by open interest. The paper excludes non-reportable traders. Datastream prices form a rolled weekly series;
returns run Tuesday to Tuesday to align with position observation dates. Controls include rates, credit spread,
dividend yield, VIX, term slope and backwardation. This is commodity behavior, not NQ/ES evidence.

Equation 2 explains **changes in positions** using prior returns, 52-week high/low indicators and interactions.
Equation 3 separately asks whether positions predict **returns**. Success of the first does not establish the second.

## What does it find?

Commercials generally increase short exposure after rising prices, consistent with producer hedging; speculators
increase long exposure, consistent with trend-following. Both group's response to further price changes is
moderated at highs and amplified at lows. These are response slopes and positions, not a statement that every
new high reverses or every low rebounds. The results broadly persist across curve states, with qualifications
in the detailed regressions. Position levels, position changes and outright long/short results are distinct.

Table 4 generally favors returns preceding position changes. Specific counterexamples exist, so do not turn
the conclusion into a universal no-predictability theorem. For our directly relevant commodities:

| Table 4 null: position changes do not Granger-predict returns | Commercial p | Non-commercial p |
|---|---:|---:|
| WTI oil | 0.280 | 0.566 |
| Gold | 0.623 | 0.738 |

These are source-reported four-lag tests, not our return backtests. Granger predictability is not structural causation.

Table 8, the market-timing regressions, reports 1,528 observations per market. Its strongest directly discussed
52-week positioning-interaction result is silver commercial HI52×ΔNP: coefficient 0.283, standard error 0.129,
marked significant at 5%. That isolated coefficient among many markets, groups and regressors does not supply
an independently validated silver strategy. Neither oil nor gold has a 5%-significant positioning interaction
with high/low in either trader group; gold's commercial low interaction is only marked at 10%.
WTI's commercial change-in-position coefficient is likewise only a 10% result. Do not select those exceptions
after observing the table and report them as clean holdout evidence.

The paper's own conclusion is **very limited market-timing evidence**. It presents no frozen entry/exit/stop,
net P&L, Sharpe, trade ledger, account margin path or chronological out-of-sample trading evaluation.

## Why would it make money?

The source explains why different groups trade: physical producers hedge inventory and CTAs pursue trends;
anchoring and attention may influence their response near salient prices. Commercial hedges are evaluated
together with physical exposure, so their futures P&L is not a standalone outsider strategy's objective.
No required order quantity/deadline, executable imbalance or outsider's predictive advantage is demonstrated.
Following the speculators and fading the commercials are not two independently established edges.

The data is group inventory, not signed transactions, executions or a record of each order's intent. The
[CFTC methodology](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm) says classifications
reflect predominant business purpose and staff do not know specific reasons for positions. Keep Legacy
definitions: a TFF dealer series or managed-money series is not a drop-in replacement for these groups.

## Can we build on it for the plans?

**No strategy is earned by this paper alone.** It weakens the claim that observing who bought or sold near a
52-week extreme gives a reliable next-return signal. Existing repository studies already reject COT Dealer
Rank as an Apex day trade/filter and the COT reversal-index gate on NQ/ES (`TIER_LIST.md`). A weak weekly
long-side Dealer Rank result is separately tracked and is not corroborated by this commodity paper.
The broader price-anchor monthly study is also already ranked D; this paper does not revive it.

Publication timing is a further implementation barrier. The official
[CFTC release schedule](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ReleaseSchedule/index.htm)
normally releases Tuesday's position snapshot on Friday at 15:30 Eastern, with holiday delays. Thus a
Tuesday-to-next-Tuesday return interval has already partly elapsed before the prior Tuesday snapshot becomes
public. A regression lag is not proof of availability at a proposed entry. A causal public strategy must use
actual publication/receipt time and returns after its executable fill, a different protocol from the table.
This caution does not invalidate the paper's descriptive study; it limits trading inference.

Owned Legacy COT ZIPs for 2017–26 and CL/GC minute prices exist, but that does not establish full 1992–2022
12-market Datastream replication, release vintages, matching rolls, controls or exact extreme definitions.
The prior CL COT state intake stopped at an exact-name identity gate in 2022; its same-code/new-name records
were not silently admitted. This upload supplies no amendment to that unrelated frozen intake.
No new position counts, price returns, aliases or curve proxies were tested here. Further data acquisition
does not repair the prior absence of a specified causal edge, so no unrelated collection project was started.

Possible future research requires a genuinely new, independently supported hypothesis and a release-timed,
frozen rule. It must beat the same-window price-only momentum/reversal baseline, include actual costs/rolls,
handle all eligible events, and satisfy design, validation and account gates. Intraday Apex adaptation cannot
inherit evidence from a weekly regression; personal weekly holds also need full contract/cash/margin accounting.
Exact high/low price convention, tie/inclusion rules, roll-adjustment method and negative-price treatment must
be pinned before replication rather than selected to improve performance.

## Decision, verification and retained lesson

**DON'T TEST** a new trading family on this source's behavioral coefficients. No promising-strategy queue
entry, economic definition, trading grade or current-book allocation is justified. This is an evidence-gate
decision, not a failed net-P&L backtest or proof that all COT research is useless. Performance metrics and
BASE/ADD/REPLACE/EMPTY comparisons remain unavailable because no eligible return study advanced.

Retain the findings as a source-specific addition to **execution reference E44**, rather than duplicate the
existing weekly-COT inventory/publication lesson. No order code, installation, live/VPS/broker action or
external message followed. The uploaded document was evidence, not an instruction source.
