# Organizational foresight review: process reference, no trading hypothesis

Summary: This is corporate scenario-planning literature, not financial futures research.
The authors screen 259 articles and analyze 114 about how firms anticipate and respond to change.
Their synthesis links scanning, interpretation and action, with multiple possible futures.
No market signal, forecast accuracy, strategy return or causal trading benefit is demonstrated.
Retain the review as a process reminder; no backtest, candidate or new workflow is justified.

Source: René Rohrbeck & Manuel Bade, *Environmental scanning, futures research,
strategic foresight and organizational future orientation: a review, integration,
and future research directions*, ISPIM Annual Conference 2012, Barcelona, Spain.
[SSRN2080448](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2080448).

## 1. What is it about?

How firms identify external changes, interpret what they mean, and respond.
“Futures research” here means exploring possible future business environments.
It does not refer to exchange-traded futures contracts or predict market prices.

The authors search Web of Science's business/economics subject area across four
research streams, primarily 1980 through November 2011. The automated screen
identifies 259 articles; manual filtering for the firm perspective leaves **114**
for content analysis (pp.2–4). The abstract's “more than 250” language must not
be mistaken for 250 full articles in the final content-analysis set.

| Research stream | Articles in content analysis | Main emphasis in the synthesis |
|---|---:|---|
| Environmental scanning | 45 | Search for external changes and weak signals |
| Futures research | 34 | Explore multiple possible futures and flexible plans |
| Peripheral vision | 6 | Detect developments outside the current business focus |
| Corporate/strategic foresight | 29 | Translate perceived change into organizational decisions |

These are author-reported literature counts, not independent observations of
trading performance. Table 1 on p.8 compares their assumptions, methods and aims.

## 2. What does it find?

The main result is a conceptual synthesis: these streams overlap and can be
integrated as organizational future orientation. Early recognition alone is
insufficient; scanning must connect to interpretation and action (pp.8–9).
The review discusses attention bias toward successful/current activities,
external discontinuities and rare high-impact “wild cards” (pp.6–7).

The authors argue for clearer common terminology, stronger connections to
strategic/innovation management, and more testing and reproduction of earlier
findings instead of repeatedly introducing disconnected concepts (pp.9–10).
Their descriptions of organizational benefits summarize prior literature and
case studies; this review supplies no pooled causal effect size or new experiment
establishing an organizational-performance improvement.

Evidence limitations:

- Database, keyword and business/economics filters restrict what is included.
  Manual firm-perspective selection can further shape the sample. The review
  is not an exhaustive catalogue of all research on future planning.
- The literature cutoff is November 2011; this cannot establish current market,
  trading-system or AI-research practice.
- The paper synthesizes heterogeneous conceptual, survey and case-study work.
  Frequency of a term or count of articles is not a measure of its effectiveness.
- “Desired outcomes” in Table 1 are goals of the research streams, not measured
  returns or promises that the proposed organizational systems achieve them.
- Calls for sensors, teams, maturity models and response systems are source
  proposals. They do not instruct or authorize creating agents, monitors,
  schedules or live trading controls in this project.

## 3. Why does it make money, if it does?

No trading profit is demonstrated. The proposed organizational benefit is earlier
recognition of threats/opportunities and a better response when circumstances
change. This is a planning rationale; it supplies no participant forced to trade,
signed demand, instrument, entry/exit rule, information-to-fill clock, trade
count, cost bridge, net edge or account contribution.

Scenario planning is useful for assessing robustness, but does not estimate the
probability of a scenario or show that a particular hedge or strategy pays for
its costs. The paper cannot justify changing an Apex size or selecting a trading
direction. Financial strategy metrics and plan placement are not applicable.

## 4. Can we build on it?

**As a process reminder, yes; as a trading foundation, no.** A restrained local
interpretation is: at an already-authorized review, distinguish the observed
change, the explanation supported by evidence, and the bounded next decision.
Consider more than one plausible failure scenario, and preserve the existing
rejection/retirement criteria rather than treating a weak signal as authority
to retune a strategy.

This is our adaptation, not a tested recommendation from the paper for traders.
The repository already has evidence sheets, preregistration, independent checks,
BASE/ADD/REPLACE/EMPTY comparisons, stress tests and drift checkpoints. The
related `ssrn6656198_review.md` already distinguishes operational health from
comparative evidence and retains alternative specifications. This upload does
not warrant adding a parallel process, another dashboard or a new monitor.

No price data acquisition or return backtest is warranted: there is no financial
hypothesis to reproduce. No promising-strategy queue entry, tier, portfolio
change or execution-library entry is earned. The paper contains no new finding
about fills, slippage, order handling or market behavior. Existing research
controls remain unchanged; no source-prescribed experiment was performed.
