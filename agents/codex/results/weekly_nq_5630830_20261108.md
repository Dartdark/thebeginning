## Five paper-review questions

**1. What is it about?** Stratos Chatzimanolakis, *Weekly Nasdaq Futures Strategy: A Volatility-Adjusted,
Low-Beta Alpha System for Allocators and Traders*, October2025, SSRN5630830. This is a weekly long-only
continuous-NQ technical system with EMA, VIX-relative-strength and custom RSI conditions. It presents
TrendSpider screenshots and allocator metrics, not a documented forced-flow study or independent experiment.
The21page upload and embedded rule image were inspected; rules are partly visible in images, not absent.

Treat the final explicit weekly-return definition as the final claimed metric version. The earlier conflicting
numbers remain a reconciliation issue, not proof that every figure is wrong. Sample length alone does not
establish a chronological out-of-sample test, multiplicity control or investable dollar sizing.

**3. Why does it make money, if it does?** The proposed explanation is equity drift plus filtering trends
with relative strength against VIX, while avoiding overheated RSI/high-VIX conditions. There is no demonstrated
forced counterparty, capacity estimate or incremental net edge over matched simple trend/exposure controls.
An NQ/VIX price ratio is not itself volatility-targeted sizing. The source's lowbeta does not establish an
uncorrelated return or downside hedge: correlation~0.7is materially positive. Raw price and ratio units, daily
reset/normalization, available-at times and sizing all matter before attributing an advantage to this feature.

**4. Can we build on it?** Retain the useful distinctions between trade diagnostics, periodic investor returns,
beta, correlation and executable stop risk. **No addition to either50K/150Kevaluation or funded futures plan**
as written:3.3week average holdings violate intraday flat rules. A personal-account strategy is theoretically
possible, but no independently costed native ledger or diversification benefit has been established. This
paper supplies no verified improvement to the current portfolio. Do not invent an intraday version or transfer
the displayed drawdown to a personal account. No promising-strategy queue promotion/data purchase is warranted.

**5. Can strategy code be made?** **Only conditionally.** Standard EMA/RSI components are easy to code, and
the screenshot gives a partial decision specification. Exact replication still needs custom TDM indicator
formulas/initialization, full source settings, VIX-vs-VX identity, weekly bar/receipt clocks, same-candle exit
fill semantics, and native contract/roll/cost/capital treatment. A Python or PowerLanguage implementation would
be a directional weekly long strategy, not an execution algorithm. No return engine or MultiCharts strategy
was made from guessed formulas. The committed code checks source arithmetic and invented risk paths only.

## What could make this wrong

Actual custom code and full logs might make the rule and metrics reproducible. Simple volatility scaling may
explain lowbeta legitimately; it still does not make correlation disappear. A genuine weekly mark-to-market
series might support good results, but sparse trade endpoints and rescaling are insufficient evidence here.
The cost percentage may correspond to a valid configured basis; no native amount is certified without its
notional/cash/side/round-trip definition, slippage, commissions and contract rolls. Weekly drawdown omits finer
paths relevant to margin/trailing-account breaches. No clean holdout or trial history was established.
