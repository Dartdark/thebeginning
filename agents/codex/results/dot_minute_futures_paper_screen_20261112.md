## 1. Timed cross-sectional futures ML: reading note, not a setup

**Waldow, Schnaubelt, Krauss and Fischer (2021), [Machine Learning in Futures Markets](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/14/3/119), DOI 10.3390/jrfm14030119.** About/finds: 60 US/European front-month futures, minute OHLCV, seven chronological 750-training/250-trading-day windows; baseline RF gross annual return 16.2%, Sharpe 1.19. **Trigger:** rank probabilities of outperforming the next-h-day cross-sectional median using 31 lagged-return features; buy top three, short bottom three daily. Signal VWAP ends 10:30, entry assumes 10:31–11:31 VWAP, exit uses 09:30–10:30 VWAP after one to three days; the source says EST, requiring DST clarification. **Frequency:** roughly 250 portfolio decisions/year, not 250 NQ trades. **After cost:** full-turn break-even costs are 6.3bp for baseline RF; three-day GBC reaches 24.2bp. These are cost budgets, not net returns or net ticks. Net instrument ticks are unidentified. **Who pays:** authors suggest short-term reversal; no demonstrated forced-flow payer. **Build/code:** a research implementation is feasible, but nine owned symbols cannot silently replace 60 selected markets. Minute-derived VWAP is not guaranteed executable VWAP; rolls, sizing and fills need verification. Multi-day exposure also conflicts with the evaluation's daily-flat constraint. This is a new bibliographic source in an already-explored reversal family, not an independently validated engine. Full text inspected, especially §§3.1–3.4 and Tables 1–3.

## 2. Holiday-Friday natural-gas attention: reading note, not a setup

**Gu, Kurov and Stan, [Trader Attention and Market Reaction to Fundamental News: Evidence from Natural Gas Futures](https://business.ucdenver.edu/jpmorgancenter/sites/default/files/attached-files/2._trader_attention_and_market_reaction_to_fundamental_news96.pdf), [published July 2026](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/trader-attention-and-market-reaction-to-fundamental-news-evidence/), DOI 10.1002/fut.70104.** About/finds: May 2002–December 2023 inventory announcements; holiday-shifted Friday releases have much weaker immediate surprise responses. **Causal trigger:** the release calendar is known beforehand; surprise direction becomes available only when actual inventory is released against a point-in-time consensus. A post-release trade could buy negative surprises/short positive ones, but that executable rule is not established here. The paper examines immediate response and announcement-close to next-session-open drift. **Frequency:** 31 Friday calendar events, only 29 usable in the empirical sample, about 1.3 usable events/year. **Data:** NG minute prices and release calendars are feasible; historical Bloomberg consensus vintages are not verified owned. Same-day attention measures cannot be treated as pre-entry signals. **After cost/OOS:** net ticks, executable fills and chronological trading OOS are not established. **Who pays:** delayed attention, not an identified forced seller/buyer. **Build/code:** event classification is feasible; a strategy remains unspecified. Friday-close to Sunday-open exposure is not an ordinary overnight implementation. Best present use: distinguish holiday releases in research rather than claiming 52 annual opportunities. Full manuscript inspected; no strategy promotion.

## 3. Post-FOMC Treasury drift: source-limited reading lead

**Indriawan, Jiao and Tse (2021), [The FOMC announcement returns on long-term US and German bond futures](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378426620302880), DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2020.106027.** About/finds: transaction-level ten-year US/German bond futures, January 2009–December 2018; expansionary surprises precede positive drift over 15 days, with opposite direction after contractionary surprises. The abstract reports strategy Sharpe up to four times buy-and-hold, not a net-tick ledger. **Trigger/data availability:** a direction based on the released monetary-policy surprise is plausible, but the precise surprise construction, usable publication clock, entry, exit and required order-flow inputs were not recoverable from the accessible primary preview. **Frequency:** around eight scheduled meetings/year before filters is a calendar ceiling, not an observed trade count. **After cost/OOS:** net ticks, costs and chronological trading OOS are unverified. **Who pays:** delayed information assimilation, not a verified forced-flow counterparty. **Build/code:** a descriptive event study can be coded; a source-faithful strategy cannot yet be specified. Fifteen-day holds are outside the requested short multiday/evaluation fit. This is distinct from the previously indexed pre-FOMC Treasury paper, but not an admitted ZN engine. Publisher full text was unavailable; this card intentionally makes no full-paper claim.

## Other near misses worth closing, not adding to the setup list

**Miao, Ramchander and Zumwalt (2011), [Information Driven Price Jumps and Trading Strategy: Evidence from Stock Index Futures](https://ssrn.com/abstract=1920401), [inspected manuscript reproduction](https://www.academia.edu/18131980/Information_Driven_Price_Jumps_and_Trading_Strategy_Evidence_from_Stock_Index_Futures).** About/finds: 2001–2010 full-size/pit-era Dow, Nasdaq and S&P futures, not a direct modern e-mini replication. **Trigger:** trade with actual-minus-consensus news when simultaneous surprises agree; enter at the first transaction one minute after a 10:00 release and exit one minute later. This does have a delay; it should not be dismissed as an instantaneous fill. **Frequency:** the Dow example has 516 trades in ten years, approximately 52/year. **After cost:** ten-year cumulative net returns are 15.86%, 19.42% and 29.27%, respectively, assuming one tick each way. Net mean ticks are not identifiable from those percentages. **Who pays:** slower news assimilation is proposed; no forced counterparty is established. **Build/code:** minute-rule code is feasible only after recovering point-in-time consensus and exact contracts; the inspected work does not establish chronological OOS or today's costs. It also overlaps the already-tested headline-follow family. SSRN full text was inaccessible; the reproduction, not the SSRN PDF, was inspected. Reject as current setup, not as a claim that the historical results are false.

**Gu, Kurov and Halova Wolfe (2018), [Relief Rallies after FOMC Announcements as a Resolution of Uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2018.08.003), [SSRN 2810262](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2810262).** About/trigger: buy ES five minutes before SEP-associated announcements and sell 55 minutes after. **Sample/frequency:** 2012–2016, 40 meetings, 21 SEP/press-conference events, about 4.2 trades/year. The 2012 statement/SEP clocks differ; the indexed primary excerpts do not resolve that year's trade anchor. **Availability:** scheduled event type is known beforehand; exact historical clocks still need resolution. **After cost/OOS:** reported Sharpe 1.78 and roughly 49bp first-hour average stock return are not net ES ticks; a source cost model and chronological trading holdout were not recovered. **Who pays:** announcement-uncertainty compensation, not identified forced flow. **Build/code:** a minute-calendar strategy is mechanically possible after timing is resolved, but profitability is unproved. Do not replace SEP events with every press conference after the 2019 regime change. This is a narrower subgroup of an already-mined FOMC family. Full manuscript retrieval failed; only indexed primary excerpts were available. Reading note, not a new admitted strategy.

## Why these do not become two- or three-paper bundles

- **NG attention + API/EIA forecasting:** there is a plausible division of labor, calendar attention versus surprise estimation. But the API/EIA source (SSRN 3747330) is already indexed, required forecast vintages are missing, and the rare Friday drift has no net executable OOS result. Calling this a bundle would conceal two unresolved gates. No combined return claim or test is proposed.
- **FOMC relief rally + Treasury drift:** both are exposed to the same announcement, but one addresses uncertainty resolution and the other signed policy information over a much longer horizon. Their signs, clocks and holdings are not interchangeable. There is no demonstrated joint rule or incremental contribution to ONB, Twin, EOS or Bridge.
- **Cross-sectional ML + reversal/exit literature:** an exit controller cannot create the missing universe, forced payer, realizable VWAP or net-tick evidence. Restricting the model to the owned markets is a new adaptation, not the paper's OOS result.

There is **no credible new paper-based modification to ONB, Twin, EOS, Monday Bridge or CL fade from this pass**. This is a screening conclusion, not evidence that every possible adaptation would lose.
