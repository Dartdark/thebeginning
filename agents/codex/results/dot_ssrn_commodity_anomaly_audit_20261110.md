# dot: commodity anomaly and low-risk evidence audit
Author: dot. 2026-11-10. Research only; no tests, acquisitions or strategy promotion.

## Anomalies in Commodity Futures Markets, SSRN3567629
[Accepted2021 primary manuscript](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100920/1/SSRN-id3567629.pdf), methods/results/appendix inspected. Twenty-one anomalies;26-name overall universe, varying histories. Monthly terciles require six names; hedge is half(high−low). JUMP beta uses trailing12-month daily regression; low-minus-high gross annual4.63%, four of seven tests survive10% FDR. VoV uses252-day variability/mean of91-day implied volatility; low-minus-high gross2.72%, three tests survive; carry/momentum controls weaken its incremental evidence. Uniform6.6bp monthly cost robustness is asserted, net tables only on request.

**Crucial sample limit:** overall1959–2015 is not a verified JUMP/VoV trading sample. Effective factor-specific dates and monthly membership counts are undisclosed. Subsample blanks do not recover them. No modern holdout established.

### Admission judgment
JUMP sensitivity is a different research hypothesis from curve/COT/skew sorts, but the factor's source bridge is unresolved. [Cremers–Halling–Weinbaum2013 primary preprint](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/57253/1/CHW_20131001.pdf) builds a positive-gamma, vega-neutral ATM-straddle calendar spread using CME S&P futures options1988–2011. The commodity study lists OptionMetrics S&P index options, without documenting a factor file, reconstruction or extension. Those histories must not be equated.

[Official OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/united-states/) lists historical coverage from1996 and daily corrections to previous prices/calculations. Coverage is not proof of original receipt. No free public author factor history verified. Do not substitute VIX/VVIX.

Long-low/short-high JUMP beta reverses jump-protection exposure, not crash insurance. Uniform costs and10% FDR do not establish native lots, independent-search control, margin survival or new holdout. Required: licensed factor construction, initial receipts/revisions, per-month eligibility, identified native contracts, one formation/cash ledger and daily marked risk. Remains unnumbered source/data reserve.

## Idiosyncratic volatility: omitted factors matter
[1971917 January2014 manuscript](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6418/1/JFM_SSRN_13Jan2014.pdf) combines momentum, roll yield and inverse residual-volatility ranks over1985–2011. Quintile monthly portfolios, multiple windows; average Sharpe .69, mean7.39% gross versus6.82% at6.6bp costs. It already warns residual volatility can contain systematic risk.

[2120587 May2016 follow-on](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/15720/8/FernandezPerez_Fuertes_Miffre_IRFA2016%20.pdf), a separate study rather than a renamed revision, finds average alpha3.87% under conventional benchmarks but .86%, insignificant, under commodity benchmarks. Residual construction and performance evaluation both depend on the benchmark.

Assessment: a triple-screen portfolio is not proof of three independent economic premiums. Existing momentum/carry/skew families remain the relevant comparators.

## Commodity betting-against-beta is not established in its own original table
[Frazzini–Pedersen full primary paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Betting%20Against%20Beta%20-%20Frazzini%20and%20Pedersen.pdf), Table8: commodity monthly excess .18%, t .72; alpha .21%, t .83; annual Sharpe .11, volatility19.67%; average$1.48 long/$0.71 short. Significant pooled-futures findings cannot be assigned to this insignificant commodity row. [AQR original data description](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Betting-Against-Beta-Original-Paper-Data) lists24 commodity futures through March2012.

Assessment: leveraged low-beta is not a conservative account rule or a loss cap. Funding vulnerability and integer exposures matter. This paper does not justify adding a commodity BAB strategy to the user's book.
