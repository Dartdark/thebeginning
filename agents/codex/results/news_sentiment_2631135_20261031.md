## Rule (as described; not independently backtested)

Source: Qiang Song, Anqi Liu, Steve Y. Yang, Anil Deane and Kaushik Datta,
*An Extreme Firm-Specific News Sentiment Asymmetry Based Trading Strategy*, SSRN2631135, July2015 upload.
[Archived PDF](../inputs/news_sentiment_2631135_20261031/paper.pdf). Visible title controls over stale PDF metadata.

**1. What is it about?** Use firm-specific Thomson Reuters News Analytics probabilities/relevance to select US
stocks after unusually positive news. Weekly article sentiment is `mean(relevance*(p_positive-p_negative))`;
neutral contributes zero. Shock is current sentiment minus prior-window mean divided by prior-window SD.
Trend sums successive weekly sentiment changes. Lookbacks are optimized by GICS sector using next-week return
rank correlation on2003–06. Trading is reviewed every4weeks, with weekly checks of a10%loss threshold.
Source market/news history spans2003–14; the stated backtest is2007–14 and the selected universe596stocks.

**2. What does it find?** Author reports positive-news longs outperform S&P500 and negative-news shorts have
negative Sharpe. Trend is stronger than shock, but has a large drawdown. Results below are source claims with
unspecified cost treatment, not independently reproduced net returns. Annualized mean is not necessarily CAGR.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Annualized mean return |16.90%|12.24%|6.30%|
| Annualized volatility |26.50%|25.22%|21.18%|
| Sharpe |0.64|0.49|0.30|

**3. Why does it make money, if it does?** Proposed gradual incorporation of positive firm information and
sentiment persistence. The paper does not identify a forced trader or causal flow. Ordinary equity drift, sector
exposure, momentum, liquidity and attention are alternative explanations needing controls. Win frequency alone
does not establish that profits are broadly distributed or survive selection and costs.

**4. Can we build on it?** A conditional personal-stock research lead, not a new NQ/ES signal. Original TRNA
article/entity records and historical equity universe are material missing inputs. The author has an actual
backtest, unlike a conceptual overview, but selection concerns and roughly50%DD make this low priority.
No claim of current portfolio diversification or profitability is earned. Multiday equity holdings do not fit
50K/150K evaluation or funded plans as written.

**5. Can a strategy code be made?** **Yes, conditionally, as a Python stock-selection research engine.** With
dated TRNA probabilities, relevance, RIC/entity mappings, receipt/model vintages and adjusted price/cash ledgers,
the weekly aggregation, past-only scores and training-local sector fitting can be implemented. Need to freeze
week cutoff/timezone, first later executable fill, missing-news/zero-SD policy, sector history, weighting/cash,
chosen percentile, removal/re-entry rule and weekly loss-review execution. MultiCharts price-only code cannot
create these news features. A new NLP model/headline source would be a separately registered adaptation.
No strategy implementation or profitability claim is produced merely because the arithmetic is programmable.

## Results and validation audit

- **Test-period selection:** source explicitly sweeps top1–20%and bottom1–20%on the stated2007–14backtest to
  find the best threshold. Training-period score distributions fix percentile values, but do not make choosing
  the winning percentile using later performance out of sample. Independent later validation is missing.
- **Universe clock:** top1000average-volume names, then average news-frequency filtering, yields596names.
  The period used for these screens is not made point-in-time safe; full-sample selection would introduce bias.
  Delisted securities, historical sectors and corporate actions need explicit treatment.
- **Risk/stop:** a weekly loss check is not an intraday10%stop or a loss cap. Source DD49.09%/56.37%confirms
  substantial cumulative risk. Exact stock-versus-portfolio loss/rebalance interpretation remains unspecified.
- **Feature arithmetic:** relevance enters the per-article mean, not a division by total relevance. The trend's
  sum of successive changes telescopes into an endpoint difference; it does not itself require a consistently
  rising news path. Two deterministic fixtures verify these mathematical points, not predictive power.
- **Inference:** the paper supplies no multiple-search adjustment, net-cost ledger or current-era validation.
  The volatility-regime split is a descriptive historical classification, not a registered tradable filter.
  Comparing a selected stock basket with S&P500 alone does not isolate news alpha.

Our2018–22/2023+return metrics: **not computed**. No raw corpus was replaced with OHLC, earnings dates or
present-day sentiment. No strategy signal/candidate return/account simulation ran.

## What could make this wrong

Source period differs from our modern book; selected cutoffs, uncertain universe clock, unreported costs and
unclear position lifecycle limit reproduction. Historical analytics backfill can differ from what was available
at the time. Weekly aggregation must wait for all included news to become available, then use a later fill.
News clustering and earnings gaps can dominate results; a positive classifier label does not certify a safe entry.
