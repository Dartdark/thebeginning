# Equity Research as Marketing Tool: DON'T TEST as a futures strategy
Summary: Strauss and Zhu study broker research and business generation, not investment returns.
The June 2004 draft uses 1997–2002 data from America, Europe and Japan.
Research reputation and quantity correlate with broker business; no ES/NQ directional edge is demonstrated.

## What it studies

The outcome is business won by sell-side firms: equity trading volume/market share and IPO/M&A business.
Perceived research quality is proxied by institutional-investor research rankings. Quantity includes analyst
headcount, company coverage and report production. These are firm-level measures, not trade-level entry signals.
Section 4 describes Reuters Institutional Investor surveys, SDC/Autex trading and deal data, and Nelson's
research staffing/coverage data. Current access, licensing and equivalent modern datasets were not checked.

## Source findings and limits

| Finding reported in the paper | What we can infer |
|---|---|
| Better perceived research quality is associated with more equity trading business across three regions. | Research can help attract brokerage customers; rankings are not a direct measure of investment alpha. |
| Research quantity is associated with both trading and investment-banking business. | Coverage and staffing can serve business-development purposes. This does not identify predictive reports. |
| Changes in perceived quality relate to trading business; changes in business also relate to staffing/research quantity. | The relationships are observational. Lead/lag evidence does not eliminate omitted reputation, client-network, scale or service-quality effects. |
| The conclusion differentiates quantity from quality: quantity responds to trading-business changes but does little to drive next year's trading business. | Do not simplify the abstract into a claim that publishing more reports reliably causes future revenue growth. |

References: abstract; introduction pp.3–7; data pp.17–18; results sections 5.1–5.2; conclusion pp.26–27.
The paper discusses research's limited informational value using earlier literature; its broker-business regressions
are not a direct test showing every analyst recommendation lacks value. Nor does the study establish that
all research is intentionally misleading. The paper's stronger causal language exceeds what this review treats as established.

## Fit to our strategy process

There is no scheduled mandatory flow, signed order imbalance, fixed entry/exit clock, executable risk rule,
trading return, after-cost result or funded-account comparison. A broker gaining routing share can handle both
buys and sells; gross volume or commission revenue is not net index buying. Stock-level coverage also need not
move the aggregate index. Inferring bullishness from more reports would invent a new hypothesis absent from this evidence.

The historical bundled-research/commission setting cannot be assumed unchanged today. Electronic execution,
commission practices and research-payment regulation have evolved; contemporary replication is needed before
transferring the business-model estimates. No modern regulatory or empirical update was performed here.

Practical use: when reviewing a bank report, identify its intended customer and commercial incentives, distinguish
source facts from trade recommendations, and require reproducible rules, timestamped data and net outcomes.
Keep weighting verifiable evidence over the author's employer or reputation. These reinforce existing controls;
they do not require adopting a new trading filter or avoiding all sell-side research.

## Decision

**DON'T TEST as a strategy.** No evidence sheet for a new trading candidate or strategy preregistration is warranted
because no candidate with an executable return mechanism is identified. No promising-strategy queue entry:
missing signals/data alone do not constitute a documented merit. TIER_LIST and the current TODO queue were checked
at the pinned commit; no matching SSRN/title/author candidate was identified.

Trading metrics, power, occupancy/diversification and portfolio contribution are **not established**, not zero.
No backtests, data purchases, MultiCharts code, account changes or live/VPS actions were performed.
