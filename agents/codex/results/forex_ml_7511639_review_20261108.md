# Forex ML versus econometrics: DON'T TEST — unmatched targets and insufficient evidence

Summary: Reported 78–85% accuracy concerns trading-volume classes, not directional trades.
RF, GBM and ARIMA are assigned different tasks; headline superiority is not a controlled same-target comparison.
No executable return, cost ledger or complete risk-label definition is supplied; numeric reconciliation also raises a question.
Do not build an FX/NQ strategy or spend a return trial from this source. Retain the target-comparison caution only.

## Five questions

1. **What is it about?** Comparing econometric and ensemble-learning models on daily EUR/USD, GBP/USD and
   USD/CAD data, January 1, 2010 through July 12, 2021, using price-derived moving averages, RSI, MACD,
   lags and rolling features. It also discusses AI governance and regime dependence.
2. **What does it find?** Author-reported RF volume-class accuracy is 85%, 82%, 78%; GBM risk reduction is
   20%, 18%, 15%, respectively. The paper claims error reduction up to 30%, significant advantages over ARIMA,
   and weakening advantages around shocks. These claims are not independently reproduced and lack enough
   common-target results and definitions to support the headline comparison. No strategy performance is reported.
3. **Why does it make money, if it does?** It does not demonstrate money made. A possible forecasting mechanism
   is nonlinear relationships in lagged indicators, but no identified forced flow, signed future-return result or
   net payoff connects volume/risk classifications to an investable position. Volume accuracy is not win rate;
   a risk-error reduction is not a reduction in account drawdown or cash losses.
4. **Can we build on it?** Retain the need for identical targets and causal evaluation, already required by our
   controls. There is no sufficiently specified economic edge to advance. A future reopening would first require
   released source code, exact risk labels and volume provenance, target horizons, fold dates and paired predictions
   on the same task. Then compare a causal persistence/random-walk baseline on identical observations before
   a trading definition or account test. No new raw-data acquisition is justified by this failed source gate.
5. **Can a strategy code be made?** Generic RF/GBM research code is feasible; an exact source reproduction or
   complete source-defined strategy is not. Missing items include the forecast timestamp/horizon, risk target,
   true volume definition, exact indicators/lags, tuning ranges and selected settings, fold/refit schedule,
   classification-to-position rule, exit, executable price and costs. A spot-FX implementation and an intraday
   currency-futures/NQ adaptation would be separate hypotheses. No PowerLanguage or trading code is warranted.

## What the reported numbers actually measure

| Source claim | Reported result | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| §3.2, §4.2/Table 2: RF | EUR/USD 85%, GBP/USD 82%, USD/CAD 78% | Low/Medium/High trading-volume classification; not up/down accuracy or profitable-trade frequency. |
| §3.2, §4.2/Table 2: GBM | Risk reduction 20%, 18%, 15% | Described as MSE-based in §4.1; risk-label construction, denominator and realizable portfolio are unspecified. |
| §4.1/Table 1: ARIMA | Accuracy range 48–65% | Continuous forecast model without a reconciled categorical target/threshold; cannot directly benchmark RF volume or GBM risk. |
| Abstract/highlights | Forecast error reduction up to 30% | No pair/model RMSE/MAE/MSE table or common-target prediction ledger substantiates this. |
| §4.3 | Paired tests p<0.01; folds differ ≤3 percentage points, train/test within roughly 4 points | No t-statistic, paired error sequence, test sample count or fold table enables an audit. These claims do not resolve target comparability or temporal leakage. |

§3.2 explicitly acknowledges that RF and GBM are assigned different tasks and do not constitute a controlled
method comparison. §§4.1 and 4.4 nevertheless compare their headline ranges with ARIMA and infer broad forecasting
superiority. That inference is unsupported by the task-matched evidence supplied. The paper does not report Sharpe,
Sortino, profit factor, Calmar, Ulcer, returns, turnover or trading costs; none can be inferred from these tables.

## Source and validation problems

- **Label and information clocks:** No precise future target horizon or feature cutoff establishes whether
  same-day OHLC/indicators precede the predicted volume/risk outcome. GBM risk classes and the risk–reward variable
  later used in diagnostics are not adequately defined. Cannot substitute our own definitions and call it reproduction.
- **Volume provenance:** Sources are named as Yahoo Finance and Investing.com, but exact symbols, downloads,
  volume fields and whether volume is venue-specific or a proxy are absent. Spot FX has no consolidated exchange
  volume. Availability of spot OHLC does not supply this missing target, nor would 6E futures volume reproduce it.
- **Preprocessing:** Training-fold-only z-scoring is stated, which is appropriate. However volume tertiles are
  defined over the sample period, posing potential future-dependent label boundaries; no training-only label
  treatment is stated. Linear interpolation across closures can use a later observation if used two-sidedly;
  the paper does not establish a causal alternative. These are unresolved risks, not proof of what unseen code did.
- **Splits and selection:** A 70/30 rolling-window design and five-fold CV are stated, but actual fold dates,
  chronological inner tuning, label maturity, purging, hyperparameter results and random seeds are absent.
  Similar train/test accuracy alone does not rule out shared leakage or non-independent folds.
- **Price-level fit is not future-return evidence:** R²=0.9992 for Open regressed on price-derived indicators is
  a level relationship, without an established future forecast target or executable signal. Comparing three
  pairs' aggregate accuracies also does not isolate Brexit/oil-shock causal effects; dated regime-level tables
  and matched baselines are needed.
- **Internal numeric question (§4.3):** Reported Pearson correlation Open/26-day average is 0.9987, whose
  square is 0.99740169; reported univariate R² for that average is 0.9873. In a conventional same-sample simple
  regression with intercept these should agree, a gap of 0.01010169 that rounding cannot explain. Different
  samples/estimators could explain it but are not documented. This check does not re-estimate the regression
  or imply misconduct.
- **Balanced/uneven classes:** §3.2 calls volume tertiles balanced; §4.1 invokes uneven volume classes and an
  undefined “F1-adjusted” accuracy. Test subsets could be uneven, but class counts and actual F1/accuracy definitions
  are not supplied to resolve the distinction.
